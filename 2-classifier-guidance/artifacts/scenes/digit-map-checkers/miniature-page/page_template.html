what is not drawn
  • The frozen U-Net's inside. It has skip connections; here it is one grey box, and the miniature stands in a formula for it.
  • Skip connections in the checker: PixelHead has none, so none are drawn.
  • In the miniature, the three stem convolutions and c1 become one convolution with one window per half; c2 is left out; the MLP's hidden layer of 256 and its SiLU are left out; 16 time features become 2.
  • Adam at 0.001 on 256 canvases for 4,000 updates becomes one plain step of 0.05 on two canvases.
  • Count's other three kinds of canvas (overlaid, same half, single).
  • The identity checker's real maps, logits and gradients: it was never saved, so every identity number on this page is the miniature's and stays illustrative.
  • On the logit stage, all but 12 of the 256 hidden units. The 12 drawn have the largest |w2,j hj| on that canvas at that timestep; the other 244 are one dashed line with their summed contribution. The first layer has 69,632 weights; the faint lines run from each input strip to each drawn unit as a summary, and no single weight is drawn.
  • In the step-by-step frame, the noisy canvas at each step (the guess of the clean canvas stands for it), and the pictures of all but six of the 320 seeds.
  • In the opening frame, all but 8 channels of each layer. The 8 shown are the ones with the largest mean value on that canvas at that timestep; a different canvas or timestep can pick different ones.
  • The weights on the miniature's lines: they show on hover or tap, and in the panel's tables, so the figure keeps room for the values.
  • The push in the miniature uses its starting weights, not trained ones.
how to read the page
  • Blue: pixels and time. Green: trained units. Red: the logit. Orange: anything flowing backward. Grey: frozen. Purple: count. Teal: identity.
  • In the step-by-step frame, map a is purple and map b green, and the picture goes dark where both are high; the triangles under the maps mark each map's centre across the canvas, and the number beside them is how far apart the centres sit. In g, red pixels raise the logit when brightened and blue ones lower it, and an orange border means the push is acting at that step. Each map and g picture saturates at its own 99th percentile at that step. On the right, grey is the plain product and orange the pushed one, the dashed line marks this step, and the two numbers above each plot are plain then pushed at this step.
  • A circle holds the value at that node. Hover or tap a line to read its weight; the panel lists them all.
  • In the opening frame's real pictures, blue is below 0, white is 0 and red is above, and each picture saturates at the 99th percentile of its absolute values, so one hot pixel cannot wash out the rest. Canvases are grey from −1 (black) to +1 (white).
  • The right arrow key or next moves one idea on. In the opening frame, step or a click on a stage moves the red box. Play replays the current pass, step moves it one layer, and sweep noise runs the 16 timesteps.
