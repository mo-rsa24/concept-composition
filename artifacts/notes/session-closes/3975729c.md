## What it set out to do

This session ran on 2026-11-18 from 04:10 to 04:18 UTC, inside `4-inclusive-captions/`. The user asked three things in turn:

1. Which sessions and experiments already existed on inclusive captions.
2. Where their results were, so they could be filed under `artifacts/results`.
3. Whether the MNIST experiment planned for classifier guidance should become an inclusive-captions experiment instead: make a canvas holding a 7 and an 8, train it under "7", under "8" and under "7 and 8", then draw from the product of "7" and "8".

**Words this uses**

A product of experts (PoE) adds the noise predictions of two prompts, so one image is pushed to satisfy both. Inclusive captions means a picture holding both concepts is also trained under each single name, so "a cat" sometimes sees a dog. In the SDXL pilot, run A captions the both-animal photos "a cat and a dog", and run B also captions them "cat" and "dog". The dial is the share of two-concept pictures also trained under each single label. A corrector is a set of extra Langevin steps at each noise level of the sampler.

## What it found

**The one local SDXL result is a six-seed sheet.** By eye, 0 of 6 seeds show two animals in any of its three rows (plain SDXL, run A, run B). **Training seeds move the toy's counts a lot.** With an exact number of relabelled pictures and three trainings each, separate pairs of 300 were 1, 153, 0 at 200 pictures; 157, 17, 134 at 600; 183, 29, 57 at 1,200; and 252, 74, 238 at 2,000. The session therefore asked for three training seeds per MNIST setting.

**The risk it named is leakage.** If "7" trains on canvases that also hold an 8, then "7" can learn to draw 8s, and a two-digit product would come from the data. On the exact toy, the clean-pair share of the product falls to half when each expert puts a weight of 0.00567 on each of the other's three shapes, with the pair picture's weight held at 0.1. So the session said an MNIST figure must show the product's two-digit rate beside how often "7" alone draws one 7.

**Its recommendation was to run the MNIST version.** MNIST trained from scratch sees only the canvases the project builds, so the caption rule controls what each label learns. The SDXL LoRA could not do this because SDXL had already learnt "a cat" from its own data. The session wrote a `/drip-skill-route` prompt for this and ended there. That prompt exists only at line 267 of the transcript.
