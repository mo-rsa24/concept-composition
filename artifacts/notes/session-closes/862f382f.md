**Why the paper was read**

When Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) combines two prompts as a product of experts (PoE), a cat and a dog tend to fuse into one hybrid body. Rung 2 of the route needs a small network, called a discriminator or checker, that tells two separate things from a fused body on pictures it never trained on. Every checker tried by then had failed its held-out check, scoring 0.53 to 0.71 balanced accuracy (the hit rate on each class, averaged, on a 0-to-1 scale) against a required 0.75. A new candidate in this paper's style had just been written: [a count head](../../../2-classifier-guidance/scripts/rung2/train_count_head.py), which reads SDXL's internal features and predicts a map with one blob per body.

**What the session was asked for**

## What it found

The session read the whole paper and its released code (commit `a8a699c`) ahead of the reader and delivered all five answers. Page numbers below are the arXiv PDF's. The ten caveats are inferences from the named passages, and none has been tested.

**Layers and timesteps**

The heads read all 9 resnet layers of SDXL's decoder (12 on Stable Diffusion 1.5). Each layer is resized to the 128 by 128 latent grid, brought to 384 channels and mixed by learnt weights (p4, p14). SDXL's mid block is left out because it "did not improve the quality" of spatial readouts (p16). Each head also takes the timestep as an input, which the paper calls "very important": without it, outputs "quickly fall off the manifold of natural imagery" (p13).

**Examples and labels**

A pose head works from as few as 100 pictures, with labels made by off-the-shelf models such as OpenPose, MiDaS and HED (p2, p4). The measure below is PCK at α = 0.05, the percentage of pose keypoints landing within 5% of the pose's box size (Table 2, p15).

| Method on SDXL | Training pictures | Added weights | PCK (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| No control | none | none | 1.19 |
| Readout head | 100 | 5.9 million | 15.24 |
| Readout head | 1,000 | 5.9 million | 19.65 |
| Readout head | 8,500 | 5.9 million | 24.29 |
| T2I-Adapter | 3 million | 79 million | 24.06 |

Training the SDXL pose head used batches of 8 for at most 5,000 steps and took 2 h 25 min on one A100 GPU (p13).

**The guidance rule**

The head's reading is compared with a target, and the gradient of that distance nudges the noisy picture (section 3.2, p3). Guidance runs over the first half of 100 sampling steps with a step size of 0.02 per latent value, and stops before the end to protect photorealism (Table 1, p12). The paper and code differ in two places. The paper prints the push as an edit to the predicted noise, while the code takes one fresh AdamW step on the latent itself. The paper describes L2-norm scaling, while the AdamW step scales each latent value on its own (`rg_pipeline.py:154-188`).

**Where it fails**

Guidance costs more: SDXL pose control took 44.0 s and 27.7 GB of GPU memory per sampling run, against 14.2 s and 15.6 GB without guidance (p13). It "can sometimes produce improbable images, in the form of cartoonish or unrealistic imagery satisfying the readout constraints" (p8). The pose readout "can sometimes hallucinate extra detail" (p16).

**Early steps**

The paper reports a central figure in the readout "within the first step of sampling and a coarse silhouette within the first 10%" of steps (p15). This is the window where PoE's fused body forms.

**The count head against the ten caveats**

The read map labels the caveats R1 to R10 and judges the script as of commit `c5e61cec`. The session named R1 and R2 as the two to fix before the count head's result means anything.

| Caveat | What it asks | Does the count head meet it |
|---|---|---|
| R1, labels | Labels from a tool that does its job well | No. Its labels come from the GroundingDINO detector, which calls 80 of 105 hand-called hybrids two bodies (balanced accuracy 0.632) |
| R2, timestep input | The head receives the timestep | No. `CountHead` takes features only |
| R3, layers | The paper's 9 decoder layers with learnt mixing | Partly. It reads the mid block and up blocks 0 and 1, skips up block 2, and has no mixing weights |
| R4, noisy states | Train and check on the same kind of state | Partly. It trains on the product's own half-drawn states, and its check adds noise to finished pictures |
| R5, early steps | Accuracy reported step by step at the start | Meets as a check (0.75 on at least 10 of the first 20 of 50 steps). Step-0 states carry finished-picture labels, so the head may learn the average outcome |
| R6, sparse target | Reweight empty pixels against filled ones | Partly. No reweighting |
| R7, fooling the head | Judge a push independently of the labeller | Does not apply yet, since the script does not push. Its check uses hand calls |
| R8, push size | Relate the push to the size of the missing correction | Does not apply yet, since the script does not push |
| R9, which animals | A count treats two dogs as two | Meets as reported. It prints accuracy against every wrong case |
| R10, held-out pictures | The check uses pictures never trained on | Partly. The 7 held-out pairs are clean. Up to 4 of the 20 cat × dog check pictures may match training drawings (seeds 7 and 8), inferred without comparing sampler settings |
