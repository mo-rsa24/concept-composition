## What it set out to do

The project asks whether a small classifier's gradient, added to a diffusion sampler at each step, can make a product of experts (PoE) draw two separate things where it normally draws one fused thing. That added gradient is called **the push**, and the classifier that supplies it is called **the checker**.

On SDXL cat × dog (rung 2 of the route, step 10) the work had stalled on the checker. Its best held-out balanced accuracy (the mean of the hit rates on each class, 0.5 is chance) was 0.655, and CLIP on finished pictures reached 0.771. Every SDXL training picture cost a render and a label call.

This session used `/drip-skill-route --from` to add a stage on MNIST digits, where labels are exact and free, to [the classifier-guidance route](../../../2-classifier-guidance/plans/route-classifier-guided-poe-from-toy-to-animals.md). The stage became **step 7b** of the route and **row A2b** of [the outcome board](../../../2-classifier-guidance/report/classifier-guided-poe/board.md). Its question: how good must a checker be before its push turns a fused digit into two digits?

The session designed the stage over 11 rounds with the user. It trained no diffusion model and no checker. The only network it trained was a small prototype digit classifier.

## What it found

**The design it settled, round by round**

| Round | Settled |
|---|---|
| 1. Placement | Step 7b runs beside rung 2 and blocks nothing |
| 2. Data | 32 × 64 pixel canvases of real MNIST digits. The **two-digit share** (fraction of training canvases holding two digits) is 0, 0.01 or 0.1. Five of the 45 digit pairs never appear together in training: 0 & 3, 1 & 9, 3 & 4, 3 & 6, 4 & 7, drawn by `random.Random(0)`. The pair 7 & 8 is seen |
| 3. Model | One class-conditional pixel U-Net per share, label dropped on 10% of canvases, DDIM with 50 steps. A two-digit canvas trains once per digit it holds |
| 4. First measurement | Plain product on 7 & 8 and the five held-out pairs, 64 seeds, all three models, read before any checker exists |
| 5. Checker | A small head on the frozen U-Net's decoder features, with the asked pair as an input, recorded as a 14-row table in section 7b |
| 6. Comparisons | Five versions on seeds 0 to 63: plain product, pair-aware push, separate-label push ("is there a 7?" plus "is there an 8?"), size-matched random push, best-of-N |
| 7. Sweeps | Weaker checkers made with less data (1%, 5%, 25%, 100%) or flipped labels (10%, 20%), and the push cap at 0.1, 0.3, 1.0 |
| 8. Bar | Counts of canvases with two clear, right digits: plain product at most 8 of 64, pair-aware push at least 32 of 64, random and separate-label within 6 of plain, best-of-N at least 8 below the push, held-out pairs at least 160 of 320 |
| 9. Planning | No plan tree, and the bar is copied to a review file before any model trains |
| 10. Time | Three two-hour sittings, about 6 hours of the user's time and an estimated 3 to 4 hours of computing |
| 11. Prompt | A pasteable `/drip-execute-plan --request` prompt, stored in section 7b |

**The 0.771 rule.** Round 7 wrote a decision rule for step 10. If the compose count crosses the bar at a checker accuracy below 0.771, keep fetching cat × dog data. If it crosses only above 0.771, switch to a checker that judges each animal separately.

**What the maths allows.** The session read Liu et al. 2022 and Bradley et al. 2025 from the PDFs. The guided sampler draws in proportion to `p_PoE(x) · c(x)^s`, where `c` is the checker and `s` the push strength, so a picture the product gives zero weight stays at zero. Bradley's section 3.2 says the product "cannot produce truly out-of-distribution samples". Liu et al. still drew more objects than any training image held, on CLEVR (30,000 images of 1 to 5 objects, labelled by one object's position). The session concluded that unseen pairs are possible only if each expert saw its digit beside some other digit, which is why the share exists.

**Measured in this session**

- The prototype judge (a small CNN trained on single MNIST digits, 421,642 weights) reads 98.2% of the 10,000 MNIST test digits correctly.
- On 10 pasted canvases, that judge matched how each canvas was built on 7 of 10.
- A fused 7 and 8 read as a confident 8 (probability 1.00), so confidence alone cannot detect fusing.
- A correct 8 read at probability 0.78 was called the wrong identity, because the rule needs 0.9.
- Sample draws of 30 canvases held 0, 1 and 3 two-digit canvases at shares 0, 0.01 and 0.1.
