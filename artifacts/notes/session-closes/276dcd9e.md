## What it set out to do

This session ran from 2026-11-17 07:18 UTC to 2026-11-18 04:03 UTC, inside `2-classifier-guidance/` on branch `exec/classifier-guidance-ladder`. It walked [plan 20](../../../2-classifier-guidance/plans/20-our-photos-both-ways.md), step 8 of [the classifier-guidance route](../../../2-classifier-guidance/plans/route-classifier-guided-poe-from-toy-to-animals.md), through a self-paced loop.

The question was whether a classifier that knows what a separate cat and dog look like can stop a product of a cat expert and a dog expert from fusing them. The session tested this in two experiments on our own images:

1. **Released-model experiment.** The authors' 256 px unconditional guided-diffusion model stays frozen, and only classifiers are trained.
2. **Trained-model experiment.** We train their 64 px model on our images twice, once without the both-animal images and once with all images. A null label replaces the real one 10% of the time, so one network supplies a cat, a dog and a null expert.

**The pass mark, written before any run.** In at least one experiment, on seeds 1 to 16, the pair-aware push gives two separate animals on at least 4 more of the 16 seeds than the separate-label push, with pictures no more damaged than the plain product's. The reader judges this from full-resolution tiles. **No sheet was ever seen, so this has no verdict.**

**Words this uses**

The *product* is the sampler that combines the cat and dog experts, and it tends to draw one fused animal. A *push* is the gradient of a classifier added to each sampling step. A *separate-label classifier* answers "is a cat present?" and "is a dog present?". A *pair-aware classifier* is trained to tell real both-animal images from the product's own finished draws. Those draws are its *fake pile*. A *sheet* is a grid with one row per seed and one column per sampling method. `$D` means `/datasets/mmolefe/poe_repair_min/outputs/classifier_guidance_photos`.

## What it found

**The image set is 550 images, and 312 of them are renders.** It holds 200 cat, 200 dog and 150 both-animal images at 256 and 64 px. COCO photos supply 100, 100 and 38 of those, and SD3.5 renders supply 100, 100 and 112. Each 256 px image shrunk to 64 px differs from its stored 64 px file by a median of 0.13 and at most 0.20 grey levels out of 255. The same image shifted 8 px differs by 53.96, so both sizes hold the same crop.

**Four images are candidates to strike, and the reader has not ruled on them.** By eye at sheet scale, `both_015` and `both_027` show only the dog, and `both_000` and `both_017` are collages.

**Our separate-label classifiers score well on held-out images.** Accuracy is the share of held-out images labelled correctly. Step 0 is a clean image, and step 900 is close to pure noise.

| Classifier | Step 0 (cat, dog) | Step 250 | Step 500 | Step 900 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 256 px | 0.93, 0.95 | 0.89, 0.93 | 0.85, 0.78 | 0.60, 0.51 |
| 64 px | 0.73, 0.84 | 0.75, 0.80 | 0.71, 0.76 | 0.47, 0.58 |

**Our 256 px classifier still could not steer the released model.** At push scale 10, seeds 1 to 4 drew fruit, a dog, a man and a bird. At scales 30 and 100 the pictures broke into texture. On 184 of the old product draws, mostly food and objects by eye, our classifier said cat on 184 and dog on 182, with mean probabilities of 0.97 and 0.96. The released ImageNet classifier said cat on 3 and dog on 24, with mean probabilities of 0.02 and 0.13.

**The likely cause is a missing pile of pictures with neither animal.** Every image this classifier trained on held a cat, a dog or both. The session's explanation is that a classifier already 97% sure has a near-zero gradient, so its push steers nothing. That gradient was reasoned about and never measured. The lesson is filed in [the project's list of mistakes](../../../context/research-guidelines.md#the-mistakes-this-project-has-already-made).

**The released classifier's own classes do steer it.** The reader chose this fix. It uses 5 cat classes (281 to 285) and 118 dog classes (151 to 268) at scale 10. All 4 check draws were animals: 2 cat-dog hybrids and 2 single dog-like animals. In the first 16 of the new product draws, all 16 are animals, about 6 read as blends and one may hold two animals. These are calls by eye at grid scale. The earlier product gave about 5 animal-like shapes in 16.
