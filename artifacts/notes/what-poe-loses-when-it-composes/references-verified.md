## Corrections

**Item 6, title.** The title is *Catastrophic Compositional Generation: Why Vanilla Diffusion Models Fail to Extrapolate*, with "Generation" where the draft said "generalization". The authors are Duncan Soiffer, Chandler Squires, Yuan Guan, Jason Hartford, Pradeep Ravikumar, and it was submitted 22 June 2026 with no venue stated.

**Item 5, authors.** The guessed author list is right: Sachit Gaudi, Gautam Sreekumar, Vishnu Boddeti. The arXiv page states no venue, and ICLR 2025 comes from the ICLR proceedings page.

**Item 3, venue.** The arXiv page states no venue. ICML 2025 is confirmed by PMLR volume 267, pages 5319–5354.

**Item 7, venue.** The arXiv page says the paper was accepted as a spotlight at ICLR 2025. The draft gave no venue.

**Item 19, capitalisation.** The title is written *Diffusion Models already have a Semantic Latent Space*, with "already" and "have" lower case.

**Item 20, which Hinton paper.** Hinton 1999 and Hinton 2002 are two separate papers. The 2002 journal paper is the one listed. The 1999 paper is *Products of experts*, in the 9th International Conference on Artificial Neural Networks (ICANN '99), [doi:10.1049/cp:19991075](https://doi.org/10.1049/cp:19991075). Both DOIs were confirmed through Crossref.

**Item 21, date and author.** The post is by LAION e.V. and dated 30 Aug 2024, and its own suggested citation gives the year as 2024.

**Item 22, access.** The URL resolves with HTTP 200. The Hugging Face API reports the dataset as gated (automatic approval) and last modified 2024-07-02.

**Full titles that the draft shortened.** These are items 10 (Attend-and-Excite), 12 (LLM-grounded Diffusion), 13 (SDXL), 15 (LAION-5B), 17 (CLIP) and 18 (Grounding DINO). The full titles are in the list above.

**Venues not checked.** Items 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 17 and 18 have no venue on their arXiv pages, so they are listed as arXiv preprints. Their published venues were not looked up.

## Figures fetched

Both images came from the arXiv HTML rendering and were opened and inspected before saving.

**[Bradley et al. Figure 5](figures/bradley-2025-projective-composition.png)**

The paper is Bradley et al. (2025), *Mechanisms of Projective Composition of Diffusion Models*, arXiv:2502.04549 (HTML version v3). The image URL is https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04549v3/figures/clevr-color-comp.png, and the image is 1172 × 862 px.

Caption, quoted: "Figure 5: Composing yellow objects with objects of other colors. Yellow objects successfully compose with blue, cyan and magenta objects but not with brown, gray, green, or red objects. Per the histograms (left), in RGB-colorspace yellow has R, G distributed like the background (gray) while B has a distinct distribution peaked closer to zero. Taking $M_{\text{yellow}}\approx\{B\}$, Theorem 5.3 predicts that standard diffusion can sample from compositions of yellow with any color where the B channel is distributed like the background: namely, blue, cyan, magenta per the histograms. (Other colors may theoretically compose per Theorem 6.1, but be difficult to sample.) (Additional samples in Figure 11.)"

The figure composes a yellow-object CLEVR model with seven other colour models. The 3 colours whose blue channel looks like the background (blue, cyan, magenta) give a scene with both objects (tick marks), and the other 4 colours, which share a channel with yellow, lose one of the objects (cross marks).

This is Figure 5 because the paper's Figure 1 only shows composition succeeding: SDXL composing the author's dog with oil-painting style. It has no failure case. A second close fit, not downloaded, is Figure 7 at https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04549v3/figures/dog_horse_hat_3.png. Its caption reads: "Figure 7: Composing Entangled Concepts. The left image composes the text-conditions “photo of a dog” with “photo of a horse”, which both control the subject of the image, and produces unexpected results. In contrast, the right image composes “photo of a dog” with “photo, with red hat,” which intuitively correspond to disentangled features. Both samples from SDXL using score-composition with an unconditional background; details in Appendix C."

**[Du et al. Figure 2](figures/du-2023-product-sampling.png)**

The paper is Du et al. (2023), *Reduce, Reuse, Recycle: Compositional Generation with Energy-Based Diffusion Models and MCMC*, arXiv:2302.11552 (HTML version v6). The image URL is https://arxiv.org/html/2302.11552v6/comp_fig4.png, and the image is 2048 × 588 px.

Caption, quoted: "Figure 2: An illustration of product and mixture compositional models, and the improved sampling performance of MCMC in both cases. Left to right: Component distributions, ground truth composed distribution, reverse diffusion samples, HMC samples. Top: product, bottom: mixture. Reverse diffusion fails to sample from composed models."

In the top row, a ring of 8 Gaussian clusters is multiplied by a vertical bar, and the true product is just the 2 clusters inside the bar. Summing the two scores during reverse diffusion still scatters points around the ring, while HMC sampling lands on the 2 correct clusters. The bottom row shows the same comparison for a mixture of two distributions.
