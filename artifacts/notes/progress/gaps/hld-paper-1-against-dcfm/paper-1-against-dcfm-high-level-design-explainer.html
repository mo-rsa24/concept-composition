Where each box comes from, and what is not drawn

Where each box comes from

n data silos: the client counts per dataset, DCFM pp. 6 to 8, as listed in Loop 4's DCFM note under "What DCFM leaves open". Raw images never leave a silo (the note's opening paragraph).

One flow-matching expert per silo: Stage I, DCFM p5, Eqs. 8 to 11, per the note's "What it does".

Retrain every expert: DCFM-A, p5, Eqs. 12 to 14. The clients run an allgather over the weights, so every client holds every peer's expert, then retrains its own against the frozen peers (p5, read in the PDF). "DCFM-A generally requires 1-3 rounds (R ~ 2 on average)", at a cost of R times N model transfers per client (p9, Table 3, read in the PDF). The penalty is CoInD's carried across clients (the note's opening; the attack plan's paragraph on code).

Distil into one student: DCFM-B, p6, Eqs. 15 to 17. "After Stage I, each client shares their model with either a trusted server, or with other clients" (p5), and the student is trained on points the frozen experts generate (p6, Eq. 15). "Every peer uploads local model to central server, and receives a distilled model in return", a cost of 2 model transfers per client (p9, Table 3). All read in the PDF.

Compose the retrained experts: the flow form of classifier-free composition, p3, Eq. 5, per the note's "What it does".

Unseen combinations, scored: Table 1, p7, as copied in the note and in the numbers file rows DCFM-T1. 25.19 is Decentralized Diffusion Models, which composes finished experts at sampling time. The sample count behind each FID is not stated in DCFM (p7).

Two data holders and Two finished experts: problem-statements.md, Paper 1, "The problem" and "The setting" (allowed: the two trained models, samples drawn from them, and a helper trained on those samples; excluded: any joint image, joint prompt or model trained on the union).

A small checker and Merge, then push: problem-statements.md, Paper 1, "The proposed method": the merged noise predictions minus the checker's push, lambda_t sigma_t times the gradient of log C_t(both concepts present | x_t). Each expert keeps its own region only where the places are fixed (digits on halves, the fixed-place CLEVR test). In the two-colour CLEVR test the experts are told what to draw and not where, so the push goes on their plain product (method-search/05-two-experts-by-colour/design.md, lines 27 and 28), and on Colored MNIST the merge is also a plain product, because a digit and its colour sit on one digit (gaps/04-exploiting-dcfm.md, lead 1 and experiment 1).

Objects and their effects, scored: the CLEVR target of 7 objects from problem-statements.md, "What it is measured on"; the shadow and the X-ray border from the note's table and from the attack plan's row 1 (measure C, the heart's left border).

How to read the lines

A solid arrow hands a model, its samples or its predictions to the next step. Its label says what crosses and when: training time, after training, or sampling time. "Nothing crosses" marks a step that uses only the holder's own data.

The dashed arrow at the bottom is not a hand-over. It marks the one assumption that separates the methods: every DCFM penalty pushes the joint velocity toward the unconditional velocity plus one difference per attribute (p3, Eq. 7; p5, Eq. 12; p6, Eq. 16), which removes any effect that needs both attributes at once. Paper 1's evaluation measures exactly those effects. DCFM never tests this; the note infers it from the equations, not from a result.

The boxes are lined up by time: data on the top row, training on the second and third, sampling on the fourth, scoring on the last.

What is not drawn

The finished-expert baseline behind 25.19 (Decentralized Diffusion Models) also moves weights: one chosen client receives the other N minus 1 models at the end of local training (p9). Paper 1's setting allows the two trained models to whoever composes them, and the picture does not draw that hand-over as a box.

DCFM-B run on a single client after one exchange instead of a trusted server (p6): same crossing as the server, so not drawn separately.

Stage I's masked labels and DCFM-A's routing vector (p5): inside the training boxes, not crossings.

DCFM's other baselines (federated flow, Diffusion Federated Dataset) and its robot-planning and chest X-ray results: in the note; only one Colored MNIST pair of numbers is on the face.

Where Paper 1's samples are drawn and who runs the checker: the problem statement allows the two trained models and their samples to whoever composes, and says no more.

Paper 1's numbers: none on the face. Experiment 1 of the attack plan (two-silo Colored MNIST, plain product against product with the push) has not run, and the digit result on the void list stays out of any claim until the owner decides it.

DCFM's code is not public (attack plan, checked 2026-11-25), so any DCFM row Paper 1 reports beyond the printed numbers is a reimplementation.
