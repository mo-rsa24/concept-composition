# What we tried: reproducing rival methods and their printed numbers

This group covers the reruns of rival methods' published numbers kept under `reproductions/`, run for the rival reruns step (D2) on 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-23, plus the checker architecture comparison (D3) whose folder sits beside them. It holds seven attempts, one per folder that has a review: Rectify Then Diffuse (RTD), CO3, CoInD, Token Merging (ToMe), CoMat, Feynman-Kac Correctors (FKC) and the checker choice. Every reproduction used one test written before its first run: a row counts as reproduced when the printed score lies inside a pass band of our score ± 1.96 × √2 × our bootstrap standard error over prompts (no paper prints a standard error, so the printed one is taken as equal to ours), and as not reproduced otherwise; the rule sits in `scripts/reproductions/verdict.py` in the replications hub. Every score is on the prompts the subject guard allows, with the count stated, by the owner's answer to board question 14. Across the folders, [the reproduced-or-not table](../../reproductions/table.csv) records 2 rows reproduced (CoMat on Color, ToMe on Texture), 3 not reproduced (RTD and CO3 on Complex, ToMe on Shape), 13 not comparable and 2 blocked. [The reviewer's pass of 2026-11-24](../2026-11-24-review.md#is-the-contribution-new) checks none of these verdicts except FKC's, which it agrees was not reproduced by the owner's choice, and [the manager's table of things that may be wrong](../../manager/status.md#things-that-may-be-wrong) has no row on any reproduction; its one row bearing on them is that the same CO3 run gives different pictures on different card types. Folders with a design and no review (Attend-and-Excite, Bradley-Projective, Bradley-Local, CLEVR, Liu, Logical Guidance, SuperDiff, and the short inclusive fine-tune with the push (D5), closed as not run) have no entry here. Every difference between our score and a printed one below was computed for this ledger from the two numbers the review gives.

### Rectify Then Diffuse (RTD) on T2I-CompBench Complex and RareBench Concat

- **Asked:** Does the authors' released RTD code, run at their printed settings, give the ImageReward scores RTD's Table 2 prints?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** The authors' `RTDPipeline` at commit `241eb37` on SDXL Base 1.0 (deterministic DDIM, 50 steps, guidance 5.0, one update of 0.02 of the starting noise's length), on 988 of 1,000 T2I-CompBench Complex prompts and 33 of 40 RareBench Concat prompts at seeds 0, 1 and 2. That is 3,063 pictures for RTD and 3,063 for plain SDXL from the same starting noise, on RTX 3090s, scored by ImageReward v1.0 from the `image-reward` 1.5 package.
- **Bar, written first:** reproduced when |ours − printed| ≤ 1.96 × √(SE_ours² + SE_printed²), with SE the bootstrap standard error from 10,000 resamples of prompts (each prompt's 3 pictures kept together). Plain SDXL takes the same test against its printed score, and if it fails while prompts and scorer are right, the RTD row goes to diagnosis before any verdict. Controls: with the update size at 0, the RTD path must match plain SDXL's final latent to under 1e-5 (largest absolute difference), and the real update must move it by more than 1e-3.
- **Result:** ImageReward is a learned score of how well a picture matches its prompt, averaged over pictures; higher is better and it can be negative.
  - Complex: RTD scores 0.2423 (pass band 0.1751 to 0.3095) against 0.4661 printed. Ours is 0.2238 below the printed score, and the printed score sits 0.1566 above the top of the band.
  - Concat: RTD scores 0.4166 (band −0.0268 to 0.8600) against 0.4464 printed, 0.0298 below and inside the band.
  - Plain SDXL, the check on prompts and scorer: Complex 0.2637 (band 0.2019 to 0.3256) against 0.3083 printed, 0.0446 below; Concat 0.3035 (band −0.1269 to 0.7338) against 0.1018 printed, 0.2017 above. Both reproduced.
  - RTD minus plain SDXL, matched picture by picture: −0.0214 ImageReward on Complex (standard error 0.0153 over prompts), where the two printed scores differ by +0.1578; +0.1131 on Concat (standard error 0.0983).
  - Controls: the zero update differs from plain SDXL by 0.0 against the 1e-5 bar; the real update moves the final latent by 3.25 against the 1e-3 floor.
  - On the 463 two-subject Complex prompts (from the CO3 review), RTD scores 0.3689 against plain SDXL's 0.3678, a gain of 0.0011 ImageReward (standard error 0.0239).
- **Verdict:** null on Complex (not reproduced); support on Concat (reproduced, a weak test because 33 prompts give a band from −0.0268 to 0.8600). By the owner's answer to board question 26, the printed 0.4661 stays Paper 2's bar on Complex (target 0.4761), with our 0.2423 reported beside it. Two records disagree on Concat's matched standard error: the RTD review gives 0.0983, and [the rival reproduction finding](../../../../../1-lora-correction/report/against-other-methods/do-the-rivals-reproduce-their-printed-scores.md#where-the-sources-disagree) recomputes 0.0982; no result file stores it.
- **Why it failed:** unknown, in the review's own word. Verified (measured): the pictures are clean on 8 Complex prompts drawn at random; the update ran (none of the 2,964 Complex scores is identical between the two methods, and the zero-update control passes); the code and pinned libraries are the authors'; plain SDXL returns the paper's plain scores, so prompts and scorer match. Verified on 8 prompts only: ImageReward does not always follow the composition (on the lemon and lime prompt, RTD's separate lemons and limes score −0.90 against −0.02 for a plain SDXL picture of limes with lemon-coloured flesh), and how often this happens is not measured. Inferred, and separated by no run: the authors' NVIDIA H200 against our RTX 3090s, an ImageReward package or preprocessing difference the paper does not state, or the printed number itself.
- **Kind of failure:** on Complex, three kinds remain candidates and nothing run separates them. **The idea** (RTD as printed): the authors' code, settings, prompts and scorer all checked out, both controls passed, and RTD shows no gain over matched plain SDXL (−0.0214, standard error 0.0153) (verified). **Done wrong**: the card type or an unstated ImageReward package or preprocessing differs from the authors' (inferred). **Could not measure it**: ImageReward scored a correct composition below a fused one on 1 of 8 prompts looked at (verified on 8, frequency unmeasured). On Concat, **test design**: 33 prompts give a band from −0.0268 to 0.8600, wide enough to pass almost any printed score near ours (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** unclear. Two runs would tell the kinds apart: the same pipeline rendered on an H200 and scored with the authors' ImageReward package and preprocessing (if 0.4661 comes back, the setup was the cause), and a blind count of composition on a sample of matched RTD and plain SDXL pictures (if RTD composes more often while ImageReward says it does not, the scorer was the cause). If neither moves the number, the idea as printed fails here.
- **Rules out:** Do not credit RTD with its printed +0.1578 gain over plain SDXL on Complex; on this setup it shows none, so compare against RTD only on matched pictures from the same setup. Do not rerun the released pipeline unchanged to chase the gap: it has returned 0.2423 with both controls and the plain SDXL check passing.
- **Leaves open:** which of the three open causes explains the Complex miss, and how often ImageReward scores a correct composition below a fused one.
- **Evidence:** [the RTD review](../../reproductions/RTD/review.md), [the RTD design](../../reproductions/RTD/design.md), [plain SDXL beside RTD on eight random Complex prompts](../../reproductions/RTD/plain-sdxl-against-rtd-eight-random-complex-prompts-seed0.png), [the checks behind the Complex miss](../../reproductions/RTD/why-the-complex-score-misses.png); W&B `5i9dt7by` (RTD Complex), `tc75fx0i` (RTD Concat), `8802binw` (plain SDXL Complex), `1gh0vhil` (plain SDXL Concat); scoring jobs 93064 and 93068; the rival reruns step (D2); board question 26.

Inferred: the released script's settings differ from section 4.1's on guidance, corrected steps and beta, and the design names that as a possible cause of a miss; the three causes RTD left open (card type, ImageReward package, the printed number) apply here too. Verified elsewhere, effect on this score not measured: Loop 6 found the same CO3 run gives different pictures on an RTX 8000 and an RTX 3090 (none of 21 checked trajectories within 3 of 255 in mean pixel value). Inferred in the design: RTD probably did not rerun CO3, since 19 of the 20 baseline ImageReward cells in RTD's Table 2 equal CO3's own table.

The card-type and scorer candidates left open by RTD (**done wrong**, **could not measure it**) apply here too (inferred). **The idea** is the weakest candidate: CO3 does lift matched plain SDXL, by 0.0950 on the two-subject prompts (standard error 0.0303) (verified). On Concat, **test design**: the band runs from −0.0678 to 0.9425 on 33 prompts (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** unclear. A rerun with section 4.1's settings (DDIM at guidance 5.0, 10 corrected steps, beta 0.8) on the same prompts, seeds and cards would tell whether the settings explain the miss; if it still misses, the RTD checks above (an H200, the authors' scorer package) decide between setup and the printed number.
- **Rules out:** Do not cite CO3's printed 0.4406 as what its released code does on this setup, and do not carry RTD's printed ordering of RTD above CO3 into this project. Any comparison against CO3 states the released script's settings and uses matched pictures.

- **Evidence:** [the CO3 review](../../reproductions/CO3/review.md), [the CO3 design](../../reproductions/CO3/design.md), [what the paper states beside what the released script runs](../../reproductions/CO3/paper-against-released-script.png); W&B `02fl0ia0` (Complex), `i11r81ox` (Concat), `6ej2ebnk` and `qci2qjgy` (1% rung); scoring job 93326; the rival reruns step (D2); board questions 16 and 18.

### CoInD and its composed baseline on CelebA

- **Asked:** Do CoInD's released SD1.5 weights reproduce the conformity scores in its Table 6: 49.15% for CoInD and 14.19% for Composed GLIDE (the Liu row), where the conformity score is the percentage of generated faces a classifier reads as both smiling and male?
- **Paper:** 1.
- **Tried:** Both released U-Nets were unpacked from `sd_celeba.zip` (13.8 GB of safetensors; the 8 pickle files stayed sealed) and each rendered one batch of 5 faces. A ResNet-18 conformity classifier was trained from the authors' config for 10,000 and for 30,000 steps and read on CelebA's 19,962 test faces. No generated face was scored: CelebA became context by the owner's answer to board question 35 before the 1% rung.
- **Bar, written first:** the classifier counts when its overall test accuracy is within one percentage point of both accuracies CoInD prints, 98.2% for gender and 92.1% for smile (board question 30, `MAX_ACCURACY_GAP` in `coind_celeba.py`); this replaced the 90% per-group bar the design first set. The classifier must memorise at least 15 of 16 images. A conformity row was to be judged by the pass-band rule over the first 2,500 images per method (board question 27), and to stay not comparable if the two classifiers disagreed by more than the band.
- **Result:**
  - Memorising: 16 of 16 right on both male and smiling, against a bar of 15.
  - 10,000-step classifier: 97.93% gender (0.27 points under the print) and 92.96% smile (0.86 points over), W&B `zolb4ltw`.
  - 30,000-step classifier: 97.98% gender (0.22 points under) and 92.38% smile (0.28 points over), W&B `uo78zzgu`.
  - Weakest group: reading smiling on smiling men, 88.75% at 10,000 steps and 89.01% at 30,000, both under the replaced 90% per-group bar; tripling the training moved it by 0.26 percentage points.
  - Checkpoint control: the first face from each U-Net differs by checksum, so two different U-Nets loaded. Rendering took 6.1 seconds per face on an RTX 3090. Nobody has looked at these faces.
  - Conformity scores: not measured.
- **Verdict:** not run for the conformity rows; support for the classifier check.
- **Why it failed:** it stopped before any face was scored, by the owner's decision on board question 35: Paper 1 gives each model its own region of the picture, and CelebA asks for several attributes on one object (stated reason). Verified: the classifier misses smiling on about 11% of smiling men; inferred: that cell would pull a conformity score down, since the score counts faces read as both smiling and male.
- **Kind of failure:** none of the six for CoInD's method, which was never scored: the stop was the owner's scope decision. The nearest kind is **wrong data**, for Paper 1 and not for CoInD: CelebA asks for several attributes on one object, which Paper 1's own-region method cannot address (stated in the owner's answer to board question 35). The classifier check, the only part that ran, passed (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** unclear. Scoring the first 2,500 faces per method with both classifiers, about 8.5 GPU-hours, would tell whether 49.15% and 14.19% come back; it is not owed, because CelebA is context.
- **Rules out:** Do not use CelebA, or Shapes3D and Colored MNIST, which the same answer made context, as a Paper 1 target. Do not train this classifier longer to fix the smiling-men cell: three times the steps moved it 0.26 points.
- **Leaves open:** whether the released weights give 49.15% and 14.19%; the 10 smoke-test faces are unread.
- **Evidence:** [the CoInD review](../../reproductions/CoInD/review.md), [the CoInD design](../../reproductions/CoInD/design.md); W&B `zolb4ltw` and `uo78zzgu`; jobs 93120, 93123, 93130, 93131 and 93210; no picture is filed, because CelebA's licence keeps every face on the cluster; the rival reruns step (D2); board questions 27, 30 and 35.

### Token Merging (ToMe) on T2I-CompBench Shape and Texture

Control: with token updates and attention refinement off, ToMe's loop must match plain SDXL to under 1e-5 (largest absolute latent difference) on one card.
- **Result:** BLIP-VQA is a question-answering model's probability that the prompt's attributes appear in the picture, averaged over pictures, from 0 to 1, higher is better.
  - Scorer canary: 0.7110, 0.9217 and 0.8554 on the benchmark's three example pictures, the scores its repository ships, a largest gap of 0.0 against a bar of 0.01.
  - Plain SDXL on Shape: 0.4825 (band 0.4301 to 0.5348) against 0.5408 printed by ToMe and RPG, 0.0583 below, with the printed score 0.0060 above the band's top: not reproduced. CoMat prints 0.4687, inside our band.

  - ToMe on Shape: 0.5502 on 293 prompts (band 0.4976 to 0.6028) against 0.6051 printed, 0.0549 below, with the printed score 0.0023 above the band's top. On the 291 prompts needing no parse repair: 0.5495 (band 0.4968 to 0.6022).
  - ToMe on Texture: 0.6503 on 300 prompts (band 0.5970 to 0.7036) against 0.6894 printed, 0.0391 below and inside the band.
  - ToMe's gain over plain SDXL: 0.0677 on Shape against 0.0643 in the paper's printed numbers, and 0.1271 on Texture against 0.1257. On the two-subject prompts the gain is 0.0869 on 209 Shape prompts (standard error 0.0195) and 0.1366 on 234 Texture prompts (standard error 0.0238).
  - Repeatability, stock SDXL in this environment: the default attention backends differ by up to 0.00195 on one U-Net call over 4 repeats and by 1.476 in the final latent of a full 50-step render over 3 repeats; the math backend repeats exactly (0.0) but runs out of a 24 GB card during ToMe's gradient steps. Seen on three nodes.
  - Control: 0.0 against 1e-5 on the math backend; the real update moves the latent by 3.56 against the 1e-3 floor. Cost: median 57 seconds per picture.
- **Verdict:** null on Shape (not reproduced, by a small margin); support on Texture (reproduced). By the owner's answer to board question 39, Shape keeps its verdict with the missed plain SDXL check printed beside it. Records disagree: the review's opening line says "ToMe has no verdict yet" while its own sections, [the ToMe rows file](../../reproductions/ToMe/rows.json) and the reproduced-or-not table give both verdicts, and the rival reproduction finding read on 2026-11-24 lists ToMe as having none.
- **Why it failed:** on Shape, verified: plain SDXL on the same pipeline misses its printed Shape score by a similar amount (0.0583 against ToMe's 0.0549), and ToMe's gain over plain SDXL nearly equals the printed gain (0.0677 against 0.0643). Inferred, as the review says: part of the gap comes from the setup. Verified: two papers print different plain SDXL Shape scores (0.5408 and 0.4687), and ours sits inside the band around CoMat's. Verified to exist, effect on the score not measured: run-to-run noise from the attention backends sits inside every ToMe score. Inferred and untested: the released config optimises 5 steps where the paper says 10.
- **Why it worked:** on Texture, verified: plain SDXL reproduces, no prompt needed a parse repair, and ToMe's gain over plain SDXL matches the printed gain to 0.0014.
- **Kind of failure:** on Shape, **done wrong** (inferred): plain SDXL misses by a similar amount on the same pipeline, so the shared setup, which the paper leaves unstated on guidance, negative prompt and seeds, and which optimises 5 steps where the paper says 10, sits below the authors'. **Test design** (verified): one seed, run-to-run attention noise inside every score, a printed score only 0.0023 above the band's top, and a reference plain SDXL score that two papers print differently (0.5408 and 0.4687). **The idea** is not supported as the cause: ToMe's gain over plain SDXL, 0.0677, sits beside the printed 0.0643 (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** unclear, leaning toward the setup. A pipeline on which plain SDXL returns ToMe's printed 0.5408, then ToMe on that pipeline, would tell; seeds 1 and 2 would narrow the band so a 0.0023 margin decides less.
- **Rules out:** Do not read a ToMe score without the plain SDXL rerun on the same pipeline beside it; read ToMe as a gain over matched plain SDXL. Do not expect a ToMe render to repeat bit for bit on torch 2.0.1's default attention, so compare averages over prompts and never single pictures. Do not run ToMe's released parser unrepaired on Complex, where 145 of 988 prompts crash it.
- **Leaves open:** which printed plain SDXL Shape score is right for this benchmark, whether the paper's 10 optimised steps reach 0.6051, and any seed beyond 0.
- **Evidence:** [the ToMe review](../../reproductions/ToMe/review.md), [the ToMe design](../../reproductions/ToMe/design.md), [the 1% sheet of 16 pictures at seed 0](../../reproductions/ToMe/tome-one-percent-seed0.png); W&B `8cowoyea` (Shape), `c8c153mf` (Shape without repaired prompts), `ljypsxnd` (Texture), `92suk4nc` and `acw7fxeb` (plain SDXL Shape and Texture); jobs 93303, 93317 and 93213 (scorer canary); the rival reruns step (D2); board questions 17, 18, 20, 39 and 43.

### Feynman-Kac Correctors (FKC) on GenEval two objects

- **Asked:** Does FKC on SDXL give the 0.78 two-object GenEval score in its Table A4, on GenEval's released 553 prompts?
- **Paper:** 2 (the GenEval row is a Paper 2 target; [the rivals list](../../targets/rivals.md) files FKC as a Paper 1 rival).
- **Tried:** Plain SDXL on the 99 two-object prompts at 4 images each (396 images), scored by GenEval's Mask2Former evaluation at commit `af4902f`, and one batch of 8 FKC particles timed with a port of the authors' EDM2 weighting and resampling to SDXL. FKC itself was never rendered, by the owner's answer to board question 41.

### Choosing Paper 2's checker architecture (D3)

- **Asked:** Of three ways of reading the half-drawn picture (a reader of SDXL's own features, a classifier on the noisy latent, a reader of the cross-attention maps), which one's gradient push gives CO3 the most pictures with two clear subjects on pairs it never trained on?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** nothing ran. The design fixed 12 tuning pairs at seeds 6001 to 6008 and 12 held-out pairs at seeds 7001 to 7008, about 20 GPU-hours in all, and the owner closed the route on 2026-11-25, answering board question 111 with A, before any candidate trained.
- **Bar, written first:** a candidate is eligible only if a zero push reproduces both plain PoE (the sampler that adds the two single-subject noise predictions) and CO3 to under 1e-5 (largest absolute latent difference), it rejects at least 80% of 200 pictures of other subjects, and its size-matched random push makes at most 2 pictures with two clear subjects of 96. The eligible candidate with the most two-clear pictures of 96 on the CO3 row wins, a lead under 5 counting as a tie; it beats CO3 alone when it leads by at least 5 of 96 and by more than 1.96 standard errors over 10,000 resamples of the 12 pairs. The owner set this on board question 87 on 2026-11-24, before any result existed.
- **Result:** no D3 number exists. The review records the findings from neighbouring steps that closed it:
  - A lookup that knows only which animal pair a picture belongs to scores 0.757 balanced accuracy on the fresh seeds (balanced accuracy is the mean of the hit rates on two-clear and other pictures; 0.5 is chance, 0.75 was every reader's bar).
  - The first per-place reader scored 0.688 on the tuning seeds and 0.632 on the fresh ones, the size-corrected reader 0.635, both below that lookup. Scored only between pictures of the same pair, the per-place reader ranks two-clear pictures above others 0.39 to 0.51 of the time across steps 5 to 30 (0.5 is chance).
  - The kept checker's push has a median cosine of +0.105 at step 0 on CO3, and +0.025 to +0.089 on plain PoE, with r_t = ε(joint) − (ε(A) + ε(B) − ε(empty)), the term composition drops (cosine 1 means the same direction, 0 unrelated).
  - The kept checker and two retrained ones separate CO3's clean pictures from its failures at 0.602, 0.617 and 0.648 balanced accuracy, under the 0.65 floor.
- **Verdict:** closed (not run).
- **Why it failed:** verified (measured in the identity-reader, gradient-direction and checker-balancing steps): the readers tried so far learned which pair a picture shows rather than what it shows, the trained checkers cannot see CO3's failure, and the push lines up only weakly with the dropped term.
