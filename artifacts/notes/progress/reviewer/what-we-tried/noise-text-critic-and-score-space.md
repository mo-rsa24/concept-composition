# What we tried: turning concepts into scores, and the score path as a third space

This group covers two loops that asked whether something other than SDXL's own features could read a half-drawn picture early enough to steer it. The loop that turned concepts into scores (Loop 9, folder `noise-text-critic/`) tested sentence scores: a CLIP-style image encoder's similarity to a right sentence minus a wrong one, from pretrained NoisyCLIP, frozen CLIP and Latent CLIP, and from a model trained here on digits. The loop on the score path as a third space (Loop 10, folder `score-space/`) tested whether a diffusion model's own noise predictions, read as a set, say which concepts are present, and whether text and scores could share one space where adding means composing. The group holds **12 attempts**: 10 from the sentence-score loop and the score-path loop that ran or were judged, and 2 designed and then not run (the sentence-score push on CO3, and the checker choosing CO3's setting). Entries are in the order their results landed on the [progress board](../../board.json) on 2026-11-23 and 2026-11-24. The digit test the score-path loop designed last (whether the experts' vote or simply holding a side places the digits) ran as Loop 6's step M11 and is recorded in [the method-search group](method-search.md). The loops that read the field for gaps (Loop 14) and for directions and dials (Loop 15) ran no experiments; their open ideas close this page.

Every balanced accuracy below is on a 0 to 1 scale where 0.5 is chance: the mean of the share of label-1 and the share of label-0 pictures called correctly.

### Sentence scores reading five properties on half-drawn SDXL latents (W2)

- **Asked:** Can a sentence score tell a right sentence from a wrong one on an SDXL latent at steps 0 to 15 of 50, where a push would act, for counting, prompt match, left-right order, position and size, and attribute binding?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** The 220 labelled cat and dog pictures in `eval_set_catdog_v2.json` (441 latents once the 64 relation pictures were mirrored and recoloured) were noised forward to steps 0 to 15 with 4 noise draws per step, plus steps 20, 30, 40 and 49 as a check, and scored by NoisyCLIP, frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 and Latent CLIP. Thresholds were chosen on one half of the seeds and read on the other half. Size and colour labels came from SDXL's word attention maps for "cat" and "dog".
- **Bar, written first:** a property reads when its mean balanced accuracy over steps 0 to 15 on the reading half is at least 0.75; it beats frozen CLIP when the gap exceeds 1.96 standard errors from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of seeds; it is inconclusive when its labels fail the 90% eye check or either half holds fewer than 8 pictures of a label. Constants in `w2_read.py`.
- **Result:** Mean balanced accuracy over steps 0 to 15 (NoisyCLIP, frozen CLIP, Latent CLIP): counting 0.515, 0.496, 0.560; prompt match 0.503, 0.478, 0.479; left-right order 0.479, 0.506, 0.507; position and size 0.538, 0.482, 0.510 (1 right picture per half); binding 0.492, 0.467, 0.481. No gap over frozen CLIP passed 1.96 standard errors except NoisyCLIP on position and size (0.056, standard error 0.015), which rests on 2 right pictures and does not count. As a check that the models work, NoisyCLIP's prompt match reads 0.67, 0.78, 0.86 and 0.82 at steps 20, 30, 40 and 49, and the step-49 check passed (0.91 for NoisyCLIP and 0.73 for Latent CLIP on the threshold half, against 0.6). On finished pictures NoisyCLIP's counting score was right on 107 of 109 clean pairs, 23 of 46 two-of-a-kind pictures and 18 of 29 hybrids. The label eye check: left-right 62 of 64 right (96.9%, passes), size 37 of 57 (64.9%, fails), recoloured copies 18 of 32 (56%, fails), against 90%.
- **Verdict:** null on counting, prompt match and left-right order; inconclusive on position and size and on binding. **The records disagree:** this review says null, while the later fairness test's review (W2c, below) says W2 is inconclusive by the bars fixed beforehand, because the positive control read 0.637 against its 0.65 floor. Board question 79 kept both: W2's own verdict on its title line, and the fairness test's reading as its own finding. Loop 12's design audit (row 16) says only the label is at stake.
- **Why it failed:** NoisyCLIP's noise training moves reading earlier, to about step 20 of 50 (it leads frozen CLIP on prompt match by 0.15 at step 20 and 0.16 at step 30), and not into steps 0 to 15 (verified). Size and colour labels failed because the word "cat" lands mostly on the cat's head, making the cat look small in 20 size calls and leaving its body uncoloured or coloured as the dog in 7 of 16 checked pictures (verified by eye). The counting sentences mix species with count: "one animal that is part cat and part dog" scores within 0.03 of the right sentence on clean pairs, so two cats fall on the wrong side half the time (cosines measured, reading inferred). Whether NoisyCLIP's paper, which reports separation by latent 10 of 50, counts steps the same way as this sampler is not settled (inferred).
- **Kind of failure:** **the idea**, for counting, prompt match and left-right order: the test was fair on those three, because the models read prompt match once the picture is mostly drawn (0.82 at step 49), the input path matched the authors' at cosine 0.995, and the later positive control showed SDXL's own features read the same pictures (W2c). **Could not measure it**, for position and size and for binding: the labels came from word maps that cover the cat's head, and failed the eye check (37 of 57 and 18 of 32 against 90%). **Test design**, for the verdict's label only: the positive control was added afterwards and landed 0.013 under its floor, which is why two verdicts exist.
- **Idea still alive?** Closed for counting, prompt match and left-right order at SDXL steps 0 to 15. Unclear for position and size and for binding: labels that pass a full-size eye check, then the same read, would tell a failure of the idea from a failure of the labels.
- **Rules out:** Do not use pretrained NoisyCLIP, frozen CLIP or Latent CLIP sentence scores to read counting, prompt match or left-right order at SDXL steps 0 to 15. Do not take a subject's region from SDXL's word attention map for size or colour labels, because the noun's attention covers the head. Do not let a step pass a push gate on one half with 15 pictures per class, where one step's balanced accuracy has a standard error of about 0.07 (inferred from the counts).
- **Leaves open:** position and size, and binding, with labels that pass the eye check; whether a sentence score reads mid-run, after step 20.
- **Evidence:** [the W2 review](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/review.md), [its design](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/design.md), [the result file](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/result.json), [balanced accuracy by step for all five properties](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/balanced-accuracy-by-step.png), [six finished pictures with NoisyCLIP's counting scores](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/example/noisyclip-counting-on-six-finished-pictures.png), [the eye-check calls](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/label-check/eye-labels.csv), W&B `70g3qw2f`, job 94105 (board step W2; questions 68 and 79).

### Whether CLIP's text encoder alone separates colour binding and word order (W2's text-only word check)

- **Asked:** Before any image is read, can the text encoder NoisyCLIP keeps frozen (CLIP ViT-L/14) tell "a red cat and a blue dog" from its swap, and "a cat to the left of a dog" from its swap, at all?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** `word_check.py` on the laptop's CPU over six noun pairs. Each swapped pair was averaged over both word orders, so what survives is the part of the swap a sum of word slots cannot produce. The ratio is that part's size over twice the swap's size: 0 for a pure sum of word slots, 1 when the whole swap survives. The same encoder with random weights was the control.
- **Bar, written first:** none recorded.
- **Result:** Colour binding: ratio 0.72 (range 0.48 to 0.94) in trained CLIP against 0.13 with random weights; swapped sentences' cosine 0.956. Left-right order: ratio 0.28 (0.22 to 0.33) against 0.08; swapped cosine 0.965; an order swap moves a sentence 0.19 to 0.34, against 0.63 to 0.87 for changing one noun. With random weights the colour-swap direction is the same for every noun pair (mean cosine 0.96 across pairs); in trained CLIP it is not (0.24).
- **Verdict:** support, with no bar written beforehand, so this is a measurement and not a judged pass.
- **Why it worked:** Trained CLIP's text side carries colour binding as something beyond a sum of word slots, and carries word order weakly (verified on six noun pairs).
- **Kind of failure:** none. No bar was written first, so this is a support by measurement and not a passed bar.
- **Idea still alive?** Alive, with the caveat that it covers only CLIP ViT-L/14's text side on six noun pairs: colour binding is carried strongly, word order weakly, and nothing here speaks for the image side.
- **Rules out:** Do not blame the text encoder when a CLIP-style score fails on colour binding; look at the image side. Do not expect a large left-right target from this text encoder: an order swap moves a sentence about a third as far as changing one noun.
- **Leaves open:** the image side, which this check never reads; part binding; noun pairs beyond the six.
- **Evidence:** [the word check in the W2 review](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/review.md#text-only-word-check), [its numbers](../../noise-text-critic/02-can-it-read/word-check/word-check.json) (board step W2).

### The digit model's ten noise predictions read which digits are present (X5)

- **Asked:** Does the score path, read as the signature (the ten maps $d_t(c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)$ for digit labels 0 to 9 on one noisy canvas, signed and unscaled), let a small probe read which digits are present, and better than the noisy canvas?
- **Paper:** both.
- **Tried:** On the digit toy (32 by 64 canvases of pasted MNIST digits, a pixel model of 6,141,185 weights that never saw two digits on one canvas), one probe per timestep was fitted on 4,000 `train` canvases and read on 2,000 `seen_test` canvases with a different noise draw, in two windows: the brief's steps 0 to 15 (timesteps 999 to 693) and the window matched to SDXL's steps 0 to 15 (timesteps 712 to 480). Inputs beside the signature: the noisy canvas, raw noise, the error readout (each label's prediction against the true noise), path length, and the signature with labels shuffled. The probe changed twice before the full read, with no bar changed: a linear probe could not follow a digit that moves, so it became one small convolutional probe (question 72), and its control was rerun at full size with only the canvas row gating (question 74). Jobs 94160, 94180, 94184, 0.74 GPU-hours.
- **Bar, written first:** `MIN_PRESENCE = 0.75` and `MIN_OVER_NOISE = 0.10` in `x5_signal.py`: presence accuracy on the signature at least 0.75 at some step of a window, and at least 0.1 above the same probe on raw noise at that step. Null when the positive control and overfit test pass and both windows miss; inconclusive when the canvas row misses its control (0.9 at timestep 100) or the overfit test fails.
- **Result:** Early window: signature best 0.513 at timestep 754 (raw noise 0.493 there), a miss on both clauses. Matched window: signature best 0.700 at timestep 480, raw noise 0.499, so 0.20 over noise and 0.05 short of 0.75. At timestep 480 the noisy canvas reads 0.593, path length 0.621, the error readout 0.742 (its peak 0.746 at timestep 495, and 0.61 to 0.66 in the early window where nothing else reads); shuffled labels 0.49 to 0.51 throughout. On two separate digits, the asked pair against one wrong digit, the signature reads 0.655 at timestep 480, where Loop 8's two-map reader read 0.511. All 16 steps stacked read 0.607 in the early window and 0.674 in the matched one. Controls at timestep 100: linear probe 0.779 signature and 0.764 canvas (missed 0.9); convolutional at 1,000 canvases 0.802 and 0.890 (missed); convolutional at 4,000 canvases canvas 0.950 (passed) and signature 0.904. Overfit 16 of 16 (15 needed).
- **Verdict:** null. **The records disagree on what the null means.** The review and verdict call it a null by the written rule. The independent audit calls X5 "a fair test of readability and a weak test of additivity", and the alignment check, in the loop's own words, calls the failure "mostly the setup". Loop 12's design audit marks the window, the probe's data and the borrowed bar (rows 26 to 28) as possibly able to move the 0.05 miss, and says the null stands (row 29). A second disagreement: the verdict credits the 0.655 against 0.511 identity gap to the three things the signature keeps (all ten labels, the sign, the size), while Loop 8's identity-reader review finds that with one recipe in the same window the signature adds 0.049 (0.612 against 0.563); the audit says the files do not settle whether the two pairs of numbers measure the same thing. The manager records that the review once held five copying slips, corrected to its result file with the verdict unchanged (commit `77cf7bb7`).
- **Why it failed:** At the brief's steps 0 to 15 the digit canvas keeps 0.6% to 8.7% of its signal (amplitude share) and every input reads chance (verified). In the matched window the signature was still rising when the window ended: 0.680 at timestep 495 and 0.700 at 480 (verified). The audit adds four reasons, each inferred: the probe was still gaining from data (the canvas control rose from 0.890 at 1,000 canvases to 0.950 at 4,000, and the signature gives it ten channels to the canvas's one); the signature's own ceiling (0.904) sits below the canvas's (0.950), so an absolute 0.75 penalises it; the canvases were noised forward from known pictures, not drawn along a sampler's path; and 0.75 was borrowed from the unseen-pair tests, reached here from one run with no confidence interval.
- **Kind of failure:** **Test design**: the 0.75 bar was borrowed from unseen-pair tests, the window ended while the signature was still rising, the probe was still gaining from data, and the 0.05 miss comes from one run with no confidence interval (each from the audit, inferred). **Wrong data**: canvases noised forward from known pictures with one shared draw, where the owner's score path runs along a sampler's run (inferred). In the early window the result is **the idea** on digits: every input including the canvas read chance there while the controls passed (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** Unclear for reading digits from the signature at SDXL-matched noise: a probe fitted on all 20,000 training canvases, reported with a confidence interval, would tell whether 0.700 is the probe's limit or the signal's. Closed for reading at the digit model's timesteps 999 to 693. The question the signature was meant to serve, composing in the model's own coordinates, lives on in the block-wise form (see the X6 to X9 entry).
- **Rules out:** Do not read or push at the digit model's timesteps 999 to 693: nothing reads there, so a checker's gradient there is noise. Do not use the error readout as a push input, because it needs the noise that made the canvas, which a sampler never has (inferred that its score partly reflects that access). Do not use a linear probe on maps of digits that move. Do not read a stack of steps that share one noise draw as evidence that a path carries information, since a linear combination can cancel the shared draw (inferred).
- **Leaves open:** timesteps below 480, which no longer match SDXL's early steps; the signature along the sampler's own path; the stacked read with an independent draw per step; a probe fitted on all 20,000 training canvases.
- **Evidence:** [the X5 review](../../score-space/05-signal/review.md), [the design](../../score-space/04-design.md), [the result file](../../score-space/05-signal/full.json), [presence accuracy against timestep for every input](../../score-space/05-signal/presence-by-timestep.png), [where each probe's input comes from](../../score-space/05-signal/what-each-probe-reads.png), [the independent audit](../../score-space/12-independent-audit.md), [Loop 12's design audit](../../leakage/05-design-audit.md), [the manager's record](../../manager/status.md#decided-for-you-since-you-last-looked), W&B `gq51nl74` (board step X5; questions 71, 72, 74 and 76).

### A positive control and the fairest settings for the sentence scores (W2c)

- **Asked:** Is the sentence-score null fair: does a reader known to work read the same pictures under W2's exact rules, and do sentence scores still fail when each gets its best fair chance?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** The kept readout checker on SDXL's own U-Net features (`readout-catdog-more3-long/head_step_002000.pt`) ran as the positive control under W2's protocol. The sentence scores were rerun with prompt ensembling over 5 fixed wordings, at steps 20 to 29 (NoisyCLIP's training window), on a third task (the clean pair against every reject), and on 195 held-out real sampler runs of cat and dog (105 clean, 90 rejects) read at steps 0 to 20 with no noise added. The design was written after W2's scores were seen, with every addition a fixed outside practice and the bars fixed before any W2c score. Jobs 94113 and 94114 (the latter restarted after node mscluster63 failed 65 minutes in).
- **Bar, written first:** the control holds when the readout checker's mean over steps 0 to 15 on the clean pair is at least 0.65 and beats frozen CLIP by more than 1.96 standard errors; a sentence score reads when any row reaches a mean of 0.75 over steps 0 to 15; W2 stands when the control holds and no row reads; W2 is inconclusive when the control does not hold; each sentence row should reach 0.75 at step 49 on counting and the clean pair.
- **Result:** The control read 0.637 on the clean pair over steps 0 to 15 (0.801 over steps 20 to 29), 0.013 under its floor, and beat frozen CLIP by 0.150 (standard error 0.035); it read 0.646 on counting and 0.600 on prompt match. On real sampler states the readout checker read 0.696 over steps 0 to 15, and 0.50, 0.63, 0.73, 0.87 and 0.87 at steps 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20, beating frozen CLIP by 0.215 (standard error 0.042). The best sentence row was Latent CLIP's 0.574 on real states; no sentence row reached 0.64 at any single step from 0 to 20 there (highest 0.63, Latent CLIP at step 8). Ensembling moved no row by more than 0.04 over steps 0 to 15. In its own window NoisyCLIP read prompt match at 0.711 (0.734 ensembled) and the clean pair at 0.687 (0.711). At step 49 every sentence model passed the clean pair and failed counting (0.71, 0.67 and 0.54).
- **Verdict:** inconclusive by its bar, with no sentence score reading in any fair setting. **The records disagree:** W2's own review calls the same data null (see the W2 entry), and board question 79 keeps both readings side by side. Loop 12's design audit (row 16) notes that judging the floor on real states, where the control read 0.696, would set the bar after the result.
- **Why it failed:** The control's floor was judged on forward-noised pictures, where it read 0.637, while on real sampler states it read 0.696 (verified). The 220 pictures had been used to choose which checker iteration to keep, so the control row is slightly optimistic (stated in the design). Every comparison of the readout checker with a sentence score is mixed, because the reader and its input both change (inferred).
- **Kind of failure:** **The idea**, for sentence scores: every fair setting was tried (ensembling, NoisyCLIP's own window, real sampler states, a third task) and no row's mean reached 0.6, while the readout checker beat frozen CLIP by 0.150 (standard error 0.035) on the same pictures (verified). **Test design**, for the inconclusive label: the control's floor was judged on noised pictures, where it read 0.637, and not on the sampler states the push acts on, where it read 0.696 (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** Closed for sentence scores as an early reader. The inconclusive label could only be lifted by judging the floor on real states, which would now set the bar after the result, so it stays as recorded.
- **Rules out:** Do not retry sentence scores at SDXL steps 0 to 15 with prompt ensembling, NoisyCLIP's own window or the sampler's real states: each was tried and no row's mean reached 0.6. When a positive control's floor is written, say whether it is judged on noised pictures or sampler states, because the same reader scored 0.637 on one and 0.696 on the other (inferred lesson).
- **Leaves open:** The control read clean pairs, not identity: the reviewer notes "Loop 9's readout reached 0.87 for clean pairs, not identity". One reader, on cat and dog only.

- **Asked:** Does the counting failure come from W2's wording, which mixed species with count, so that sentences naming no species would read plurality?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** Right sentence "a photo of two animals" against "a photo of one animal" and "a photo of one animal with mixed features", plain and in the 5 ensembled wordings, scored on W2's saved image vectors so only the text changed. Job 94210, a few GPU-minutes.
- **Bar, written first:** a mean of at least 0.75 over steps 0 to 15 reads; 0.75 at step 49 passes the finished-picture check. Fixed by the owner's choice of 2026-11-24 before any score.

### The shared text-and-score space, and closing the score path (X6 to X8 skipped, X9)

- **Asked:** Can text, noisy canvases and the score path share one space in which adding two scores means adding two words, and what does the loop close?
- **Paper:** both.
- **Tried:** Nothing was trained. The design's three run groups (training the two-way and three-way spaces, drawing them, and two arithmetic tests) sat behind X5's bar, and the owner confirmed skipping them on question 76. The verdict then closed or narrowed three ideas, and after the independent audit it reads: the score path as a new source of information, closed; a shared space where adding scores is adding text, exact form closed and block-wise form open; a noise-aware CLIP pushing at SDXL's steps 0 to 15, closed for layout and count.
- **Bar, written first:** for the spaces, unseen-pair balanced accuracy at least 0.75 and within 0.05 of seen pairs; for the first arithmetic test, top-1 caption accuracy at least 0.75 on canvases judged two clear digits, with label-shuffled paths within 0.05 of chance; for the second, a mean cosine whose 95% bootstrap interval lies above the shuffled control's.
- **Result:** none for the spaces or arithmetic. The loop used 0.74 of its 6 GPU-hours.
- **Verdict:** not run (X6 to X8); closed (the loop).
- **Why it failed:** X5 missed its bar (verified). The audit argues the digit testbed could not pose the owner's question at all, because no digit model has a joint label, and the arithmetic tests that would have touched additivity sat behind a readability gate (inferred). **The records disagree on the closures.** On the score path, the session closed it because by the data-processing inequality it holds nothing the noisy canvas lacks; the audit agrees only for "a new source of information" and leaves it open as the coordinates in which to compose, since the inequality bounds information and says nothing about whether sums work there. On the shared space, the session replaced it with questions about the sampler and where the experts' data overlap; the audit finds the exact form closed by theory (text vectors add only under the same conditional independence the product of experts assumes: Linear Spaces of Meanings, p. 5; Composable Diffusion, p. 7) and the form where each subject owns its own block of coordinates open (Bradley et al., Theorem 6.1, p. 8). Both agree on closing the early CLIP push for layout and count.
- **Kind of failure:** **Test design**: the arithmetic tests, the only ones touching additivity, were gated behind a readability bar and never ran (verified). **Wrong data**: no digit model has a joint label, so "adding two scores means adding two words" could not be posed on digits (inferred, the audit). **Not understood**: the data-processing inequality was used to close the score path, and it bounds information while saying nothing about whether sums work in that space (inferred, the audit). Nothing here tested the idea fairly.
- **Idea still alive?** Closed in its exact form, by theory rather than by a run (inferred from Linear Spaces of Meanings, p. 5, and Composable Diffusion, p. 7). Retry worth it in the block-wise form: read each subject's own signed $d_t$ on CO3's path at the steps where the subjects have separated, turn the two into masks and judge them by Loop 6's written mask rule (under 0.1 GPU-hours, estimate).
- **Rules out:** Do not pose "adding scores means adding words" on a model with no joint label. Do not search for one shared space where "cat" plus "dog" equals "cat and dog" for subjects that co-occur, because the and-score that carries co-occurrence is exactly what such a sum sets to zero (inferred from the two theorems). Do not put the additivity tests behind a readability bar when additivity is the question.
- **Leaves open:** The block-wise form on SDXL: read each subject's own signed $d_t$ on CO3's path as a map of which body it acts on (idea D3), judged by Loop 6's written mask rule. Loop 12's design audit proposed it as a rerun under 0.1 GPU-hours; board question 97 decided it would not run as a rerun, and Loop 12 would instead read it from its own saved CO3 records at no GPU cost if they hold each animal's per-cell push. Ideas D1 (seed ranking by path length), D2 (judging renders by the model's own error for a right against a swapped caption), D4 and D6 passed to Loop 6 untested.
- **Evidence:** [the X9 verdict](../../score-space/09-verdict.md), [the independent audit](../../score-space/12-independent-audit.md), [the alignment check](../../score-space/13-alignment-check.md), [the ideas page](../../score-space/03-ideas.md), [Loop 12's design audit](../../leakage/05-design-audit.md) (board steps X6, X7, X8 and X9; questions 76, 89 and 97).

### A noise-aware model trained here, read on digits it never saw (W4)

- **Asked:** Does a sentence score learned on some concepts carry to concepts outside its training set, when an image encoder is trained on noisy inputs against a frozen CLIP text encoder (NoisyCLIP's recipe)?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** A small image encoder trained on 20,000 noisy 32 by 64 digit canvases holding only digits 0 to 4 (data hash `f70b0fb3…`), 8,000 updates at batch 256 in 158 seconds of training, then read on 1,000 seen canvases and once on 2,000 canvases of digits 5 to 9. Captions were written as words ("the digit three and the digit seven, apart"). Jobs 94208 and 94211.
- **Bar, written first:** support when, on unseen digits over the toy's steps 14 to 25 (the window matched to SDXL), balanced accuracy is at least 0.75, within 0.05 of seen, and at least 0.75 on two digits apart against wrong digits; null when the overfit test passes and any of the three misses. Constants in `w4_train.py`.
- **Result:** Seen 0.758, unseen 0.590 (0.168 below); digits apart against wrong digits 0.637 seen and 0.502 unseen; steps 0 to 15, read second, 0.536 seen and 0.504 unseen. By step from 14 to 25, seen rises 0.64, 0.67, 0.72, 0.77, 0.83, 0.88, 0.89 and unseen 0.52, 0.55, 0.59, 0.58, 0.67, 0.64, 0.63. The overfit test placed 16 of 16 (15 needed); the 1% run read 0.660.
- **Verdict:** null.
- **Why it failed:** The miss is on unseen digits read at the same noise as the seen ones (verified). The image encoder never saw digits 5 to 9 and nothing in training ties their shapes to CLIP's words for them, so the frozen text encoder gives the image side no route to new concepts (inferred). **The records disagree on how far this reaches:** the review concludes an SDXL version (about 70 GPU-hours, estimate) would read only the pairs it trained on and does not recommend it; Loop 12's design audit (row 22) says digits are the harder transfer, because SDXL's subject words were learned by CLIP beside pictures, so that extension is possibly wrong (inferred).
- **Kind of failure:** **The idea**, on digits: the test was fair there, because the overfit test passed (16 of 16), the encoder learned the seen digits (0.758) and the unseen digits were read once at the same noise (verified). **Wrong data**, possibly, for what it says about SDXL: digit names are words CLIP never learned beside these shapes, a harder transfer than SDXL's subject words (inferred, the design audit).
- **Idea still alive?** Closed on digits. Unclear for SDXL subjects: a cheap transfer test on words CLIP learned beside pictures would tell whether the digit failure carries over, before any SDXL-scale retrain.
- **Rules out:** Do not expect a noise-aware score trained on some digits to read digits outside its captions through frozen CLIP words alone. Do not spend an SDXL-scale retrain on the hope of new-pair transfer without a cheaper test on words CLIP learned beside pictures (inferred from the audit).
- **Leaves open:** transfer on natural-image subjects; an SDXL version.
- **Evidence:** [the W4 review](../../noise-text-critic/04-train-our-own/review.md), [its design](../../noise-text-critic/04-train-our-own/design.md), [the final numbers](../../noise-text-critic/04-train-our-own/final.json), the encoder at `/datasets/mmolefe/poe_repair_min/loop-9/w4/full/encoder.pt`, W&B `en52x031` (board step W4).

### The sentence-score push on CO3 (W3)

- **Asked:** Does adding the gradient of a right-minus-wrong sentence score to CO3 raise faithful pairs on the 12 tuning pairs, beside CO3 alone and a random push of the same length?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** Nothing was rendered. The design and `w3_steer.py` hold the push at a fixed length of 0.4 of CO3's guided prediction, a same-length random push and a soft clamp.
- **Bar, written first:** support when the push leads CO3 alone by at least 5 of 96 faithful pairs and by more than 1.96 standard errors (10,000 bootstrap resamples of the 12 pairs), and the random push does not; null when it has no such lead; inconclusive when the zero-push check fails or the random push also leads.
- **Result:** none; 0 of the 4.5 budgeted GPU-hours spent.
- **Verdict:** not run, stopped by the owner on questions 70 and 77.
- **Why it failed:** No step from 0 to 15 was readable by any sentence score (verified, W2 and W2c). W2's written gate, a threshold-half balanced accuracy of at least 0.6, would still have let Latent CLIP's counting push on 7 steps whose reading-half values average 0.56 (verified).
- **Kind of failure:** none of its own, since it never ran. It inherits **the idea** failure of its reader: no sentence score read any step it would push on (W2 and W2c).
- **Idea still alive?** Closed for a sentence-score push. A push on CO3 with a reader that does read early is a different attempt: Loop 6's first baseline test (M1) pushed with the readout checker and made 7 faithful pairs of 96 against CO3 alone's 11, recorded in [the method-search group](method-search.md).
- **Rules out:** Do not push with a score whose steps passed a gate on one half only. Anyone reusing W3's code inherits the design audit's issue (row 20): a fixed length with nothing to tell it the target is reached, and no re-noising or rescaling.
- **Leaves open:** whether a reader that does read steps 0 to 15, such as the readout head on SDXL's features, steers CO3 through this code unchanged.
- **Evidence:** [the W3 review](../../noise-text-critic/03-does-it-steer/review.md), [its design](../../noise-text-critic/03-does-it-steer/design.md) (board step W3; questions 70 and 77).

### Sentence scores on binding, CO3's own failure (W2d)

- **Asked:** Can a sentence score read binding (which parts, or which colour, belong to which subject) on the failure CO3 actually makes?
- **Paper:** both.
- **Tried:** Two labellers called CO3's 96 tuning pictures blind as own parts, borrowed or not two separate subjects. For colour, whole-animal regions from a detector replaced W2's word-map regions, and 57 of 62 cat and dog pictures were recoloured red and blue (5 left out because the detector found 1 or 3 animals). Jobs 94139 and 94209.
- **Bar, written first:** labels hold when the labellers agree on at least 80% of pictures both call own parts or borrowed; at least 8 pictures of each label per half; colour copies pass a 90% eye check at full size before any score; then 0.75 on the finished picture before any early step counts, and a mean of 0.75 over steps 0 to 15.
- **Result:** Part labels: agreement on 69 of 73 (94.5%) and 91 of 96 calls overall; final counts 40 own parts, 33 borrowed, 23 not two. Only bobcat × quail, seal × gull and puma × condor hold both labels, with 7 own-parts and 15 borrowed pictures, under the minimum. Colour: 26 of 32 copies passed (81%), against 90%; the 6 failures came from 3 pictures, each failing in both copies.
- **Verdict:** not run: binding left untested by the owner's choice of 2026-11-24 to wrap up the loop; the part labels are kept. **The records disagree on what CO3 draws:** Loop 6's first baseline test (M1) says CO3 draws two separate bodies in 96 of 96 tuning pictures, while these labels call 23 of the 96 "not two" (for example a boar with two pheasants). The manager notes that if these labels hold, about a third of CO3's failures are extra subjects, which no borrowed-parts fix can repair, and leaves it to the owner. The reviewer also records that M1's two-clear count is 32 of 96 and these labels' own-parts count is 40, on two rubrics. Loop 12's design audit (row 19) marks this step as one that could get a verdict: labelling M1's 192 pushed and random-push pictures would reach the minimum.
- **Why it failed:** Binding failure is a property of the pair (verified from the labels): cheetah × cougar and duck × goose borrow parts in all 7 of their two-subject pictures, and eagle × fox is clean in 8 of 8, so few pairs hold both labels. The colour masks missed tails, dark back fur and a white leg, about an eighth or a sixth of an animal (verified by eye). The image side was judged unlikely to read binding after it missed counting even on finished pictures (inferred).
- **Kind of failure:** **Could not measure it**: the colour labels failed their eye check at 26 of 32 against 90% (verified). **Test design**: CO3's 96 tuning pictures hold only 7 own-parts and 15 borrowed pictures in the pairs that carry both labels, under the minimum of 8 per label per half (verified). No sentence score was ever scored on binding, so nothing here bears on the idea.
- **Idea still alive?** Retry worth it in principle: label M1's 192 pushed and random-push pictures by eye to reach the minimum, repair the colour masks, then score (about 0.3 GPU-hours, estimate, plus 192 eye labels). The loop and Loop 12's design audit both judge a pass unlikely, because the same scores missed counting even on finished pictures (inferred).
- **Rules out:** Do not run a within-pair binding test on CO3's 96 tuning pictures alone: too few pairs hold both labels. Do not treat CO3's borrowed parts as seed-level noise; they are fixed per pair. Do not score recoloured pictures before a full-size eye check of the masks.
- **Leaves open:** binding read by any reader; whether CO3's failures are extra subjects or borrowed parts.
- **Evidence:** [the W2d review](../../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/review.md), [its design](../../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/design.md), [the part-binding calls](../../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/part-binding-calls.csv), [the colour eye check](../../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/colour-eye-check.csv), [the manager's table of things that may be wrong](../../manager/status.md#things-that-may-be-wrong), [the reviewer's records that disagree](../2026-11-24-review.md#records-that-disagree-reported-not-resolved) (board step W2d).

### A linear probe on frozen image vectors, read on unseen pairs (W2b)

- **Asked:** Does the smallest trained reader, one linear layer per step on a frozen sentence model's image vectors, read counting and prompt match on animal and object pairs it never saw?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** Logistic regressions per model, property and step from 0 to 15 on NoisyCLIP, frozen CLIP and Latent CLIP vectors, fitted on 3,268 pictures from 119 training pairs, read on 349 held-back seen pictures and once on 422 pictures from 7 unseen pairs, with one shared noise draw; a probe on 768 fixed random numbers per picture as the control. Job 94220, 13 minutes.
- **Bar, written first:** support when unseen balanced accuracy averaged over steps 0 to 15 is at least 0.75 and within 0.05 of seen; inconclusive when an unseen class holds fewer than 30 pictures or the seen score is under 0.55 at every step; the random control must stay at or under 0.6. Constants in `w2b_probe.py`.
- **Result:** Counting seen and unseen, prompt match seen and unseen: NoisyCLIP 0.637, 0.560, 0.644, 0.586; frozen CLIP 0.579, 0.526, 0.614, 0.552; Latent CLIP 0.575, 0.527, 0.579, 0.527; random numbers 0.502, 0.506, 0.505, 0.497.
- **Verdict:** null for all three encoders.
- **Why it failed:** The probe found real but weak early information (it beats the random control), and every encoder falls by 0.05 to 0.08 from seen to unseen pairs (verified). The early information sits mostly in the diffusion model's own features rather than in a CLIP-style image vector (inferred from the readout checker's 0.87, a mixed comparison).
- **Kind of failure:** **The idea**: the test was fair, with every unseen class above its minimum of 30 pictures, the random control at chance (0.497 to 0.506) and real information found on seen pairs (verified).
- **Idea still alive?** Closed for a linear head on frozen CLIP-style image vectors at steps 0 to 15.
- **Rules out:** Do not train a linear head on frozen CLIP-style image vectors to read early SDXL latents.
- **Leaves open:** spatial relation, position and size, and binding, which have no unseen-pair labels; nonlinear heads on these vectors.
- **Evidence:** [the W2b review](../../noise-text-critic/02b-linear-probe/review.md), [its design](../../noise-text-critic/02b-linear-probe/design.md), [the result file](../../noise-text-critic/02b-linear-probe/result.json); no W&B run id is recorded in the review (board step W2b).

### The sentence-score loop's verdict (W5)

- **Asked:** Should a sentence score be the push's reader in either paper, or the start of a third paper?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** A verdict over W2 to W4, one row per property, using about 3 of 8.7 approved GPU-hours.
- **Bar, written first:** none of its own; it rests on W2's 0.75.
- **Result:** Best sentence score over steps 0 to 15 per property: counting 0.561 (Latent CLIP); prompt match 0.526 (NoisyCLIP ensembled); left-right order 0.507; position and size 0.538 with 1 right picture per half; binding not scored. On the finished picture, counting reaches 0.71 at best with W2's wording and 0.750 with species-free ensembled wording; prompt match 0.82.
- **Verdict:** closed: drop sentence scores as the push's reader for both papers, keep them as a tested baseline that fails early.
- **Why it failed:** No sentence score reads any property at steps 0 to 15, while a small reader on SDXL's own features reads 0.87 by step 15 on real states (verified). **The records disagree on whose fault it is:** the alignment check, in the loop's own words, says "the idea, not the setup"; the independent audit says binding and a mid-run identity reader on CO3 after step 10 were never scored, so that part of the idea is untested. Loop 12's design audit finds no issue threatening the verdict itself.

- **Evidence:** [the W5 verdict](../../noise-text-critic/05-verdict.md), [the loop's one-page summary](../../noise-text-critic/00-executive-summary.md), [the alignment check](../../score-space/13-alignment-check.md), [the independent audit, closure (c)](../../score-space/12-independent-audit.md#c-noisyclip-or-latent-clip-to-push-sdxls-early-steps) (board step W5).

### The readout checker choosing CO3's weight sharpness at each corrected step (designed by the score-path loop)

- **Asked:** Does the readout checker lift CO3 when it chooses, with no gradient, among four values of CO3's weight sharpness $\beta$ (0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.1) at each of CO3's 6 corrected steps, against a random choice and one choice per picture?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** Designed only, on M1's 12 tuning pairs at seeds 6001 to 6008, 96 pictures per row, scored blind by eye on M1's rubric.
- **Bar, written first:** M1's, on faithful pairs of 96: support when the checker's choice leads CO3 alone by at least 5 of 96 and by more than 1.96 standard errors, and leads the random choice by the same margin; null when it does not lead CO3 alone.
- **Result:** none. Estimated cost about 9.6 GPU-hours, or about 3.1 for a first read on 6 of the 12 pairs.
- **Verdict:** not run, declined by the owner on question 80.
- **Why it failed:** Loop 6's full read showed the checker does not see CO3's failure: its yes-or-no call is right at best 60% of the time against a 65% floor (verified there). Loop 12's design audit (row 30) adds that the choice acts at CO3's corrected steps 0 to 5, where the latent holds 0.6% to 1.7% of its variance as picture, and that $\beta$ is set jointly for both subjects (inferred). The independent audit flags the once-per-picture row as a best-of-4, allowed only as a baseline, and the stepwise row as a gray zone for the owner. **The records disagree on what the file says:** the independent audit quotes lines naming "preference pairs an LPO-style LoRA could train on" and an outcome where choosing once is enough, while the file now holds 45 lines and says the once-per-picture row "can never be the method"; the design audit infers it was rewritten after the audit.
- **Kind of failure:** none of its own, since it never ran. What stopped it is **could not measure it**: the only checker available cannot tell CO3's good pictures from its borrowed ones (verified in Loop 6's read). The design also carries a **test design** risk: it chooses at steps where the latent is almost all noise (inferred).
- **Idea still alive?** Unclear: a checker first shown to separate CO3's good from borrowed pictures at the steps where it would choose, then this test as designed, would tell whether choosing helps.
- **Rules out:** Do not run a checker-chooses-the-setting test with a checker not first shown to tell CO3's good pictures from its borrowed ones at the steps where it would choose. Do not let a best-of-N row win as the method.
- **Leaves open:** the same test with a checker that does see CO3's failure; the design stays on file to be reopened then.
- **Evidence:** [the design](../../score-space/11-the-checker-chooses-on-co3.md), [the independent audit, question 3](../../score-space/12-independent-audit.md#question-3-recommendations-that-conflict-with-the-owners-intent), [Loop 12's design audit](../../leakage/05-design-audit.md) (board question 80).

## Open ideas from the gap-reading and directions loops (Loops 14 and 15)

The loop that read the field for gaps (Loop 14, folder `gaps/`, steps G1 to G4) and the loop on directions and dials (Loop 15, folder `directions/`, steps H1 to H4) recorded no test result of their own: every project number on their pages is copied from another loop's review and belongs to that loop's group. Loop 15's three ranked experiments ran elsewhere and are recorded there: whether the joint prompt is the wrong target (Loop 6, M14), a score that names no subject (Loop 6, M15) and the co-occurrence dial on new pairs (Loop 12, Z10). What their pages leave open:

- **Narrow Paper 2 to look-alike subject pairs**, where plain SD3 draws both look-alike subjects in about 53% of images and the best frozen fix about 69% ([the gap-reading recommendation](../../gaps/01-is-paper-2-still-open.md#recommendation)); which model Paper 2 targets waits on board question 104.
- **Ranked open problems** ([the open-problems page](../../gaps/03-open-problems.md)): borrowed parts and extra subjects on look-alike pairs; composing finished experts on combinations none saw; what a product loses where objects interact (shadows, occlusion); composing below DCFM's minimum share of seen combinations; chest X-rays where one finding changes how another looks.
- **Five DCFM experiments, cheapest first** ([the DCFM page](../../gaps/04-exploiting-dcfm.md#the-experiments-cheapest-first)), from two-holder Colored MNIST to an X-ray smoke run; and 19 reviewer questions for Paper 1, led by "is it better than pasting" ([the reviewer-questions page](../../gaps/02-reviewer-questions-paper-1.md)).
- **A digit-only and colour-only push at the checker push's length** on the Colored MNIST product, to tell an interaction from stronger attributes ([the limits page](../../directions/04-limits-and-what-to-test.md#colored-mnist-does-the-checker-push-supply-an-interaction)).
- **A count dial by scaling a noun's attention ("dog times 2")**, untested in every paper read; whether M13's whole-latent cosine hides agreement on the bodies; whether the low-rank structure that reaches unseen cells on the toy holds on SDXL's pairs ([the limits page](../../directions/04-limits-and-what-to-test.md#still-open)).
- **Binding strength has no agreed measure**, so no binding dial can be tested yet, and no paper sweeps a strength against "two subjects together" scoring hybrid and two separately ([the dials page](../../directions/03-dials.md#still-open)).
- **Steering along a scene-relation probe, and robust-classifier guidance outside its label set**, both untested in the literature read ([the directions page](../../directions/01-directions-that-generalise.md#still-open)); whether a weighted score correction carries across samplers ([the shared-representations page](../../directions/02-shared-representations.md#still-open)).
