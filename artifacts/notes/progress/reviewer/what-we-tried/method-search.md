# What we tried: the method search for Paper 1 and Paper 2

This group covers Loop 6's search for one method per paper: steps M1 to M15 on [the progress board](../../board.json), plus the checker steps that closed with them (choosing the checker's architecture, D3, and the short inclusive fine-tune with the push, D5). Paper 1 composes two diffusion models trained on separate data (digits, then CLEVR scenes) and must beat sampling each model alone and pasting the results. Paper 2 adds a correction to CO3 inside SDXL and must beat CO3 alone. The group holds 13 entries: 10 attempts that ran and 3 that closed before running, one of which covers four steps closed by the same decision. Tuning the winning method (M3) and freezing one recipe per paper (M4) are not here, because neither has finished: both wait on the owner's decision on the papers' shape. Entries follow the order in which each step's deciding runs finished; the two-colour CLEVR test started rendering early but finished last. Three terms recur. A **two clear** picture holds one clear subject A and one clear subject B as separate bodies with no borrowed parts, drawing style ignored, called blind at full size under the [blind count rubric](../../method-search/01-beat-the-baseline/blind-count-rubric.md). **Balanced accuracy** is the mean of a reader's hit rates on the two classes, where 0.5 is chance and 1.0 is perfect. **The dropped term, r_t,** is the joint prompt's noise prediction minus the plain product's (first subject plus second subject minus the empty prompt): the part of the joint prompt that composition leaves out.

### Beating each paper's simplest baseline

- **Asked:** Does each paper's method draw more correct pairs than the cheapest thing a reviewer would try first?
- **Paper:** both.
- **Tried:** Paper 1: two MNIST digit models (one draws 0 to 4, one draws 5 to 9) choose a half of the 32 by 64 pixel canvas over the first 10 of 50 steps and hold it, and a checker trained only on the models' own samples pushes the sampler; four rows of 320 canvases (5 tuning digit pairs at seeds 0 to 63): sample each alone and paste, the method with no push, with the checker push, and with a random push of the same size. Paper 2: CO3 at its released settings, alone, with the kept checker's push (strength 30 on steps 0 to 15, capped at 0.4 of CO3's guided prediction), and with a random push of the same size, on 12 tuning animal pairs at seeds 6001 to 6008: 96 SDXL pictures per row, 288 called blind by eye.
- **Bar, written first:** Paper 1: support if the push row beats paste by more than 1.96 standard errors of the matched difference (10,000 bootstrap resamples within each pair). Paper 2: support if the push row leads CO3 alone by at least 5 faithful pairs of 96 and by more than 1.96 standard errors, where a faithful pair also needed an intact photograph. Both: inconclusive if a zero push fails to reproduce the unpushed run within 1e-5.
- **Result:** Paper 1: 295 clean digit pairs of 320 with the push against 287 for paste, a lead of 8 under the bar of 13.54 (1.96 times a standard error of 6.91). Read after the run, with no bar of their own: the push beats no push by 36 of 320 (margin 15.61) and a random push by 37 (margin 15.47), a random push is 1 below no push, and the method without a push is 28 below paste (margin 16.57). The digit judge agreed with a blind eye on 58 of 64 canvases. Paper 2: 7 faithful pairs of 96 with the push, 11 for CO3 alone, 12 with the random push: a lead of −4 against a bar of 10.08 (1.96 times a standard error of 5.14). On two clear, style ignored: 32 of 96 alone, 30 with the push, 35 with the random push (the push 2 below, margin 11.25). Both zero-push checks gave a largest difference of 0.0.
- **Verdict:** null for both papers.
- **Why it failed:** Paper 1, verified: the checker's direction does real work (36 pairs where a random push of the same size changes nothing), but that lift only brings the method level with paste, and without it the method is 28 below paste. Paper 2, inferred when it ran and measured later: the checker's two presence outputs trained only on cat and dog pictures, so on the 12 tuning pairs they had never seen the subjects asked for (stated in the design); the next step measured that the kept checker cannot tell CO3's two clear pictures from the rest at any step (best balanced accuracy 0.602 against a 0.65 floor). Verified: drawing style drove the faithful count, since 73 of CO3's 96 pictures fail the intact-photograph mark and reviewers noted a drawn style on 53, 41 and 53 of the 96 in the three rows. The six reviewers passed 7, 0, 6, 4, 4 and 9 of their 48 pictures each, a spread the size of the bar.
- **Kind of failure:** Paper 1, **test design**: on digits with fixed halves paste is already near its ceiling (287 of 320, leaving 33 canvases of room), so a tie there says little about composing where paste cannot work. Paper 2, **wrong data**: the presence outputs behind the push never trained on the 12 subjects asked for (stated in the design). **Could not measure it**: the faithful-pair bar required a photograph, and 73 of CO3's 96 pictures fail that mark, mostly on drawing style. **Not understood**: the checker was built to tell two bodies from one, while CO3 already draws two bodies in all 96, so its failure is identity (verified by the next step's read, best 0.602 against a 0.65 floor).
- **Idea still alive?** Paper 1: retry worth it, on a test where paste fails (the CLEVR tests below took this up). Paper 2: unclear from this attempt alone, because the checker never saw these subjects; what would tell them apart is a checker that first separates CO3's two-clear pictures from its failures, which the later reads did not find, and the owner closed the route (question 111).
- **Rules out:** Do not judge a Paper 2 correction on faithful pairs that require a photograph when the prompts do not ask for one; judge on two clear, style ignored, with damage reported beside it (the owner's yes on board question 87). Do not read a Paper 1 method's lead over its own unpushed version as a win; compare it with paste. Do not split pictures among single reviewers for a 5-of-96 bar; use two reviewers per picture and a third for their disagreements.
- **Leaves open:** Whether a checker trained on the subjects asked for would lift CO3; anything on held-out pairs or seeds, since only tuning data was used. Records disagree, not resolved: this review says CO3 drew two separate bodies in all 96 tuning pictures (0 single or fused), while Loop 9's part labels call 23 of the same 96 "not two", for example a boar with two pheasants (the [reviewer's pass](../2026-11-24-review.md#choosing-paper-2s-identity-reader-2026-11-25-0250) and [the manager's list of things that may be wrong](../../manager/status.md#things-that-may-be-wrong)).

### Pasting small CLEVR samples before any tuning on CLEVR

- **Asked:** Does sampling each small CLEVR model alone and pasting its object into place already succeed, so that counting objects could not tell Paper 1's method from paste?
- **Paper:** 1 (the board lists it as serving both).
- **Tried:** Four EDM2 `edm2-img64-xs` models at 64 pixels, each trained once for 2,000,896 images seen on 5,000 renders: one model per fixed position, each holding one object, and one on empty scenes. An 18 by 18 pixel window around each asked position (18 by 24 at position 3) was cut from that expert's sample and laid on a background sample: 1,024 pasted images with 2 experts and 1,024 with 3, read by a small per-position classifier, and 32 per count by eye.
- **Bar, written first:** The paste rate (the mean in-place rate of the three objects on the 3-expert pastes) at or above 0.906 predicts 7 objects under Bradley's rule, since 0.906 to the 7th power is about 0.50, and stops this step; below 0.906 the small CLEVR test (M6) runs; inconclusive if an expert alone misses 90%, the classifier misses 99%, or the window control fails.
- **Result:** Every object in place in 1,024 of 1,024 images with 2 experts and with 3: a paste rate of 1.0 against 0.906, and no object where none was asked. The eye agreed with the classifier on 64 of 64. Each expert alone placed its object in 256 of 256 samples, the empty-scene model drew no object in 256 of 256, and the classifier scored 100% per position on 400 held-out renders. The window control as first written missed its 95% mark at all three positions (94.4%, 93.9% and 91.7% of changed pixels inside the window); the owner changed it on board question 66, before any paste existed, to count each object's own footprint with a taller window at position 3, which held 100%, 100% and 99.8%. About 8.5 GPU-hours against 9 to 12 approved.
- **Verdict:** closed: paste is predicted to reach 7 objects, so the step stopped.
- **Why it failed:** Verified: each expert places its object alone at 256 of 256 and the windows do not overlap at these positions, so a paste needs nothing composed. Inferred in the design before the run: an expert must pass 90% to count and the stop line is 90.6%, so any expert that passes would usually trigger the stop.
- **Kind of failure:** **Test design**: objects at fixed, separated positions let paste win by construction, and the design itself predicted that any expert passing its 90% check would usually trigger the 90.6% stop. The test did what it was built to do; it failed only as a place to measure Paper 1.
- **Idea still alive?** Retry worth it: Paper 1's method on CLEVR was not tested here; the fix is a scene where paste fails, with objects that overlap or shade each other, which the two-colour test tried.
- **Rules out:** Do not test Paper 1 on objects at fixed, separated positions, where paste wins by construction. Do not set an expert-alone bar that by itself nearly guarantees the stop rule. For short EDM2 runs, keep the learning-rate ramp as a fraction of training (0.93%) and train at batch 256: verified on the overfit rung, the fixed 10-million-image ramp left samples as colour noise, and batch 2,048 made only 125 weight updates in 256,000 images, with the classifier finding the object in 0 of 16 samples.
- **Leaves open:** Crowded scenes where objects overlap or shade each other, which became the two-colour test below. The faintly lighter square many pasted objects sit on, where the expert's floor meets the background's, is not measured. The [reviewer's pass](../2026-11-24-review.md#loop-6-find-the-best-method) records the window control's change after it failed as the one disclosed bar change in Loop 6, made before any paste was scored.
- **Evidence:** [the review](../../method-search/01c-clevr-paste-first/review.md), [the 3-expert pastes](../../method-search/01c-clevr-paste-first/paste-three-experts-first-32.png), [each expert alone](../../method-search/01c-clevr-paste-first/experts-alone-first-16-samples.png), W&B `zv6oef8h` (the position 1 expert) and `guf6vtho` (the classifier) (board step M7).

### Giving each subject its own region of the picture

- **Asked:** Does letting each subject's noise prediction act only inside its own region fix CO3's identity failures (borrowed parts, the wrong species, an extra subject)?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** First rung, on "a cat and a dog" at 8 seeds with SDXL: each subject's prediction weighted by a soft mask around boxes detected on the joint prompt's own successful render of the same seed (boxes no deployed method would have), applied from step 0 of 50, against random boxes of the same sizes, swapped prompts and the same prompt in both boxes; then again with bootstrapping, where each region is denoised alone over a constant colour for the first 15 of 50 steps. 48 and then 40 pictures were called blind by two reviewers. Second rung: CO3 reads its own regions from each subject word's cross-attention at step 10; two box readers were checked on 24 reads each before any picture was drawn.
- **Bar, written first:** First rung (2026-11-04): right boxes at least 4 clear pairs of 8 to continue; 0 or 1 is a null held until bootstrapping runs; "regions without names" if right boxes reach 4 and swapped or same-prompt boxes are within 1. Second rung's mask check: stop if more than 25% of reads fail, a read failing when a box covers at least 80% of the grid or the two boxes overlap at an intersection over union of 0.8 or more. Second rung's comparison: its own masks lead CO3 alone by at least 5 faithful pairs of 96 and by more than 1.96 standard errors.
- **Result:** Without bootstrapping, right boxes drew 0 clear pairs of 8 (6 fused cat-dogs and 2 single animals), every region row drew 0, and the joint prompt drew 7. With bootstrapping, right boxes drew 8 of 8 clear pairs, random boxes 4, swapped prompts 7, and the same prompt twice 0 clear but two animals on all 8 (two cats); both reviewers passed 0 of the 8 right-box pictures as undamaged, against 5 of 8 for the joint prompt. Second rung: the first reader failed 11 of 24 reads (7 on overlap, 4 on box size; median overlap 0.53), and the second, on fresh seeds, failed 10 of 24 (8 of them gave one word no cell at all), against a limit of 6. No second-rung picture was drawn. About 3.75 GPU-hours.
- **Verdict:** closed under board question 83, decided by Loop 13 for the owner. The first rung's written verdict with bootstrapping is "regions without names"; the second rung stopped at its mask check.
- **Why it failed:** Verified: fusing by region from step 0 fused the animals even with boxes from a render that had succeeded (0 of 8); bootstrapping separated them only by cutting each region off, leaving cut-outs on flat grey (0 of 8 undamaged); at step 10 one word's attention covers the other subject's body on 4 of the 12 tuning pairs, so no comparison of the two maps can give each subject its own cells. Inferred in [the method review](../../method-search/04-bind-each-body/method-review.md): every try separated the subjects with a signal that had not yet separated them, and once regions were imposed nothing blended them back; masking only the noise predictions leaves self-attention free to mix the bodies (Bounded Attention, found by search and not read); CO3 re-noises with the empty prompt's prediction and rescales the latent after each edit, which this composer did not, and that may explain the damage (untested).
- **Kind of failure:** **Done wrong**: from step 0 the masks weighted two predictions that each describe a whole picture, so they blended (0 of 8); the constant-colour bootstrap separated the animals only by cutting them off, leaving damage on 8 of 8; self-attention was left unbounded and CO3's renoise and rescale were skipped (both inferred causes). **Not understood**: the second rung assumed word attention at step 10 already separates the subjects, while one word covers both bodies on 4 of 12 pairs (verified). **Test design**: the first rung covered one pair with boxes from renders that had already succeeded, and the comparison with CO3 was never made.
- **Idea still alive?** Retry worth it: regions were never tested on CO3, and bootstrapped regions did separate the animals on 8 of 8, so the idea was not fairly tested. The fix is named in the method review: read regions after step 16 or from CO3's finished picture, bound self-attention between regions, blend in late steps, and count only undamaged two-clear pictures. The [reviewer's pass](../2026-11-24-review.md#the-aligned-classifier-on-co3-2026-11-25) records Loop 12's own-prediction-in-its-box test, the nearest later attempt, at 14 two-clear of 48 against CO3's 8 (p = 0.073, short of its bar); it sits in the leakage group.
- **Rules out:** Do not read subject regions from word cross-attention at step 10 or earlier. Do not use constant-colour bootstrapping as the move that separates the subjects. Do not count a damaged clear pair as a pass; count undamaged pictures beside clear pairs. Do not judge a new region reader on seeds an earlier reader was already judged on.
- **Leaves open:** Whether regions read after step 16 or from CO3's finished picture, with self-attention bounded between regions and a blending stage at the end, would fix identity; the second rung's comparison with CO3 alone was never made. The review, the method review and the [reviewer's pass](../2026-11-24-review.md#loop-6-find-the-best-method) agree on closing it.
- **Evidence:** [the review](../../method-search/04-bind-each-body/review.md), [the method review](../../method-search/04-bind-each-body/method-review.md), [the bootstrapped cat and dog sheet](../../method-search/04-bind-each-body/rung-1-bootstrapped-five-rows-eight-seeds.png), [the step-10 word attention with its boxes](../../method-search/04-bind-each-body/rung-2-step-10-word-attention-boxes.png); no W&B run recorded (board step M9).

### Balancing the checker's training data, and whether any checker sees CO3's failure

- **Asked:** Does Paper 2's checker learn what a correct pair looks like better from a list balanced across pairs than from the current list, where 60% of clean draws are cat and dog; and before that, can any of these checkers tell CO3's good pictures from its failures?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** Two clean-pair checkers on SDXL's frozen features, trained 2,000 updates each: the current list (2,464 pictures, 1,603 of them cat and dog, 158 pairs) and the balanced list (877 pictures, 16 cat and dog, 158 pairs). Before the planned comparison renders, a read added on the reviewer's first fix (board questions 85 and 93): CO3 rerun on the 12 tuning pairs at seeds 6001 to 6008, reproducing the 96 trajectories behind the first test's pictures, and the kept checker and both new checkers scored the state at steps 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 against the blind calls (32 two clear, 64 not).
- **Bar, written first:** The read (2026-11-24): balanced accuracy of the sign of the clean-pair score at least 0.75 at some step means the checker sees the failure; under 0.65 at every step means it does not, and pausing for a checker that reads identity goes to the owner; between, or fewer than 80 trajectories reproducing, is inconclusive. The comparison, never reached: the balanced list wins if its CO3 row leads the current list's by at least 5 two-clear pictures of 96 and by more than 1.96 standard errors, and a tie also sends the balanced list on.
- **Result:** All 96 trajectories reproduce once the 21 first drawn on a Quadro RTX 8000 were rerun on that card type. Best balanced accuracy: kept checker 0.602 (step 5), current list 0.617 (step 5), balanced list 0.648 (step 10), all under the 0.65 floor. Area under the curve (how often a two-clear picture outscores a failed one, 0.5 is chance) near 0.7 from step 5 for all three, the kept checker's best 0.712 at step 10. On their own held-out training tiles at update 2,000: current list 0.571, balanced list 0.504. The first read, on the 75 trajectories that reproduced on an RTX 3090, peaked at 0.616 and was inconclusive by its count.
- **Verdict:** closed after its read, under the owner's answer to board question 100: no checker sees CO3's failure, so the comparison renders did not run and no list went forward.
- **Why it failed:** Verified: all three checkers rank a two-clear picture above a borrowed-part picture about 7 times in 10, but none splits them at its zero point, so the push, which follows the score toward "clean pair" on every picture, has no reason to move a borrowed-part picture more than a good one; the balanced checker was also at chance on its own held-out tiles. Inferred by the [reviewer](../2026-11-24-review.md#loop-6-find-the-best-method): the checker was built to tell two bodies from one, while CO3 already draws two bodies, so its remaining failure is identity, which these labels do not teach.
- **Kind of failure:** **The idea**, for a clean-pair checker pushing CO3: three checkers, read on all 96 reproduced trajectories against a bar written first, none reaching the 0.65 floor, is a fair test. **Not understood**: the labels teach two bodies against one, while CO3's failure is identity (inferred by the reviewer). **Wrong data**: the balanced checker is at chance on its own held-out tiles (0.504), and the balanced list is also smaller (877 against 2,464), so size and balance are mixed. The list comparison itself never ran.
- **Idea still alive?** Closed for a clean-pair checker as Paper 2's correction on CO3. Balancing the list is untested and has no use until some checker sees CO3's failure.
- **Rules out:** Do not tune a checker's training data, architecture or labels before showing it can tell CO3's good pictures from its failures at the steps where it pushes. Do not swap the yes-or-no cut for the area under the curve after seeing the numbers (the manager's list). Do not compare CO3 runs drawn on different card types: the 21 trajectories first drawn on a Quadro RTX 8000 differed by 3.0 to 27.4 of 255 in mean pixel value when rerun on an RTX 3090.
- **Leaves open:** Whether the list size, not its balance, drives any difference, since the balanced list is also smaller; the reviewer's size-matched list (the current list cut to 877) never ran. The review's opening line still says the step "has no verdict yet" while its read section gives one, and the board marks it done and closed.

### Reading identity from CO3's states with no training, cell by cell

- **Asked:** Can a reader with no training see from CO3's own sampler states which animal it drew where?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** On the 96 saved tuning trajectories at steps 5 to 30, SDXL predicted the noise under the empty, joint and each animal's prompt; each of the latent's 128 by 128 cells went to the animal whose prediction is closer to the joint one, and a picture's score was the less-represented animal's share of cells (0 to 0.5). Beside it, as a diagnostic, a language-model judge read each step's decoded one-step clean estimate (576 pictures), after a positive control on the 96 finished pictures.
- **Bar, written first:** From the reviewer's pass (2026-11-25): call "two clear" when the score is at least 0.30; balanced accuracy of at least 0.75 at any step from 5 to 30 means the reader sees the failure; under 0.65 at every step with an area under the curve never above 0.714 means it stops; between is inconclusive and goes to the owner; an area under the curve of at least 0.75 alone returns to the owner as ranking only (question 96). The judge's reads count only if it reaches 0.75 on the finished pictures.
- **Result:** Best balanced accuracy 0.688 at step 20; best area under the curve 0.742 at step 25. The judge's control scored 0.914; on the clean estimates it read 0.664, 0.688, 0.773, 0.789, 0.852 and 0.867 at steps 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30. Over the 12 pairs, a pair's share of two-clear pictures and its mean score at step 20 have a rank correlation of 0.557: hippopotamus and swallow, 0 of 8 two clear, scores 0.081, and eagle and fox, 8 of 8, scores 0.394. The judge used 1,462,496 input and 61,732 output tokens.
- **Verdict:** inconclusive.
- **Why it failed:** Verified by the reviewer's computation: compared only between pictures of the same pair, the reader is at chance, with a within-pair area under the curve of 0.427, 0.512, 0.439, 0.476, 0.500 and 0.390 at steps 5 to 30 over 82 couples (0.5 is chance); a lookup that knows only which pair a picture belongs to scores 0.757 balanced accuracy on fresh seeds, above this reader; and on fresh seeds this reader fell to 0.632 (next entry). So its signal was the pair, not the picture. Verified: the share of cells reads low wherever one animal is much larger than the other, however clearly both are drawn.
- **Kind of failure:** **Could not measure it**: a share of cells measures how much of the picture each animal holds, so it reads relative size (hippopotamus and swallow 0.081 whatever is drawn). **Test design**: the bar scored the reader across pairs on the tuning seeds, where a lookup that knows only the pair passes; no within-pair score was in the bar.
- **Idea still alive?** Closed for the per-place share reader: scored within pair, which removes the pair effect and is a fair test of reading the picture, it is at chance (0.39 to 0.51). Reading identity from CO3's states at all stays open from step 15, where the judge on the decoded estimate reads 0.773.
- **Rules out:** Do not score an identity reader only on new seeds of the same pairs, since a pair lookup passes 0.75 there; score it within pair, on couples of pictures from the same pair, with pairs held out. Do not read a share of cells as identity; it measures relative size.
- **Leaves open:** The judge shows identity is present in the decoded state from step 15 (0.773 balanced accuracy, 0.744 within pair), but little at steps 5 and 10 (0.567 and 0.610 within pair, from the [reviewer's pass](../2026-11-24-review.md#the-last-identity-reader-2026-11-25)). Records disagree, not resolved: the board marks this step "closed, not run" and its proof line names Loop 8's identity checker and an attention-map reader, while the review records this per-place reader as run and inconclusive; and the push window after a pass is steps 5 to 15 in this design and steps 15 to 25 in the next reader's design.
- **Evidence:** [the review](../../method-search/07-a-checker-that-reads-identity/review.md); no W&B run recorded (board step M12).

### Correcting the per-place reader for each animal's size

- **Asked:** Does correcting each animal's share for its expected size, and failing any picture showing more than two animals, let the per-place reader see borrowing on seeds it has not been read on?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** 96 fresh CO3 trajectories (the 12 tuning pairs at seeds 6009 to 6016), their finished pictures called blind by two reviewers who agreed on 84 of 96, a third call settling the other 12: 41 two clear. Each animal's expected share came from boxes around it drawn alone by plain SDXL at seeds 8001 to 8004.
- **Bar, written first:** 2026-11-25: call "two clear" when the size-corrected score is at least 0.60; balanced accuracy of at least 0.75 at any step from 5 to 30 means it sees the failure; under 0.65 at every step with an area under the curve never above 0.742 means it stops; between is inconclusive.
- **Result:** Best balanced accuracy 0.635 at step 25 and best area under the curve 0.691; without the extra-animal rule the best was 0.623. The first reader, uncorrected, on the same fresh trajectories reached 0.632, against its 0.688 on the tuning trajectories. Box areas came out at 0.39 to 0.94 of the picture for every animal, and every animal pair's expected first share fell between 0.44 and 0.60 (hippopotamus and swallow 0.566, where something near 0.9 was needed); only lighthouse and sailboat moved, to 0.262. The extra-animal rule fired on 40 steps and lifted the best balanced accuracy by about 0.01 to 0.04.
- **Verdict:** null: the reader does not see the failure and stops.
- **Why it failed:** Verified: plain SDXL draws any single subject to fill the frame, so the expected shares could not see size, and the correction moved each pair's scores together while changing little between pictures. Verified: part of the first reader's 0.688 came from the tuning seeds it was first read on.
- **Kind of failure:** **Done wrong**: the expected shares came from single-subject renders that fill the frame (box areas 0.39 to 0.94), so the size correction had almost nothing to correct with (verified). **Test design**: still scored across pairs, with no within-pair score in the bar.
- **Idea still alive?** Closed: a size correction set per pair moves all of a pair's scores together (inferred), so it cannot change the within-pair ranking, which the uncorrected reader already showed at chance; the extra-animal rule added only 0.01 to 0.04.
- **Rules out:** Do not estimate a subject's expected size from single-subject renders by plain SDXL. Do not build another variant of the per-place share reader.
- **Leaves open:** Whether a reader trained with pairs held out would pass: the reviewer's choice, a 129-weight identity output on the kept feature head at about 0.5 GPU-hours, never ran, because the checker route closed (question 111).
- **Evidence:** [the review](../../method-search/08-size-corrected-identity-reader/review.md); no W&B run recorded (board step M12).

### Whether the kept checker's push points along the dropped term

- **Asked:** Does the kept checker's push move CO3's noise prediction along r_t, the part of the joint prompt's prediction that composition drops?
- **Paper:** 2.
- **Tried:** On 192 saved CO3 trajectories (seeds 6001 to 6016) and 48 plain-product trajectories (seeds 6001 to 6004), at steps 0, 5, 10 and 15: the cosine between the checker's gradient and r_t, beside a random direction. Then a sign check on the actual push code at 12 saved CO3 states.
- **Bar, written first:** 2026-11-25: per step, a median cosine of the push direction with r_t of at least +0.10 at some step means it points the right way and the identity head may run; a median under 0.02 in size at every step means it points elsewhere; anything between is inconclusive; a median of −0.10 or below counts as pointing against. The design defined the push direction as the checker's gradient.
- **Result:** The gradient's median cosine with r_t: CO3 −0.105, −0.043, −0.053 and −0.042 at steps 0, 5, 10 and 15; plain product −0.075, −0.089, −0.042 and −0.025; random direction 0.000 (spread 0.004 to 0.005). ### Whether the joint prompt is the wrong target where it fails

- **Asked:** Does the kept push follow the dropped term less where the joint prompt itself fails to draw both subjects, which would make the joint prompt the wrong target to copy?
- **Paper:** both.
- **Tried:** SDXL drew the joint prompt alone (guidance 7.5, 50 steps) from CO3's starting noise at the 192 seeds of the push-direction check, and two reviewers called the 192 pictures blind (agreeing on 168, a third call settling 24). The push's cosines with r_t from that check were split by the joint render's call.
- **Bar, written first:** 2026-11-25, with every threshold negated after the sign check and before any split: "the joint is the wrong target" if at some step the difference (fails minus succeeds) is at least +0.05 with its 95% interval above 0 and the succeeds median is below +0.02; "the checker is wrong" if at every step the interval includes 0 or the difference is below +0.02, and the succeeds median reaches +0.02 at some step; anything else inconclusive.
- **Result:** The joint prompt alone drew two clear in 34 of 192 pictures (18%), and CO3 in 73 on the same seeds; both two clear on 19, joint only on 15, CO3 only on 54, neither on 104. The push's median cosine with r_t where the joint succeeds against where it fails: +0.148 and +0.097 at step 0, a difference of −0.051 (interval −0.129 to −0.002); −0.008 at step 5 (−0.091 to +0.083), +0.006 at step 10 (−0.027 to +0.042), −0.015 at step 15 (−0.054 to +0.015). The clean-pair output alone at step 0: +0.122 against +0.046 (interval −0.125 to −0.019).
- **Verdict:** two readings, recorded side by side. The owner's reading, chosen after the split: the joint is the wrong target where it fails, at step 0 only (met by 0.001), inconclusive at steps 5 to 15. The rule as written, signs negated: the checker is wrong, since no step reaches +0.05.
- **Why it worked:** Verified: negating every threshold kept the rule deciding the same way on the gradient's data, which flipped the condition twice once the cosines were read in the push's direction, so the letter and the intent of the bar point opposite ways. Verified at step 0 only: the surprise the design named happened, the push following r_t more where the joint succeeds, so the push is a weak copy of the joint prompt, strongest where the joint already draws two clear subjects.
- **Kind of failure:** **Test design**: the sign was corrected by negating every threshold, which flipped the condition twice, so the bar's letter and intent point opposite ways and the reading was chosen after the split; step 0 is met by only 0.001, and only 34 trajectories sit in the joint-succeeds group. **Not understood**: the bar was written while the push's direction was taken to be the checker's gradient.
- **Idea still alive?** Unclear for the cosine split: what would tell the readings apart is a condition restated in words before any split and run on fresh seeds with more joint-success trajectories. The plain count stands on its own: the joint prompt alone is two clear on 34 of 192 against CO3's 73, so copying the joint prompt aims at a worse picture on these pairs.

Records disagree, not resolved: [the manager's list](../../manager/status.md#things-that-may-be-wrong) says the subject-free score step was closed on the "checker is wrong" verdict, while the owner's later choice of the intended reading reopened it and it ran.

### Two CLEVR experts trained on separate colours

- **Asked:** Can two diffusion models, one trained only on red CLEVR objects and one only on blue, each told how many to draw and not where, compose scenes neither saw, with x red and y blue objects all separate, as the count rises from 2 to 6?
- **Paper:** 1.
- **Tried:** Two EDM2 experts at 64 pixels, each trained for 2,000,896 images seen on 15,000 renders of 1 to 3 objects of its colour, plus an empty-scene model. Two count checkers were trained for that row: one on the experts' denoised maps, one on the noisy picture. Seeds 0 to 31 of every cell, 864 pictures, were called blind by two reviewers, and a count classifier scored every picture.
- **Bar, written first:** Each expert alone must hold exactly k objects of its colour in at least 90% of samples at each count. Support if the checker row leads the plain product, the corrector and mask paste each by at least 5 points of exact share averaged over the 9 cells and by more than 1.96 standard errors over cells; paste explains it if mask paste matches or beats the checker row; the corrector explains it if the corrector row is within 1.96 standard errors of the checker row; null with no lead over the plain product; inconclusive if an expert misses 90%, the count classifier misses 95% on real renders, or the eye and the classifier disagree on more than 1 picture in 10. The checker on the experts' maps reached 0.645 against 0.75 (0.82 on apart pastes with the right counts, 0.57 touching, 0.37 one count off); a diagnosis at four noise windows peaked at 0.741 on nearly clean pastes. The checker on the noisy picture reached 0.641. Each expert alone, by eye on 32 per count: red 32, 31 and 29 of 32 at 1, 2 and 3 objects, blue 32, 31 and 31, the two reviewers agreeing on all 192.
- **Verdict:** inconclusive by the bars written first, because the count classifier missed 95% and disagreed with the eye on more than 1 in 10. Under the owner's answer to board question 114 the rows are read on the eye calls; both checker rows are reported as not run.
- **Why it failed:** Verified: wherever both experts draw at the same place the colours add into a dark purple-black object that is neither red nor blue, in every plain and corrector picture called by eye, and the corrector moves the classifier's share by 0.004. Verified: mask paste fails by overlap, the blue object lying over the red one, and it breaks at 2 objects. Verified: the count classifier trained on real renders, where objects never overlap, so it reads a red sliver beside a blue object as one of each. For Paper 1's method row, **wrong data**: the checker's inputs (the experts' denoised maps, then the noisy picture) carry almost no count of small blocks, best 0.741 and 0.641 against 0.75. **Could not measure it**: the count classifier read a pasted red sliver beside a blue block as one of each and missed its 95% bar. Paper 1's method on CLEVR: unclear, because it was never measured; what would tell them apart is a checker that reaches 0.75 on held-out pastes from an input that carries the count, or another signal that keeps the two experts from drawing at the same place, judged by blind eye calls against mask paste on overlapping scenes.

- **Leaves open:** Paper 1's method has no measurement on CLEVR, since neither checker could learn to tell a right count from one off by one.
