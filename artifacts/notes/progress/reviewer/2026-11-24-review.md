## Loop 6: find the best method

### In plain words

Loop 6 looks for one recipe per paper: for Paper 1 it must beat sampling each model alone and pasting
the results, for Paper 2 it must beat CO3. Both first tests came out level: 295 clean digit pairs of 320
against paste's 287, and 7 faithful pictures of 96 against CO3's 11. The next Paper 2 steps choose the
checker's data, architecture and labels with the push fixed at steps 0 to 15 of 50, although the other
loops find identity readable only later, and nobody has checked whether this checker can tell CO3's
good pictures from its borrowed-part ones at any step. Run that cheap check, judge on "two clear
subjects, style ignored", and give the CLEVR two-colour test a paste row, before the next 40 or so
GPU-hours.

### Design problems

- **No positive control for Paper 2.** No step shows that any push can lift CO3's identity count, or
  that the checker separates CO3's two-clear pictures from its borrowed-part ones. M5, D3 and M2 can all
  tie near 11 of 96 and say nothing about data, architecture or labels (inferred). Neither the M5
  design (lines 79 to 91) nor the D3 design (lines 88 to 106) has such a row.
- **Ties will be settled by drawing style.** `checker_choice/selection.py` lines 45 to 55 break a tie
  of under 5 faithful pairs by the fewest damaged pictures, and M5 does the same (design line 88). With
  a base near 11 and a per-reviewer spread of 0 to 9, most outcomes will be ties, so the choice would
  follow which checker makes CO3 draw photographs (inferred).
- **The window is fixed before it is known.** Strength, steps and cap stay at the plain-product recipe
  through M5, D3 and M2 and are tuned only in M3 (Loop 6's brief, objective 7).
- **The D3 and M5 designs still state the old problem**: that SDXL fuses the two subjects (D3 design
  line 5; M5 design lines 5 to 7), although CO3 is the row that decides (board question 57).
- **The two-colour CLEVR test cannot separate Paper 1 from paste.** It has no paste row, no
  image-quality bar (`clevr-scaling-plan.md` line 77, rule 9) and no stated training set for its count
  checker. This repeats the trap of the first Paper 1 tests: a method can beat the plain product and
  still not beat paste.
- **One bar changed after a result, disclosed.** The CLEVR paste test's window check changed after it
  failed (owner's option A on question 66), before any paste was scored
  (`01c-clevr-paste-first/review.md` lines 88 to 91). No other bar change was found.
- **No test data used for tuning.** Held-out pairs and seeds 7001 to 7008 are untouched; lighthouse and
  sailboat is both a tuning and an unseen pair and is already reported both ways (D3 design lines 138
  to 139).
- **Cost.** About 18 GPU-hours spent; the designs commit about 49 more (M5 11, D3 20 plus 9 for the
  unseen-category test, M10 7.6, D5 1.4), which leaves about 20 of the 87-hour limit for M2, M3 and M8,
  which have no cost yet (inferred tally).

### The fix

1. **Measure whether the checker can see CO3's failure, before M5 renders its comparison.** Re-run CO3
   at its released settings on M1's 12 pairs at seeds 6001 to 6008, which reproduces the trajectories
   behind M1's 96 pictures. Read the kept checker's clean-pair score at steps 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and
   30, and score per pair how well it separates the 32 two-clear pictures from the 64 two-bodies
   pictures, as balanced accuracy. About 1.2 GPU-hours (96 runs at 43.2 seconds, M1 review line 47).
   If some window reaches 0.75 (bar written before the read), move M5's and D3's push there as one
   stated change. If no step from 0 to 30 reaches 0.65, the clean-pair search cannot fix CO3: pause D3
   and M2 and move the effort to an identity reader (Loop 8's ten-label signature in the later window,
   ported to SDXL, or the training-free candidates in `leakage/04-paper-2-method.md`). Both routes keep
   SDXL's weights untouched and one trajectory per seed.
2. **Decide M5 and D3 on "two clear subjects, style ignored", and report damage beside it.** Two
   reviewers per picture with their agreement reported and a third for disagreements, as M9 did. This
   changes a written bar, so it needs the owner's yes. No GPU cost.
3. **Give the two-colour CLEVR test a mask-paste row and a stated checker training set.** Lay each
   colour expert's foreground (its difference from the empty scene) over the other's sample, from
   samples M10 already draws, on the CPU. Paper 1 has a CLEVR claim only if the checker row leads
   mask-paste by 5 points of exact share and more than 1.96 standard errors. If paste matches it, the
   scenes need objects that overlap or shade each other, as Loop 4 proposed.
4. **Optional: a size-matched list in M5**, the current list cut to 877 pictures. About 2.5 GPU-hours to
   train and 1.6 for its 96 CO3 renders.

**It worked if** the checker's read of CO3's states clears 0.75 in a stated window and the search then
leads CO3 alone by Paper 2's bar on style-ignored counts; and on CLEVR, the checker row beats mask-paste.

### Design problems

- **V7 keeps the chained scoring.** Each property is scored only on canvases that passed the one before
  (`08-remove-one-checker/design.md` line 24; `11-remove-one-with-identity/design.md` lines 25 and 47,
  checked). Removing count changes who reaches the order column (134 against 38 canvases), so V7 can
  fail its "no other column moves" limit even with a perfect identity checker (inferred).
- **V6 has no baseline that needs no training.** Its signature holds all ten labels' predictions, which
  is what a diffusion model uses to classify with no training: pick the label whose noise prediction
  fits best (Diffusion Classifier, arXiv 2303.16203, pp. 3 to 4, Eq. 6 to 8, per its reading note).
  Without that baseline, 0.861 may be the frozen model's own knowledge of digits. The shuffled-label
  control does not test this.
- **V6 changed input and window together.** The V7 design discloses it at line 27; the grid itself is a
  clean 2 by 2.
- **V6 is read on forward-noised pasted canvases only, while V7 pushes on sampler states.** Loop 9 found
  a reader scores differently on the two (0.696 against 0.637, `noise-text-critic/05-verdict.md` line 23).
- **V4 aims at the wrong property and the wrong rival**: the count checker on SDXL steps 0 to 15 against
  the plain product (`03-design.md` lines 183 and 187), while Paper 2's open failure is identity against
  CO3.
- **On SDXL the signature has no fixed list of other labels**, which must be fixed before V4 runs
  (`10-reading-identity/review.md` line 72).
- **V3's push length was not bracketed**: 0.4 was the largest of the three values tried
  (`06-does-it-compose/review.md` line 59).
- **The open record disagreements still stand.** `requests.md` lines 220 and 238 list lines of
  `03-design.md` that disagree with what ran (push window, right-digit scoring, definition of null,
  V2's third bar, the map sum). Reported, not resolved here.

## Loop 12: where CO3 leaks

### In plain words

CO3 draws two animals but often gives one the other's parts, and this loop looks for where that starts
so it can test a fix there. Its measurement was sound and came back empty: none of three readings inside
the model predicts which pairs borrow (best rank correlation 0.49 against a bar of 0.706), and the most
alike pair never borrows. The fix it proposed weights each place on the canvas separately, but reads those
places at CO3's first six steps, where the one recorded case shows each animal's prediction still drawing
both bodies, the pattern behind Loop 6's three region misses. Test the per-place idea later in the run,
about steps 15 to 30, first with regions drawn by eye, and score it the way Paper 2's checker search is
now scored.

### Design problems

- **The proposed fix repeats Loop 6's region failure**: it separates the animals with a signal that has
  not yet separated them, and nothing blends regions back.
- **The per-place rule's direction is not defined**: the question and the ideas note describe two
  different rules.
- **No positive control**: nothing shows that pushing place by place fixes borrowing even with the right
  places; boxes drawn by eye are that control.
- **No control for extra correction alone**: CO3's whole-canvas weights in the same window are needed
  beside it.
- **Three scoring rules in play**: the brief's own-parts call, question 91's faithful pairs, and question
  87's two clear subjects; and a 96-picture bar applied to 48.
- **The six pairs were chosen for their CO3 labels**, so CO3 alone must be called again blind in the same

### In plain words

The idea adds, while sampling, the one term a product of two experts leaves out: how much more likely
the two concepts are together in this noisy picture than apart, read off a small classifier. The maths
is right for a single model and for combinations its data contains, and the term itself is already
published (CFGComposer supplies it from a jointly trained model), but no paper the project has read
estimates it with a classifier for combinations nobody saw, so that narrow claim is open. The trap is
that on such a combination the training data's ratio is zero, so a classifier that learns the data
faithfully pushes the picture away from the asked pair. Run it with the rebuilt estimator below, first
on combinations an expert saw but the classifier did not, with a push of the same length that carries
no interaction beside it.

### Question 3: can the ratio be learned on combinations nobody saw

**The data's own answer is "this pair does not happen".** On an unseen pair the training data's ratio is
zero (Question 1, row 2). A classifier that learns the data well therefore learns a large negative log
ratio there, and its gradient pushes the picture away from the asked combination, toward combinations
the holders saw (inferred). What composition needs is the ratio in a world where the pair does happen,
and no holder's data defines that world. Every estimator reaches the unseen pair only through a
structure that stops it from representing the pair's absence.

**The three candidates, judged.**

1. **Shuffled B against real triples: drop it.** With B shuffled across the batch, the best classifier's
   log odds are $\log p_t(B \mid x, A) - \log p(B)$, so its gradient is the wanted term plus
   $\nabla_x \log p_t(B \mid x)$, which is B's guidance counted a second time (inferred, from the optimal
   logistic classifier). Drawing the negative B from a trained $p_t(B \mid x)$ head instead of shuffling
   fixes that target (inferred). It cannot fix the unseen pairs: they appear only among the negatives, so
   the classifier learns from the labels alone that they are fake.
2. **Separate heads and a "both present as separate things" head: rebuild it, and try it first.** As
   written, the last head is the parked checker under a new name, and on Colored MNIST it asks the wrong
   question, because the digit and its colour are one object. Rebuilt: one small network on noisy
   pictures with a digit head, a colour head and an interaction head whose score for a pair is a rank-$r$
   product of a digit embedding and a colour embedding read from the picture. Its pair softmax runs over
   seen pairs only, so an unseen pair's score never receives a gradient and is set by the embeddings
   alone. Push with the interaction head's gradient only. This is the one design in which the pair's
   absence cannot be learned, so it is the one that can reach an unseen pair (inferred).
3. **A few pictures that show the combinations: not as the method on Colored MNIST.** Recolouring any
   training digit makes every combination for free, so any count of such pictures hands over the answer,
   and DCFM uses none, so its numbers stop being a comparison. Keep it as the positive control on seen
   combinations only. The exact toy already found that a push needs pictures of the pair (its
   discriminator saw 3,500), and that the push at pair weight 0.01 beat inclusive captions at 0.3 (A1).

**When a rank-$r$ interaction is even countable.** DCFM's 19 of 100 cells pay for the main effects. A
rank-$r$ interaction on 10 digits by 10 colours adds $r(18 - r)$ numbers, so the seen cells must reach
about 36 of 100 for rank 1, 51 for rank 2 and 64 for rank 3, and the seen cells must also be spread
across rows and columns (inferred, parameter counting; a necessary count, not a guarantee). DCFM's split
sits at 50 of 100, so rank 1 to 2 is the ceiling there. Lead 2's diagonal split at 10 of 100 cannot
support any interaction.

**What the project's own digits say.** A checker learned "two digits sitting apart" on digits 0 to 4 and
kept it on unseen 5 to 9 (balanced accuracy 0.791 against 0.782), while a checker for which digit is
present read 0.555 on an unseen digit ([the unseen-digit finding](../../../../2-classifier-guidance/report/abstract-checkers-on-digits/does-a-checker-learn-plurality-on-unseen-digits.md)).
Where generalises and which does not. Binding a colour to a digit is closer to "which", so the rebuilt
head may fail for the same reason, and that is what the gate in fix 3 tests (inferred).

### Question 4: is Colored MNIST with two holders a fair first test

**Fair as a cheap look at whether the estimator reaches an unseen pair; not evidence for Paper 2.** It

**DCFM's printed numbers are context, not a row.** Its 11.41 and 25.19 come from 10 clients (p. 7,

**What the checker arm already contains.** A checker for "this digit in this colour" is a joint
classifier, and the gradient of its log probability is the digit push plus the colour push plus the

### In plain words

The arm trains a small network to output the difference between what one expert predicts when asked for
"digit 4 in red" and what the plain product of both experts predicts, then adds that difference while
sampling. For a combination some holder saw, that difference is real and learnable, and it is large,
because the plain product averages both holders and the other holder never drew that combination. For a
combination no holder saw, no expert sample holds it and no network gives a joint prediction for it, so the
network is asked to guess its correction from the digit and the colour separately, which is a fair and
cheap question (about 1 GPU-hour) as long as the design says so. Run it after pinning the target, training
only on seen pairs, passing the same held-back seen pairs as arm 3, and scoring it beside a twin trained on
shuffled combinations and beside simply asking an expert for the unseen pair.

### Question 1: what the regression target is, and whether any joint signal exists

**The target, written out.** For a combination $(d, c)$ that holder $k$ saw, at a state $x_t$ and step
$t$,

$$r_t(x_t; d, c) = \epsilon_k(x_t, t \mid d, c) - \epsilon_{\text{PoE}}(x_t, t \mid d, c),$$

where $\epsilon_k(\cdot \mid d, c)$ is holder $k$'s expert asked with both labels and no guidance, and
$\epsilon_{\text{PoE}}$ is arm 1's formula at $g = 1$ exactly (design, line 31). Each seen combination
has exactly one such holder in this split (split.json, `holders`). The samples only decide where $x_t$
lies; the target is a pair of network outputs at that $x_t$. This is the same construction as the ICLR
adapter's target, $\Delta_t = g_s(\epsilon_j - \epsilon_a - \epsilon_b + \epsilon_\varnothing)$, formed
along the plain product's own sampling trajectories (`poe_repair/training_cache.py`, lines 11 to 13;
[the SuperDiff adapter plan](../../../../1-lora-correction/plans/06-is-the-gap-the-samplers-or-the-models/plans/baselines/08-adapters-that-learn-superdiffs-own-residual.md),
line 70).

| Assumption | Mark | Source |

### Question 1: is the paper described correctly, and does it transfer to the dropped term

| Claim | Mark | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-entropy plus a cosine penalty, $L = L_{CE} + \lambda \sum_{y_t=1}^{C} [1 - \cos(\nabla_x f_\theta(x)_{y_t}, g(x, y_t))]$ | holds | Eq. 4 and 5, p. 4 |
| The target is the class score minus the unconditional score from a diffusion model | holds | Eq. 7 and 8, p. 5, where $g = \nabla \log p(x_t \mid y) - \nabla \log p(x_t) = \nabla \log p(y \mid x_t)$ |
| What the idea leaves out | omitted, and each one matters here | the penalised gradient is of the logit $f_\theta(x)_y$, for every one of the $C$ classes at every training image, not the true class only (Eq. 4, p. 4; "C targeted gradients for each training sample", p. 16); the classifier reads clean images and the target is taken at one medium noise level, because at small noise the two scores are "almost identical" and at large noise "irrelevant to the input image" (p. 5; p. 16); both scores come from one diffusion model with an extra null class (p. 16) |
| The penalty makes a classifier's push steer better | unknown, no precedent in the read papers | the paper never guides a sampler. It has "no quantitative metrics" for alignment (p. 3, read via the notes) and its evidence is robust accuracy, 0% to 55.39% under AutoAttack as $\lambda$ goes 0 to 2 (Table 1, p. 7; Fig. 5, p. 8), at a clean-accuracy cost on CIFAR-100 of 55.94% against 74.36% (Table 2, p. 7; [H1 section 1](../directions/01-directions-that-generalise.md#1-classifier-gradients-robust-and-perceptually-aligned), line 83) |
| Plain cross-entropy leaves input gradients unconstrained | holds | Kawar, Ganz and Elad, arXiv 2208.08664, p. 5 (the reading notes, line 3) |
| The penalty "at each noisy training picture" | an extension the paper did not test | the paper uses one noise level; here it would act at all 50 steps, where the target shrinks late and its direction is mostly noise (inferred from p. 5's reason for a medium level) |

**Why Ganz's target and the dropped term behave differently.** Ganz's target is $\nabla \log p(y \mid x_t)$, the gradient of the
very quantity cross-entropy trains the output to estimate, so the two loss terms agree at the optimum and the penalty only chooses
among solutions cross-entropy already accepts (inferred). The dropped term is the gradient of another quantity,
$\log \frac{p(d,c \mid x)}{p(d \mid x)\,p(c \mid x)}$. On arm 2's checker output, cross-entropy pulls the gradient toward the digit
push plus the colour push plus that term (this file, "The ratio classifier", Question 4), while the penalty pulls it toward the
term alone. The two fight, and $\lambda$ then decides how much of arm 2's main-effect push survives (inferred). The direction
transfers cleanly in two forms only:

- **(a) The pair as the class.** Target: the score of the expert that saw the pair minus the unconditional score, in noise units
  $-(\epsilon_k(x_t, t \mid d, c) - \bar\epsilon_\varnothing(x_t, t))$, at guidance weight 1. It contains the dropped term, agrees with
  cross-entropy, and is Ganz's construction with the pair in place of the class.
- **(b) The dropped term alone, on an output with no cross-entropy.** That is arm 4 rewritten as the gradient of one number, a tighter
  form of a model that explained 22.4% of the term's variance on the held-back pairs against its own bar of 25%
  (`leakage/09-dcfm-colored-mnist/arm-4-gate-result.json`, `explained_variance` 0.2245, `passes` false).

Form (a) is the one to build. Report the cosine with the dropped term beside it as a reading that decides nothing.

**The sign.** Every Z9 push is added to the noise prediction as $-\text{strength}\cdot\sigma_t\,\nabla_x f$ (design, line 47;
`z9_push.py` line 88; `z9_sample.py` line 82). The push therefore moves the noise prediction along $-\nabla_x f$, and to move it
along $r_t = \epsilon_k - \epsilon_{\text{product}}$ the classifier's gradient must align with $-r_t$. The idea's penalty aligns
$\nabla_x f$ with $+r_t$ (idea, "The arm on coloured digits"), which trains the push to point against the dropped term.

**The same sign in M13, reported and not resolved.** M13 recorded the cosine between the checker's gradient and $r_t$
(`m5_read_co3.py`, lines 373 to 381: `cos(g_push[0], r_t)`, with `g_push` the gradient of the summed outputs). CO3's push subtracts
strength times $\sigma_t$ times that same gradient from both of CO3's entries (`co3_push.py`, lines 36 and 65 to 68; `log_prob_both`
is the same sum, `rung2/train_readout_pair.py` line 94). So the change the push makes to CO3's noise prediction has a cosine with
$r_t$ of **+0.105** at step 0, past M13's +0.10 line for "points the right way", +0.075 to +0.025 on plain PoE, and +0.139 on
two-clear pictures against +0.088 on the rest (inferred from the code; nothing was rerun). M13's review (lines 18 to 20 and 42 to
44), H4's "read both ways" table (lines 114 to 121) and the idea's opening paragraph all read it as "against". One state settles it:
apply the push, take the change in the noise prediction, and compute its cosine with $r_t$. If the code reading holds, M13's
outcome at step 0 on CO3 becomes "points the right way", and what that means for the identity head is the owner's call, since the
owner tied the two together.

### In plain words

On coloured digits a classifier learned to push along a direction supplied by the one expert that had seen each digit and colour
together, and that push beat every control. SDXL has no such expert: no model draws these animal pairs right, and the joint prompt draws
two clear animals half as often as CO3 does. The proposal builds a stand-in for that expert from two things: each animal's own
prediction, and where that animal ended up in the finished picture. Z7 tried that stand-in directly with the boxes given and came close
to working without passing (14 against 8 of 48, carried mostly by duck and goose). So first apply the stand-in as a push with the boxes
given, on new seeds, and train the classifier only if that passes. About 1.2 RunPod hours for that first stage and about 6 in all.

### What the push is aligned to, and whether the box correction can stand in for Z11's expert

**Z11's target came from an expert that had the answer.** It was $\epsilon_k(x_t \mid d,c) - \bar\epsilon_\varnothing(x_t)$: the one
data holder that saw digit $d$ in colour $c$, minus both holders asked nothing (Z11 design, line 10). That expert draws the pair right
by construction, its prediction exists at every noisy state of every training picture, and computing it needs only the state and the
label. On CO3 nothing has all three properties.

| Candidate target on CO3 | Has the answer? | Exists at every state without knowing the outcome? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| The joint prompt's prediction minus CO3's (the dropped term $r_t$, the closest thing to "a model that saw the pair") | **no**: drawn alone from CO3's noise it is two clear on 34 of 192 pictures against CO3's 73 (M14 review, lines 22 to 40) | yes | rejected: it points toward a worse picture, and the kept push already moves along it (+0.105 at step 0, M13 review, line 31) and lifted CO3 by nothing (M1, via Z8 review, line 17) |
| Each animal's own prediction inside that animal's box, minus CO3's prediction (the proposal) | **partly**: each single-animal prompt draws its own animal without the other's parts (Z7 design, line 5), and Z7's version of it is the best any CO3 fix has done, 14 of 48 against 8, p 0.073, short of its bar (Z7 review, line 3) | **no**: the boxes are read off the finished picture of the same trajectory | **the right target, as a stand-in only for the parts failure** |
| The kept head's own clean-pair output, with no new target | no | yes | the "no penalty" reference, already measured: M1's 96 draws, push 7 faithful against 11 for CO3 alone |

**So the push is aligned to a constructed direction, not to a model's answer.** Precisely, in noise units, at sampler step $s$ in 5 to 29
with state $x_t$:

$$T(x_t) = M_A \odot \big(\epsilon_A(x_t) - \epsilon_{\text{CO3}}(x_t)\big) + M_B \odot \big(\epsilon_B(x_t) - \epsilon_{\text{CO3}}(x_t)\big)$$

- $\epsilon_{\text{CO3}}$ is the guided noise prediction the push modifies: `uncond + guidance * (cond - uncond)` inside `Pusher.__call__`
  (`co3_push.py`, the `limit` line), on all 4 latent channels at 128 by 128.
- $\epsilon_A = \epsilon_\varnothing + \lambda\,(\epsilon(x_t \mid \text{"a duck"}) - \epsilon_\varnothing)$ at CO3's own guidance $\lambda$
  (`lc.lmda`) and single-subject prompts, exactly as Z7's `guess` forms it (`z3_regions.py`, lines 63 to 66).
- $M_A$ is 1 on latent cells inside A's box and outside B's box, 0 elsewhere (`box_mask`, same file, lines 41 to 46). Where the boxes
  overlap and outside both, the target is 0: the push is asked to change nothing there.
- The push changes the noise prediction by $-\text{strength}\cdot\sigma_t\,\nabla_x f$ (`co3_push.py`: `eps - push`; M13's sign check,
  cosine −1.0 on 12 states), so the penalty is $1 - \cos(\nabla_x f,\; -T)$, the same sign rule as Z11 (Z11 design, line 11).

**It is not Z7's correction in Z7's form.** Z7 added a second corrector pass that rebuilt the state from clean-picture guesses, with a
largest-entry rescale and a rescale to the state's length (`z3_regions.py`, lines 75 to 79). The push changes the main step's noise
prediction instead. The two share a direction inside the boxes (inferred: the corrector's change in clean guess is $-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha}$
times the weighted change in noise prediction, since the weights sum to 1 in both rows) but not a size or a mechanism, so Z7's 14 of 48
says nothing yet about $T$ applied as a push.

**What the classifier must learn that Z11's did not.** On digits the target needed only the state and the asked pair. Here it needs the
outcome: where each animal will be. The checker therefore has to read each animal's eventual place from $x_t$. Two records say that
information is already in the state by step 5: Z7 applied boxes from CO3's own finished pictures from step 5 onward and broke none of
48 pictures (Z7 review, line 13), and on CO3 the two animal words already attend to separate places at step 5 on most pictures (M15
review, lines 36 to 40). Neither shows that the kept head's 128 pooled features carry it (unknown, measured by the gate below).

### Question 1: the target and how to compute it at the push's noise levels

$T$ above, formed at each state from 3 UNet calls at batch 3 (empty, A alone, B alone) plus CO3's own 2-entry call, at steps 5 to 29,

**Labels are boxes, never two-clear calls.** The 192 blind calls train nothing; they only score renders. That removes the route by which

28-pixel, 3-channel class-conditional U-Net whose digit and colour embeddings are added (`interaction: sum`), DDPM with 1,000
steps, noise prediction, 50,000 training steps at batch 256. That is the same kind of model as Z9's experts (two embeddings added;
Z9 design, line 23). The README prints CoInD's own Colored MNIST sweep: on partial support, λ = 1 gives a conformity score for
"digit and colour" of 0.5444, against 0.4586 at λ = 0.5 and 0.0295 at λ = 5.

**What it does not hold.** The penalty in `trainer.py` (line 106) compares one model with itself: the squared difference between
its prediction with both labels plus its unconditional prediction, and its prediction with each single label summed. That is
DCFM's local penalty (Eq. 10, p. 5). Nothing in the repository routes between several models or freezes a peer.

**DCFM's code is still not public.** No link in the paper and none found on 2026-11-25 (web search; Loop 14's page records the same
check on GitHub). DfD, one of DCFM's two baselines, is public at `github.com/vaseline555/DfD`, last pushed 2026-01-31, with no
licence file the API reports. Its sampler is ten lines (`strategy/dfd.py`, lines 36 to 48): each expert's noise prediction is
weighted per picture by a softmax of minus its squared length, and the mixed score takes an unadjusted Langevin step. Decentralized
Diffusion Models (McAllister et al., CVPR 2025) has no code found.

### Question 3: the bar, written first

**Where the bar is decided: DCFM's uneven split, freshly drawn.** Rows 4 and 5 were scored on the two-holder split's 50 unseen
combinations already (Z11 review, lines 23 and 24: 88.8% and 73.8%), and their settings were chosen after Z9's results there. A bar on that split
would be written after its result. DCFM's uneven split is new to every row: 10 holders, 50 of 100 combinations seen, drawn with seed
20261127 under DCFM's coverage condition (every digit and every colour seen, Definition 3.1, p. 3), each seen combination's pictures
shared among the holders by a Dirichlet draw with α = 0.1 (p. 7). The two-holder split is reported beside it with the same tests,
deciding nothing for rows 4 and 5. DCFM's even split is left out of the first run (see the cost).

**The comparisons.** Primary: row 5 against row 3b. Secondary: row 5 against row 4, the names-only push at row 5's length. Both on
the 50 unseen combinations, 5,000 pictures matched by starting noise.

**Checks that must pass before any comparison counts.**

1. The judge (Z9's) reads real unseen-combination test digits right at least 95% of the time.
2. Each Stage I expert draws its own seen combinations right at least 80% of the time, 20 pictures each (Z9 control 3).
3. A push of strength 0 gives row 1's pictures byte for byte on 5 combinations, one process, one card.
4. **The DCFM-A rebuild reproduces DCFM's direction:** row 3b at least 5 points above row 1 with an exact one-sided McNemar
   p < 0.05, and row 3b's novel FID below row 2a's. If it fails, row 3b is printed as "our rebuild did not reproduce DCFM-A's gain"
   and nothing is claimed against DCFM-A.

**Outcomes.** "Interval" is a 95% bootstrap over the 50 combinations (10,000 resamples of combinations, all their pictures kept
together), because the 100 pictures of one combination are not independent and McNemar alone treats them as if they were.

| Outcome | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Ahead of DCFM-A | row 5's accuracy at least 5 points above row 3b's, exact one-sided McNemar p < 0.05, accuracy interval above 0, and novel FID below row 3b's with the FID-difference interval below 0 |
| Level with DCFM-A | the accuracy-difference interval inside −5 to +5 points |
| Behind DCFM-A | row 3b's accuracy at least 5 points above row 5's, McNemar p < 0.05 |
| Inconclusive | none of the above, or check 4 fails |
| More than the two names (secondary) | row 5 at least 5 points above row 4, McNemar p < 0.05, interval above 0 |

Novel precision and recall (Kynkäänniemi et al., DCFM Fig. 6) are printed for every row from the same Inception features. Any row
whose novel recall is more than 0.05 below row 3b's carries a flag in the table, and no row with a flag may be called better on
diversity. One training seed per row; the design's "intervals over seeds" would multiply every training cost and is replaced by the
combination bootstrap. FID: `pytorch-fid`, 2,048 pooled features, 5,000 generated against 5,000 real test pictures of the same
combinations, real-against-real floor printed, exactly as Z9.
