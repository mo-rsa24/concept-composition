## Loop 6: find the best method

### In plain words

Loop 6 looks for one recipe per paper: for Paper 1 it must beat sampling each model alone and pasting
the results, for Paper 2 it must beat CO3. Both first tests came out level: 295 clean digit pairs of 320
against paste's 287, and 7 faithful pictures of 96 against CO3's 11. The next Paper 2 steps choose the
checker's data, architecture and labels with the push fixed at steps 0 to 15 of 50, although the other
loops find identity readable only later, and nobody has checked whether this checker can tell CO3's
good pictures from its borrowed-part ones at any step. Run that cheap check, judge on "two clear
subjects, style ignored", and give the CLEVR two-colour test a paste row, before the next 40 or so
GPU-hours.

### Design problems

- **No positive control for Paper 2.** No step shows that any push can lift CO3's identity count, or
  that the checker separates CO3's two-clear pictures from its borrowed-part ones. M5, D3 and M2 can all
  tie near 11 of 96 and say nothing about data, architecture or labels (inferred). Neither the M5
  design (lines 79 to 91) nor the D3 design (lines 88 to 106) has such a row.
- **Ties will be settled by drawing style.** `checker_choice/selection.py` lines 45 to 55 break a tie
  of under 5 faithful pairs by the fewest damaged pictures, and M5 does the same (design line 88). With
  a base near 11 and a per-reviewer spread of 0 to 9, most outcomes will be ties, so the choice would
  follow which checker makes CO3 draw photographs (inferred).
- **The window is fixed before it is known.** Strength, steps and cap stay at the plain-product recipe
  through M5, D3 and M2 and are tuned only in M3 (Loop 6's brief, objective 7).
- **The D3 and M5 designs still state the old problem**: that SDXL fuses the two subjects (D3 design
  line 5; M5 design lines 5 to 7), although CO3 is the row that decides (board question 57).
- **The two-colour CLEVR test cannot separate Paper 1 from paste.** It has no paste row, no
  image-quality bar (`clevr-scaling-plan.md` line 77, rule 9) and no stated training set for its count
  checker. This repeats the trap of the first Paper 1 tests: a method can beat the plain product and
  still not beat paste.
- **One bar changed after a result, disclosed.** The CLEVR paste test's window check changed after it
  failed (owner's option A on question 66), before any paste was scored
  (`01c-clevr-paste-first/review.md` lines 88 to 91). No other bar change was found.
- **No test data used for tuning.** Held-out pairs and seeds 7001 to 7008 are untouched; lighthouse and
  sailboat is both a tuning and an unseen pair and is already reported both ways (D3 design lines 138
  to 139).
- **Cost.** About 18 GPU-hours spent; the designs commit about 49 more (M5 11, D3 20 plus 9 for the
  unseen-category test, M10 7.6, D5 1.4), which leaves about 20 of the 87-hour limit for M2, M3 and M8,
  which have no cost yet (inferred tally).

### The fix

1. **Measure whether the checker can see CO3's failure, before M5 renders its comparison.** Re-run CO3
   at its released settings on M1's 12 pairs at seeds 6001 to 6008, which reproduces the trajectories
   behind M1's 96 pictures. Read the kept checker's clean-pair score at steps 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and
   30, and score per pair how well it separates the 32 two-clear pictures from the 64 two-bodies
   pictures, as balanced accuracy. About 1.2 GPU-hours (96 runs at 43.2 seconds, M1 review line 47).
   If some window reaches 0.75 (bar written before the read), move M5's and D3's push there as one
   stated change. If no step from 0 to 30 reaches 0.65, the clean-pair search cannot fix CO3: pause D3
   and M2 and move the effort to an identity reader (Loop 8's ten-label signature in the later window,
   ported to SDXL, or the training-free candidates in `leakage/04-paper-2-method.md`). Both routes keep
   SDXL's weights untouched and one trajectory per seed.
2. **Decide M5 and D3 on "two clear subjects, style ignored", and report damage beside it.** Two
   reviewers per picture with their agreement reported and a third for disagreements, as M9 did. This
   changes a written bar, so it needs the owner's yes. No GPU cost.
3. **Give the two-colour CLEVR test a mask-paste row and a stated checker training set.** Lay each
   colour expert's foreground (its difference from the empty scene) over the other's sample, from
   samples M10 already draws, on the CPU. Paper 1 has a CLEVR claim only if the checker row leads
   mask-paste by 5 points of exact share and more than 1.96 standard errors. If paste matches it, the
   scenes need objects that overlap or shade each other, as Loop 4 proposed.
4. **Optional: a size-matched list in M5**, the current list cut to 877 pictures. About 2.5 GPU-hours to
   train and 1.6 for its 96 CO3 renders.

**It worked if** the checker's read of CO3's states clears 0.75 in a stated window and the search then
leads CO3 alone by Paper 2's bar on style-ignored counts; and on CLEVR, the checker row beats mask-paste.

### Design problems

- **V7 keeps the chained scoring.** Each property is scored only on canvases that passed the one before
  (`08-remove-one-checker/design.md` line 24; `11-remove-one-with-identity/design.md` lines 25 and 47,
  checked). Removing count changes who reaches the order column (134 against 38 canvases), so V7 can
  fail its "no other column moves" limit even with a perfect identity checker (inferred).
- **V6 has no baseline that needs no training.** Its signature holds all ten labels' predictions, which
  is what a diffusion model uses to classify with no training: pick the label whose noise prediction
  fits best (Diffusion Classifier, arXiv 2303.16203, pp. 3 to 4, Eq. 6 to 8, per its reading note).
  Without that baseline, 0.861 may be the frozen model's own knowledge of digits. The shuffled-label
  control does not test this.
- **V6 changed input and window together.** The V7 design discloses it at line 27; the grid itself is a
  clean 2 by 2.
- **V6 is read on forward-noised pasted canvases only, while V7 pushes on sampler states.** Loop 9 found
  a reader scores differently on the two (0.696 against 0.637, `noise-text-critic/05-verdict.md` line 23).
- **V4 aims at the wrong property and the wrong rival**: the count checker on SDXL steps 0 to 15 against
  the plain product (`03-design.md` lines 183 and 187), while Paper 2's open failure is identity against
  CO3.
- **On SDXL the signature has no fixed list of other labels**, which must be fixed before V4 runs
  (`10-reading-identity/review.md` line 72).
- **V3's push length was not bracketed**: 0.4 was the largest of the three values tried
  (`06-does-it-compose/review.md` line 59).
- **The open record disagreements still stand.** `requests.md` lines 220 and 238 list lines of
  `03-design.md` that disagree with what ran (push window, right-digit scoring, definition of null,
  V2's third bar, the map sum). Reported, not resolved here.

## Loop 12: where CO3 leaks

### In plain words

CO3 draws two animals but often gives one the other's parts, and this loop looks for where that starts
so it can test a fix there. Its measurement was sound and came back empty: none of three readings inside
the model predicts which pairs borrow (best rank correlation 0.49 against a bar of 0.706), and the most
alike pair never borrows. The fix it proposed weights each place on the canvas separately, but reads those
places at CO3's first six steps, where the one recorded case shows each animal's prediction still drawing
both bodies, the pattern behind Loop 6's three region misses. Test the per-place idea later in the run,
about steps 15 to 30, first with regions drawn by eye, and score it the way Paper 2's checker search is
now scored.

### Design problems

- **The proposed fix repeats Loop 6's region failure**: it separates the animals with a signal that has
  not yet separated them, and nothing blends regions back.
- **The per-place rule's direction is not defined**: the question and the ideas note describe two
  different rules.
- **No positive control**: nothing shows that pushing place by place fixes borrowing even with the right
  places; boxes drawn by eye are that control.
- **No control for extra correction alone**: CO3's whole-canvas weights in the same window are needed
  beside it.
- **Three scoring rules in play**: the brief's own-parts call, question 91's faithful pairs, and question
  87's two clear subjects; and a 96-picture bar applied to 48.
- **The six pairs were chosen for their CO3 labels**, so CO3 alone must be called again blind in the same
  set, or the fix gains from regression to the mean (inferred). They are tuning pairs.
