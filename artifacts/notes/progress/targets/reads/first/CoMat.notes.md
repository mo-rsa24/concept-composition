## T2I-CompBench: scorer, prompts, seeds and images

**Scorer.** The paper does not name the scorer for T2I-CompBench. It says it follows the benchmark's default settings (p8) and that all results use the latest code in the T2I-CompBench GitHub repository (p8, footnote 1). The metric is recorded as the caption prints it, `T2I-CompBench result`. BLIP appears in this paper as the captioning model used during training (p7, p8), and the paper never says BLIP scores Table 1.

**Prompts.** The benchmark is described as 6,000 compositional prompts over 3 categories and 6 sub-categories (p8). The paper does not say how many prompts were evaluated per sub-category or which split was evaluated.

**Overlap with training.** Training uses the training set provided in T2I-CompBench, plus all of HRS-Bench and 5,000 prompts from ABC-6K, about 20,000 prompts in total (p8). Because the evaluated split is not stated, the paper does not rule out overlap between training and evaluation prompts.

**Seeds and images.** Neither the seed count nor the images per prompt is stated for any benchmark table.

**Reproducibility statement.** The authors say they cannot reproduce results reported in references [7] and [28] because the evaluation code has changed (p8).

**How the SDXL baseline was run: not stated.** The paper gives no evaluation sampler, step count, guidance scale, resolution, seed count or refiner setting for SDXL. The DDPM sampler with 50 steps, guidance 7.5 and 512 x 512 resolution describe the training process (p22, Table 8).

**Which SDXL UNet is unclear.** Before CoMat training, the SDXL UNet is fine-tuned for 100 steps on resized 512 x 512 images so that it generates well at that size (p22). The paper does not say whether the SDXL baseline row is the pre-trained model or this fine-tuned UNet, and it does not state the resolution at which CoMat-SDXL was evaluated.

## Other protocol facts a comparison depends on

**TIFA.** 4,000 text prompts and 25,000 questions across 12 categories, scored with a VQA model the paper does not name (p8).

**DPG-Bench.** 1065 dense prompts with an average token length of 83.91 (p8). The model accepts only 77 tokens (p8), which is below that average.

**FID.** Table 3 uses 10K data from the COCO validation set (p9). It covers SD1.5 and CoMat-SD1.5 only, so the paper prints no FID for CoMat-SDXL.

**Prompt attack.** Table 6 uses the attack of reference [16], with 20 samples per class for 1,000 ImageNet-1K classes (p10). The visual classifier is not named.

**Sensitivity test.** Table 7 uses 200 images: 100 from the ARO benchmark and 100 generated by SD1.5 and SDXL from T2I-CompBench prompts (p19).

**Table 5 backbone.** The table does not name the diffusion model. Its BLIP row equals the CoMat-SDXL row of Table 1 digit for digit, and its N/A row equals the SDXL row, so its rows are recorded as SDXL.

## Hardware and training time

Training runs 2,000 iterations on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with a LoRA of rank 128 at 512 x 512 and FP16 (p22). Table 8 states about 10 hours for SD1.5 and about 24 hours for SDXL. Local batch size is 4 for SD1.5 and 6 for SDXL. Gradients are enabled in 5 of the 50 denoising steps (p22). Table 8 is otherwise hyperparameters and is marked `no results`.

## Where the paper disagrees with itself

**Image-to-text models (p10 against Table 5, p9).** The text says all three image-to-text models boost performance. In Table 5, LLaVA is below the no-model row on Spatial (0.1963 against 0.2131) and on Non-Spatial (0.3117 against 0.3119).

**"Significant gains in all six sub-categories" (p8).** The printed Non-Spatial gains are 0.0054 for SD1.5 and 0.0068 for SDXL, and the paper gives no uncertainty for any score.

**"All three compared models ignore the unrealistic content" (p8 against Figure 3, p4).** SDXL draws a small metal teapot fused to a mushroom stem with the waterfall pouring from it. Playground v2 draws a large teapot in the first row and gold patches in the waterfall in the fourth. PixArt-Alpha draws a translucent amber violin and a gold-tinted waterfall.

**Identical FID (Table 3, p8).** The base SD1.5 row and the CoMat-SD1.5 row with a UNet discriminator on generated latents and no mixed latent both print 16.69. The paper does not comment on this.

**Small errors.** SDXL is cited as [56] on p7 and p17 and as [49] elsewhere. References [19] and [20] are the same paper. Figure 10 labels a row "Quality Sensitivity" while the text on p19 calls it "Quantity sensitivity".

**Text layer.** The extracted text of p24 holds the spider prompt from Figure 12, and no such caption is drawn on p24. It was not used.
