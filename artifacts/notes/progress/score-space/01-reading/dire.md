# DIRE for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection

arXiv 2303.09295 (v1), read from the full PDF (17 pages, appendix included). No venue is printed on the PDF.

## What it does
The paper detects whether an image was generated by a diffusion model. It inverts the image to noise with deterministic DDIM, reconstructs it with the same model, and takes the absolute pixel difference, called DIRE (diffusion reconstruction error) (p. 4). A ResNet-50 trained on DIRE images separates real from generated images and carries over to generators it never saw in training (p. 5, p. 6).

## What it reads
A clean RGB image $x_0$ at 256 × 256, cropped to 224 × 224 for the classifier (p. 5). The reconstruction model is an unconditional ADM pre-trained on LSUN-Bedroom (p. 5, p. 11). The image is inverted to $x_T$ with $S = 20$ DDIM steps by default (Eq. 8) and reconstructed with the same 20 steps (Eq. 5) (p. 4, p. 5, p. 8). The classifier reads only the residual image (Eq. 9, p. 4):

$$
\text{DIRE}(x_0) = \big|\,x_0 - R(I(x_0))\,\big|
$$

$I(\cdot)$ is the DDIM inversion chain, $R(\cdot)$ the DDIM reconstruction chain, and $|\cdot|$ an elementwise absolute value. Noise predictions $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$ are used inside the two chains but never read directly; no text embedding or internal feature is used.

## What it trains
Only the ResNet-50 detector; the diffusion model is frozen (p. 5). Loss (Eq. 10, p. 5):

$$
L(y, y') = -\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(y_i \log y'_i + (1 - y_i)\log(1 - y'_i)\big)
$$

with $N$ the mini-batch size, $y$ the real or generated label, $y'$ the prediction. Labels come from provenance: real LSUN-Bedroom images against images sampled from one generator. The default training set is 40,000 real and 40,000 ADM-generated LSUN-Bedroom images, with 1,000 for validation and 1,000 for testing per side (p. 5, Table 1 p. 4). Generated training images were sampled with 50 DDIM steps (p. 11). Augmentation is random 224 crops and horizontal flips with probability 0.5 (p. 5). Training length, optimizer and learning rate are not stated.

## How noise predictions are combined or compared
They are not combined across conditions. One unconditional model is applied along a deterministic path. Inversion step (Eq. 8, p. 4):

$$
\frac{x_{t+1}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{t+1}}} = \frac{x_t}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}} + \left(\sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha_{t+1}}{\alpha_{t+1}}} - \sqrt{\frac{1-\alpha_t}{\alpha_t}}\right)\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)
$$

with $\alpha_t$ the cumulative signal schedule. Reconstruction runs Eq. 5 with $\sigma_t = 0$, "in which one noise sample determines one generated image" (p. 3). The comparison is between the input and its round trip, in pixel space, after all $S$ steps. No noise is sampled, so there is no seed; every timestep in the 20-step subset is used once in each direction with no weighting.

## When adding scores or embeddings is valid
This paper does not address it.

## What it says about the noise and the seed
The pipeline has no random noise: DDIM with $\sigma_t = 0$ is deterministic (p. 3), so each image has one DIRE. The paper does not report seed or run-to-run variance. It explains nonzero DIRE on generated images by the discretization error of treating DDIM as Euler integration of an ODE with finite $T$, and asserts without measurement that "the deviation is more prominent for real images" (p. 11).

## Which timesteps carry the signal
Not measured per timestep. Only the number of DDIM steps is varied (Table 4, p. 7). With the detector trained on ADM images, accuracy on Stable Diffusion v1 images is 87.5% at $S = 5$, 98.2% at $S = 10$, 99.7% at $S = 20$ and 99.9% at $S = 50$; AP is 99.8% or 100% throughout. Unconditional LSUN generators are at 100% for every $S$ (p. 7).

## Unseen concepts and composition
This paper does not test held-out concepts. It holds out generators and datasets: a detector trained on ADM bedrooms is tested on 7 other bedroom generators including text-to-image models prompted "A photo of bedroom" (Table 2, p. 6), on ImageNet images from ADM and Stable Diffusion v1 prompted "A photo of {class}" (Table 3, p. 6), and on StyleGAN images (p. 7). Nothing on compositional prompts, relations, counting or binding.

## What it suggests for this loop
- Score-path encoder (inferred): a residual built from a frozen model's $\epsilon_\theta$ along a whole inversion path is enough for a small classifier to tell the model's own samples from other images at 99.9% accuracy. A contrastive encoder on score paths may therefore learn "on or off the model's manifold" before it learns the concept. PoE samples and joint samples come from different samplers, so this cue could separate them by provenance alone; a control that matches realism is needed.
- Seed (inferred): deterministic DDIM inversion makes the path a function of the image. Computing score paths along the inversion of a finished canvas, instead of along a forward sampling run from a random seed, removes the seed the brief found dominating the SDXL direction (cosine about 0.002 across seeds).
- Sum lands on joint (inferred): invert the product's final canvas with the joint-conditioned model and measure its reconstruction error. A low error says the product's output lies where the joint model would generate, a test that needs no encoder.
- Gap predicts the term (inferred): the paper's ablations say the residual carries the signal and the reconstruction alone does not (46.9% vs 99.7%), and that taking the absolute value adds 12.7 accuracy points on Stable Diffusion. For the gap test this favours a difference of paths over the paths themselves, and says sign handling matters.
- Separate-experts paper (inferred): a DIRE-style error under each expert could flag which expert's manifold a composed sample left.
- CO3 paper (inferred): reconstruction error under the joint model is a cheap per-sample score for ranking or filtering CO3 outputs.

## Closer work it cites
None. The two cited diffusion-image detectors (Ricker et al., arXiv 2210.14571; Corvi et al., arXiv 2211.00680, both p. 3) train on RGB images, which is outside the scope asked for.
