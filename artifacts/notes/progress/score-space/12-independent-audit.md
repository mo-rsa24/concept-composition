# Independent audit of Loop 10's conclusions

This page checks the conclusions Loop 10 reached on 2026-11-24 against the evidence files, the paper notes and the owner's recorded intent. It was written by a reader who ran nothing and changed no other file. Every claim cites a file and line (`file:line`, paths from this folder unless written from the repo root) or a paper and page. Anything marked (inferred) is this audit's own reasoning.

**Overall verdict.** The one measurement is honest and its numbers check out, but it answered a narrower question than the owner asked. X5 measured whether a digit model's ten noise predictions let a small probe read which digits are present. The owner's question is whether there is a representation in which adding two scores means "cat and dog". The digit testbed could not express that question at all, because no digit model has a joint label ([the ideas page, C2a](03-ideas.md), line 103), and the arithmetic tests that would have touched it sat behind a readability gate and never ran ([the brief](../prompts/session-10-the-score-path-as-a-third-space.md), line 117). The data-processing argument used to close the score path is correct about information and silent about additivity. Of the three closed ideas, this audit agrees with closing (c) in its stated form, agrees with closing (a) only as "a new source of information", and finds (b) narrowed by theory rather than closed: its exact form fails for the same reason the product of experts fails, and its weaker form, a space in which each subject owns its own block, is a live and testable route for Paper 2.

## Words this uses

**Signature.** Loop 10's repaired score path: the ten maps $d_t(c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)$, one per digit label, on one noisy canvas ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 18).

**Presence accuracy.** Balanced accuracy of calling "this digit is on the canvas", on a 0 to 1 scale where 0.5 is chance ([the design](04-design.md), line 33).

**Additive representation.** A space in which the embedding of "a cat and a dog" equals the embedding of "a cat" plus that of "a dog" minus that of the empty prompt, so that adding scores there would mean adding the words. This is the owner's question (b).

**And-score.** $u_{cd} - u_c - u_d + u_\varnothing$ for embeddings, or $\log \frac{p_t(x \mid cd)\,p_t(x \mid \varnothing)}{p_t(x \mid c)\,p_t(x \mid d)}$ for noised densities. It is zero exactly when the pair adds ([the Projective Composition note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/projective-composition.md), lines 86 to 90; [the Linear Spaces note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/linear-spaces-of-meanings.md), line 73).

## Question 1: were the three closures supported?

### (a) The score path as a third representation

**What the session concluded.** "By the data-processing inequality it holds no more information about the clean picture than $x_t$ does. So it cannot be a third source of information beside the picture" ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 42). The owner closed the loop on this reading (line 44), and the directions map records "it holds no information beyond the canvas" as the reason ([the directions map](../../../ideas/which-directions-to-pursue/README.md), line 28).

**Where this audit agrees.** The inequality is applied correctly. $d_t(c)$ is a deterministic function of $x_t$ and $t$, so the mutual information between the signature and the clean canvas cannot exceed that between $x_t$ and the clean canvas. As a claim that the score path adds information the canvas lacks, the closure holds.

**Where it does not address the owner's question.** The owner asked whether score space and text space share a representation in which adding is meaningful. That is a question about geometry (which operations commute with which), and mutual information says nothing about it (inferred). Three things show the argument proves too much or too little:

1. Every learned representation is a function of its input. CLIP's image embedding is a function of the image, and the inequality would equally "close" CLIP as a space in which to compare text and pictures (inferred).
2. Under deterministic DDIM the finished picture is a function of the starting noise, so the same argument says the finished picture holds no information beyond $x_T$. It is true and useless for steering (inferred).
3. What a sampler needs from a reader is readability at the step where it acts and a gradient that moves the right thing. The session's own result is that the signature is easier to read than the canvas, 0.700 against 0.593 at timestep 480 ([the X5 review](05-signal/review.md), line 35 to 36), which the verdict concedes ("a view of the picture that is easier to read", [the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 42). On SDXL the reader that works earliest is also bounded by the inequality: the readout checker on SDXL's own U-Net features reads a clean pair at 0.63, 0.74, 0.78 and 0.79 at steps 0, 5, 10 and 15, where a CLIP checker on the decoded guess reads 0.50 to 0.58 ([the checker loop](../../../../2-classifier-guidance/artifacts/drips/rung2-pair-aware-discriminator/checker-loop.md), lines 53 to 57), and its gradient moves layout where the decoded-guess gradient moves only texture (line 69).

**Call.** Close it in a narrower form: the score path is not a new source of information. Leave open the score path as the coordinates in which composition is done, which the data-processing argument does not touch. The SDXL form of the idea, reading each subject's own signed $d_t$ during CO3 (idea D3, [the ideas page](03-ideas.md), line 118 to 119), is untested and is the session's own best suggestion for Paper 2 ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 54).

### (b) A shared representation in which adding scores is adding text

**What the session argued.** The written record carries this argument only briefly: the questions that replace the loop "go to the sampler and to where the experts' data overlap" ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 44), and "a product of experts puts its mass where both experts' data overlap" ([the Paper 1 design](10-paper-1-placement-or-sampler.md), line 9). The fuller form the caller quotes, that the product of experts is an exact probability product whose gap to the joint comes from training-data co-occurrence, does not appear in any file this audit found, so it is judged as stated.

**Is it correct?** Half of it.

- The product is an exact product only of the clean densities. At every noisy step the sum of noised scores is not the score of the noised product ([RRR, p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.11552#page=4); [the RRR note](01-reading/reduce-reuse-recycle.md), line 44), and on the exact toy that path error alone takes the pair from 300 of 300 under the true noised product to 0, 0, 15 and 109 of 300 under summed scores ([where we are stuck](../../../ideas/beyond-the-product-of-experts/where-we-are-stuck.md), lines 98 to 100). So the sampler does not draw the product even when the product is right.
- The gap to the joint is a property of the learned conditionals. The dropped term is the gradient of the pointwise mutual information between the two concepts given the picture, which Liu et al. assume away with "the concepts are conditionally independent given x" ([Composable Diffusion, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01714#page=7); memory note `poe-interaction-term-literature`, lines 48 to 54). That mutual information comes from what the data put together, so "it comes from co-occurrence" is fair as a description of where the term originates (inferred).

**Does it close (b)?** It reframes it. Two theorems, read together, say what survives.

**The exact form is closed by theory.** Linear Spaces of Meanings proves that CLIP's text embeddings of a factored set add exactly when the factors are conditionally independent given any image under CLIP's own probability model ([Linear Spaces, Proposition 7 and Corollary 8, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14383#page=5); [its note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/linear-spaces-of-meanings.md), lines 31 to 37). That is the same conditional independence that makes the sum of scores exact. So in any representation where "cat" plus "dog" equals "cat and dog", the and-score is zero, and co-occurrence, which is exactly a nonzero and-score, cannot be carried by the sum (inferred, from [the Linear Spaces note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/linear-spaces-of-meanings.md), line 73, and [the Projective Composition note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/projective-composition.md), lines 86 to 90). A shared space would align the two failures; it would not remove them. This also answers the owner's phrase "without a shared representation, they contend": the contention is the and-score, and it lives in both spaces.

**A weaker form is alive.** Bradley et al.'s Theorem 6.1 says the summed score gives the correct composition at $t = 0$ if some invertible map $A$ puts each expert's concept in its own block of coordinates, with the background distribution elsewhere, and nobody needs to know $A$ ([Projective Composition, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04549#page=8); [its note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/projective-composition.md), lines 39 to 41). Sampling is guaranteed only when $A$ is orthogonal (Lemma 7.1, p. 9), and can fail with a correct target otherwise (Lemma 7.2, p. 9). A split of the latent grid into two regions is a coordinate partition, which is orthogonal, so Lemma 7.1 applies to it (inferred). Concept Algebra gives the same shape: concepts that are causally separable add in the centred score ([Concept Algebra, Definition 3.2 and Proposition 3.1, p. 5 to 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03693#page=5)), cat and dog as values of one species concept are not separable ([its note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/concept-algebra.md), line 84), and "subject in region 1" with "subject in region 2" could be (inferred). The owner's "shared representation" in this form is a representation in which each subject owns its own block, and the simplest one is space.

**Call.** Narrowed, not closed. The exact additive space cannot exist for co-occurring pairs. The question that replaces it is which representation gives each subject its own block, and whether a sampler can find that split on CO3 without being told. Loop 6's M9 is already this experiment with regions as the blocks, stopped only because its region reader failed ([the Loop 6 page](02-the-loops/loop-6-find-the-best-method.md), line 201).

### (c) NoisyCLIP or Latent CLIP to push SDXL's early steps

**The evidence.** No reader beat frozen CLIP at steps 0 to 15 on counting, prompt match or left-right order; the only gap above 1.96 standard errors rested on 2 right pictures ([the Loop 9 page](02-the-loops/loop-9-turn-concepts-into-scores.md), lines 166 and 202). A CLIP checker on the decoded guess reads 0.50 to 0.54 before step 10, and its push raised its own score by 1 to 13.5 logits without moving the animals ([the checker loop](../../../../2-classifier-guidance/artifacts/drips/rung2-pair-aware-discriminator/checker-loop.md), lines 53 to 57 and 69).

**Call.** Agree with closing it as a push at steps 0 to 15 for layout or count. Two things fall outside what was tested. NoisyCLIP's prompt match reads 0.67 at step 20 and 0.78 at step 30 ([the Loop 9 page](02-the-loops/loop-9-turn-concepts-into-scores.md), line 168), and binding, the failure CO3 makes, was never scored by any of these readers: W2d's part-binding test was inconclusive by design with 7 own-parts and 15 borrowed pictures in the mixed pairs, and its colour test failed its label check at 26 of 32 ([the W2d review](../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/review.md), lines 3 and 9). So a mid-run identity reader on CO3 is untested, which is a different idea from the one closed.

## Question 2: was X5 a fair test of the owner's idea?

It was a fair test of "can a probe read digit presence from the signature at SDXL-matched noise". It was a weak test of the owner's idea. These choices could each have made it miss for reasons other than the idea being wrong.

**The question measured was readability, not additivity.** X5 asks whether "a linear probe on the score path [can] read which digits are present" ([the brief](../prompts/session-10-the-score-path-as-a-third-space.md), line 50; [the design](04-design.md), line 63). The arithmetic tests C1 and C2, the only parts that touch whether sums in one space follow sums in the other, sat behind X5's bar and did not run ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), lines 31 and 32).

**The model cannot express the owner's case.** The digit model `share-0.0` never saw two digits on one canvas ([the design](04-design.md), line 49), and no digit model has a joint label ([the ideas page](03-ideas.md), line 103). The owner's Paper 2 is a model that has seen both concepts and has a joint prompt ([the problem statements](../problem-statements.md), lines 84 to 93). X5's testbed is Paper 1's setting.

**The bar was borrowed from a different kind of test.** 0.75 is the unseen-pair generalisation bar of V2 and W4 ([the brief](../prompts/session-10-the-score-path-as-a-third-space.md), line 118), reused for a feasibility probe (line 117). Its second clause reduces to "at least 0.6" because the raw-noise probe reads 0.5 by construction ([the ideas page](03-ideas.md), line 54), and the signature cleared it by 0.20. The miss on the first clause is 0.05, from one run with no confidence interval ([the executive summary](executive-summary.md), line 182).

**The probe was starved of data.** It fitted on 4,000 canvases for 1,500 updates ([the design](04-design.md), lines 78 and 80). At 1,000 fitting canvases the canvas control read 0.890 and at 4,000 it read 0.950 ([the X5 review](05-signal/review.md), lines 59 and 61), so accuracy was still rising with data. The signature gives the same small probe ten channels where the canvas gives one, which plausibly needs more data still (inferred).

**The reader's ceiling was below 1 on the signature.** At timestep 100, where the canvas keeps 0.946 of its signal, the signature row read 0.904 ([the X5 review](05-signal/review.md), line 61). Measured as the share of the distance from chance to that ceiling, the signature at timestep 480 covers about 0.50 of its range ((0.700 - 0.5) / (0.904 - 0.5)) and the canvas about 0.21 ((0.593 - 0.5) / (0.950 - 0.5)) (inferred, arithmetic on the review's numbers). An absolute bar of 0.75 penalises the input whose own ceiling is lower.

**The window stopped while the curve was rising.** The signature read 0.680 at timestep 495 and 0.700 at 480, the last step read ([the X5 review](05-signal/review.md), line 27). The window ends there because SDXL's step 15 maps to it by matching $\bar\alpha$ ([the ideas page](03-ideas.md), line 50). Matching $\bar\alpha$ between a 32 by 64 pixel canvas and SDXL's 128 by 128 latent does not match how much of the picture is recoverable, because the same noise level destroys less of a larger, more redundant image (inferred; not measured here).

**The input was not a sampler's path.** The canvases were noised forward from known pictures with one noise draw shared across steps, and the review itself flags that the stacked read may only cancel that shared draw ([the X5 review](05-signal/review.md), lines 50 and 77; [the Loop 12 brief](../prompts/session-12-where-co3-leaks.md), line 74). The owner's "score path" is the sequence along a sampling run.

**Spatial detail was pooled away.** The probe read maps pooled 2 by 2 to 16 by 32 ([the design](04-design.md), line 69), while the storage line says 4 by 4 to 8 by 16 (line 55). Which one fed the probe should be checked in `x5_signal.py`; either loses detail on 28-pixel glyphs (inferred).

**Comparisons with Loop 8 may not line up.** The design's own table gives Loop 8's normalised two-map reader 0.704 on "which digits" in the same window, rising from 0.59 to 0.81, and Loop 8's raw reader on the noisy canvas 0.713 ([the design](04-design.md), lines 42 and 43). X5's canvas probe read 0.593 at its best step. If those numbers measure the same thing, X5's probe is the weak link; if they do not, the 0.655 against 0.511 identity comparison in the verdict ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 36) also needs its definitions checked. The files do not settle which.

**The two repairs were sound.** Replacing "$d_t$ under the canvas's own labels" with all ten labels removed a leak of the answer ([the ideas page](03-ideas.md), line 49), and moving from a linear to a convolutional probe on board question 72 was recorded with no bar changed ([the X5 review](05-signal/review.md), line 64). This audit agrees with both.

## Question 4: questions the papers' authors would ask

Ranked by how much the answer could change Paper 2's direction. Neither the owner's prompts nor the session asked any of them in this form.

**1. When does part borrowing commit on CO3?** Readout Guidance applies its identity head over fractions 0.05 to 0.8 of the run, against 0 to 0.5 for spatial control ([Readout Guidance, p. 12 to 13, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.02150#page=12); [its note](../abstract-checkers/01-reading/readout-guidance.md), line 56). The project assumes identity must be fixed in steps 0 to 15 because layout is ([the directions map](../../../ideas/which-directions-to-pursue/README.md), line 24). On CO3 layout is already right in every tuning picture ([the M1 review](../method-search/01-beat-the-baseline/review.md), line 108), and borrowed parts include fur, markings and feather patterns ([the W2d design](../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/design.md), line 18), which look like what the decoded-guess push did move, "eyes, mouths and fur redrawn" ([the checker loop](../../../../2-classifier-guidance/artifacts/drips/rung2-pair-aware-discriminator/checker-loop.md), line 65). If borrowing commits after step 10, readers that read 0.72 to 0.84 at steps 10 to 20 (line 54) become usable, and the "identity cannot be read where it is decided" block disappears for CO3 (inferred).

**2. Does CO3 fail at the target or on the path, and in which space do the two subjects own separate blocks?** Projective Composition separates a wrong target from a correct target that the sampler cannot reach (Theorem 6.1, Lemmas 7.1 and 7.2, [p. 8 to 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04549#page=8)), gives a cheap necessary check, orthogonal mean differences from the background (Lemma 8.1, [p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04549#page=10)), and predicts that concepts in one similarity group fail (Figures 7 and 8, p. 10 to 11). W2d's borrowed counts are a per-pair property: cheetah and cougar 7 of 8, eagle and fox 0 ([the W2d design](../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/design.md), lines 43 to 52). Lemma 8.1's cosine per pair against those counts costs a few U-Net calls and would say whether borrowing is the predicted kind of failure (inferred).

**3. What does the joint prompt alone score on the 12 tuning pairs, and is the rubric measuring composition or style?** M1 has no joint-prompt row ([the M1 review](../method-search/01-beat-the-baseline/review.md), line 99). CO3 composes the joint and single predictions ([CO3, Eq. 14 and 15, p. 5 to 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25940v2#page=5)), so its authors would ask whether CO3 beats its own joint input on these pairs. CO3 alone has 73 of 96 pictures marked "not an intact photograph", most of them because it draws illustrations and "the prompts do not ask for a photograph", against 32 two-clear-subject pictures with style ignored and 11 faithful pairs (lines 99 and 105). A metric where style damage outnumbers composition failure can hide a composition gain (inferred).

**4. Which model can see binding at all?** DiffusionITM's zero-shot SD 2.1 reads colour binding on CLEVR at 84.5% against 53.0% for CLIP RN50x64, and SDXL scored below SD 2.1 on nearly every task with more ties ([DiffusionITM, p. 18, Table 6; p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16397#page=18); [its note](01-reading/diffusion-itm.md), lines 47, 49 and 59). Linear Spaces implies that the additive part of a CLIP sentence embedding cannot carry which noun has which attribute ([its note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/linear-spaces-of-meanings.md), lines 47 to 57, inferred from Lemma 2, p. 3). Paper 2's checker, judge and benchmark all need a reader of binding, and none has been scored ([the W2d review](../noise-text-critic/02d-binding/review.md), line 3).

**5. Is part of the failure the guidance weight?** Dynamic CFG picks the guidance scale per step from a noise-aware evaluator, with no gradient ([Dynamic CFG, Eq. 5 and 6, p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16131#page=5)). Composable Diffusion treats each concept's weight as a temperature ([p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.01714#page=7)), and RRR notes that guidance above 1 does not sample a posterior ([its note](01-reading/reduce-reuse-recycle.md), line 44). On the plain product, the part of the correction inside the experts' own span weights each expert at 1 to 3 where the product uses 7.5 ([what the correction is made of](../../../../1-lora-correction/report/when-does-the-outcome-lock-in/what-is-the-correction-made-of.md), line 10). Whether CO3's borrowing moves with its guidance or its $\beta$ per subject is unmeasured; the choosing test varies $\beta$ jointly for both subjects ([the choosing test](11-the-checker-chooses-on-co3.md), line 65).

**6. Were the readers trained on the states the sampler actually visits?** Readout Guidance trains on forward-noised images ([p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.02150#page=4)), and Dynamic CFG found its reward evaluators useful only in the least noisy third of the run ([p. 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16131#page=15)). The project's readout checker trained with half its batches from saved sampler states rose from 0.51 to 0.59 at step 0 ([the checker loop](../../../../2-classifier-guidance/artifacts/drips/rung2-pair-aware-discriminator/checker-loop.md), line 183), and X5 read forward-noised canvases only. A reader trained off the sampler's path may be judged null for that reason (inferred).

## Question 6: the most promising direction for Paper 2, and its cheapest decisive test

**The direction.** On CO3, once its corrected steps have placed two bodies, let each subject's prediction act mainly inside its own region, with the regions read from each subject's own signed score difference $d_t(A)$ and $d_t(B)$ on CO3's trajectory. It trains no diffusion model, runs one trajectory per seed, and uses no selection.

**Why this one.**

- It is the owner's shared-representation idea in the form that survives the theory: a space where each subject owns its own block, for which Theorem 6.1 makes the summed score correct and Lemma 7.1 covers sampling ([Projective Composition, p. 8 to 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04549#page=8)). Concept Algebra already computes masks from score differences ([its note](../noise-text-critic/01-reading/concept-algebra.md), line 31, p. 17).
- Regions decide species on SDXL with oracle boxes: right boxes drew 8 clear pairs of 8, and the same prompt in both regions drew two cats on all 8 seeds ([the Loop 6 page](02-the-loops/loop-6-find-the-best-method.md), lines 193 to 197 and 203). Those pictures were damaged by the bootstrapping over flat grey (line 199), which CO3's own layout would replace (inferred).
- M9 rung 2, the CO3 version, never rendered because its region reader, word attention at step 10, failed 11 of 24 reads against a limit of 6 (line 201). Board question 65 asks for a new region reader.
- It is the SDXL form of Loop 10's one positive finding: all ten signed maps read the asked pair against a wrong digit at 0.655 where two normalised maps read 0.511 ([the verdict](09-verdict.md), line 36), and the verdict's own Paper 2 suggestion is D3 (line 54). The digit number is weak support and transfers only as a direction (inferred).

**Against it.** Bounded Attention and regional prompting are rivals, so the contribution has to be the regions coming from the model's own score path on CO3's trajectory (inferred). If borrowing commits before CO3's step 10 (Question 4, item 1), regions from step 10 come too late.

**The cheapest decisive test.** M9's own mask check, rerun with the new reader, before any render.

1. Run CO3 at the released settings to step 10 on the 12 tuning pairs at seeds 6001 and 6002, the same 24 reads M9 used ([the M9 design](../method-search/04-bind-each-body/design.md), lines 90 to 92). CO3 is deterministic given its starting noise ([the choosing test](11-the-checker-chooses-on-co3.md), line 65), so these are the trajectories of M1's finished pictures.
2. At step 10, compute $d_{10}(A)$ and $d_{10}(B)$ under each subject's single prompt (two extra U-Net calls per read), take per-cell squared size, smooth, and assign each cell to the larger, giving two masks that sum to 1 (inferred design).
3. Apply M9's written rule unchanged: a read fails if either region covers at least 80% of the grid or the two overlap with intersection over union of at least 0.8; more than 6 failures of 24 stops the route.
4. Check by eye at full size that the region for subject A sits on the body that becomes A in M1's finished CO3 picture.

**Cost:** 24 CO3 runs to step 10 plus 48 U-Net calls, about a fifth of 24 full CO3 renders at 38.4 to 43.2 seconds each ([the M1 design](../method-search/01-beat-the-baseline/design.md), line 147; [the M1 review](../method-search/01-beat-the-baseline/review.md), line 47), so under 0.1 GPU-hours (inferred). **Buys:** whether the score path can supply the regions that word attention could not. A pass unblocks M9 rung 2 with its written bar (own masks lead CO3 alone by at least 5 of 96 and by more than 1.96 standard errors, random masks do not; [the M9 design](../method-search/04-bind-each-body/design.md), lines 113 to 115), which can start on the six pairs that borrow (cheetah and cougar, duck and goose, puma and condor, boar and pheasant, bobcat and quail, seal and gull) at about 2 GPU-hours (inferred). A fail closes the score-path region reader with a number, and leaves the owner the oracle-box and Bounded Attention routes.
