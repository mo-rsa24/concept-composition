# Which design issues in Loops 8, 9 and 10 could change a verdict, and which reruns would test them (step Z5)

This audit goes back over the designs of Loops 8, 9 and 10 and lists every design issue found, with whether fixing it could change the verdict of the step it sits in and what a rerun would cost. It ran nothing and changed no other file. Citations are `file:line`, with paths from `artifacts/notes/progress/` unless they start with `2-classifier-guidance/`. "The brief" is `prompts/session-12-where-co3-leaks.md`. Anything marked (inferred) is this audit's own reasoning.

The short answer: 30 issues, 13 in Loop 8, 9 in Loop 9 and 8 in Loop 10. Three could change a verdict outright and 17 possibly could; several of the 17 bear on steps not yet run (V7, V4 and the choosing test) or on how far a verdict reaches beyond its own data. Four reruns are proposed, about 1.4 GPU-hours in all.

## Words this uses

**Share of picture.** How much of a noisy input is the clean picture. The project's files use two measures of it, and they differ by a square root. The **variance share** is $\bar\alpha_t$, the fraction of the noisy input's variance that comes from the picture; the brief's list for SDXL (0.6% at step 0) uses it. The **amplitude share** is $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$, the factor on the clean picture in $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$; Loop 8's and Loop 10's "8.7% of its signal" uses it (`score-space/04-design.md:29`).

**Forward-noised.** A finished or pasted picture with noise added to reach a step's noise level. A state the sampler actually visits at that step can differ from it.

**Two maps.** Loop 8's original checker input: for each asked digit, $\lvert\epsilon_a - \epsilon_\varnothing\rvert$, scaled to sum to 1 and then multiplied by the canvas's 2,048 pixels (`abstract-checkers/03-design.md:67`; `abstract-checkers/05-does-it-learn/design.md:32`).

**Ten-label signature.** All ten digit labels' differences $d_t(c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)$ on one noisy canvas, signed and unscaled (`score-space/09-verdict.md:18`). V6's version keeps the asked pair's two and summarises the other eight by their mean and largest value (`abstract-checkers/10-reading-identity/design.md:13`).

**Balanced accuracy.** The mean of the share of label-1 and the share of label-0 inputs called correctly, from 0 to 1, where 0.5 is chance. Every bar of 0.75 below is on this scale.

**Fixed-length push.** A gradient scaled to a set fraction $b$ of the noise prediction's length and added at every pushed step, whatever the checker's reading already is.

**Chained scoring.** Scoring each property only on the canvases that passed the property before it.

**Two clear, two bodies.** Calls on Loop 6's blind rubric: `two_clear` is exactly one clear A and one clear B with no borrowed features; `two_bodies` is two bodies that fail that, for example one borrowing the other's face or parts (`method-search/01-beat-the-baseline/blind-count-rubric.md:18-19`).

**Small or fundamental.** Small: a fix inside the step's own design. Fundamental: the question the step asks cannot be answered by that design, so the question has to change.

**Verdict at risk.** Yes, possibly or no: whether fixing the issue could change the verdict the step recorded.

## Loop 8

Loop 8's verdicts rest on pasted MNIST digits noised forward, read and pushed on one digit model that never saw two digits on one canvas. Count is learned and its push works (V2 and V3), identity reads once the checker sees the ten-label signature in the window matching SDXL's steps 16 to 31 (V6), and "one checker controls one property" is null (V5). The verdict most at risk is V5's null, through its old right-digits checker and its chained scoring (rows 1 and 9). V7 is designed to test both, and rows 8 and 11 are two cheap checks that make V7's answer easier to read.

## Loop 9

## The reruns worth making

Four, about 1.4 GPU-hours in all: 0.04 measured and about 1.4 estimated. Each is small and each comes before a larger step that is already planned.

1. **Where the digit sampler settles which digits it draws.** Loop 8's V7 will push the identity checker at the digit sampler's steps 27 to 36, the steps where it reads well. If the sampler has already settled which digits it draws before step 27, the push arrives too late, and V7 could fail for timing alone. The rerun judges the predicted clean canvas at every step for the 320 plain and 320 pushed canvases of V3 and names the step after which the judge's call stops changing. If job 94161's record holds the per-step canvases (not confirmed here), it needs no GPU; otherwise it repeats that job. **Cost:** 0.04 GPU-hours, measured for job 94161 (`abstract-checkers/06-does-it-compose/review.md:78`). Run it before V7.

2. **A fair untrained rule against V6's identity checker.** V6's checker reads which digits are present at 0.861 on digits it never trained on. The baseline already queued asks whether the frozen digit model gets there with no training, using the noise that was actually added, which a sampler never has. Adding a rule that uses only the eleven predictions the checker itself sees says whether the training adds anything on equal information. **Cost:** about 0.1 GPU-hours, estimate; it reuses the signatures of job 94596 (`abstract-checkers/10-reading-identity/design.md:82`). Run it before V7.

3. **Whether the readout checker can see CO3's failure at the steps where it would choose.** The choosing test (board question 80, about 3.1 GPU-hours for its first read) lets the checker pick CO3's $\beta$ at steps 0 to 5. If the checker cannot tell CO3's 32 two-clear pictures from its 64 two-bodies pictures there, the test cannot succeed. The rerun re-runs CO3 on M1's 96 tuning seeds and reads the checker at steps 0 to 30. Loop 6's reviewer asked for the same read before Loop 6's checker search, so it is counted once here. **Cost:** about 1.2 GPU-hours, estimate from 43.2 s per CO3 picture, measured (`reviewer/2026-11-24-review.md:105`). 4. **The block-wise form of Loop 10's shared-space idea, on SDXL.** Loop 10 closed the score path after a digit test that could not ask the owner's question. The form its verdict keeps open is a space where each subject owns its own region. The rerun reads each subject's signed $d_t$ on CO3's own path, turns the two into two masks, and applies M9's written rule: a read fails when either mask covers at least 80% of the grid or the two overlap with intersection over union of at least 0.8, and more than 6 failures in 24 reads stops the route. It is a Z4 candidate already (`leakage/04-paper-2-method.md:20`). Run it after Z2 reports.
