# Pairs and their regimes

**Scope:** the 15 requested pairs, plus rheumatoid pneumoconiosis, searched through 4 November 2026.

**Our classification rules:** the four regimes belong to this project, and every assignment below is our mapping of published evidence rather than a classification used by the cited authors.

- **1 — Separate:** separately identifiable manifestations in different regions.
- **2 — Overlapping but readable:** both components identifiable in the same region.
- **3 — Merged:** a jointly produced appearance that cannot be decomposed into the two diseases.
- **4 — Concealed:** independently established disease that cannot be identified on the image.

**Our qualification:** regime 1 additionally requires evidence that appearances remain unchanged, regime 3 requires evidence that neither disease produces the appearance alone, and the original regime 4 requires structural concealment rather than merely a diagnostic mistake.

**Evidence labels:** **documented** identifies what a caption or study directly establishes; **inferred** identifies our interpretation; **candidate** identifies evidence that falls short of a defining condition; and **not documented** means not established in the sources examined, not proved absent from the literature.

**Quotation convention:** quotation marks contain exact excerpts, sometimes shorter than the complete caption or sentence, with a locator linking to the surrounding passage.

## 1. Silicosis + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Silicosis produces numerous small rounded white spots, usually most prominent in the upper lungs, which can join into large masses in complicated disease. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)
- Post-primary pulmonary tuberculosis commonly produces upper-lung patches of opacity and cavities, although this is not its only radiographic presentation. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Post-primary TB”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

Tuberculosis can alter the distribution and shape of silicotic abnormalities, contribute to conglomerate lesions, and make underlying silicosis difficult to recognise. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, radiological diagnosis section and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Documented spatial separation on radiograph:** the Case 2 caption identifies “large opacities (B) in the left upper zone” and a “cavity (cv) in right upper zone,” although unchanged appearance relative to either disease alone is not demonstrated. [Pyana Kitenge et al., 2025, *IJTLD Open*, Figure, Case 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11827670/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Inferred from CT and microbiology:** “the left conglomerate mass presented with a cavitation” places a cavity inside progressive massive fibrosis in a culture-positive case, but the authors acknowledge that ischaemic necrosis can also cavitate a silicotic mass. [Martins et al., 2010, *Case Reports in Medicine*, Figure 2 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2929613/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Prose-supported candidate:** the statement that tuberculosis “contributes to conglomeration of discrete silicotic nodules” supports joint lesion formation, but does not establish a radiographic appearance impossible in silicosis alone. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, discussion of conglomerate lesions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Inferred from prose:** “silicosis may be missed” in tuberculous abnormalities supports concealment, but the review does not supply an independently verified before-and-after figure demonstrating that mechanism. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, radiological diagnosis section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/) |

**Reader error**

The Case 2 report describes previously unrecognised probable silicosis in **1/1 illustrated silico-TB case**, with tuberculosis established by GeneXpert and silicosis inferred from occupational exposure and imaging rather than biopsy. [Pyana Kitenge et al., 2025, *IJTLD Open*, Case 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11827670/)

Our reading is that these sources support several mechanisms within the pair, but do not validate all four strict regimes with independently confirmed figures. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, Table 1 and radiological diagnosis section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)

## 2. HIV + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- In the principal study, HIV was established by antibody testing rather than by a separately identifiable chest opacity, making this a systemic modifier–lung disease pair in our classification. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Methods: “HIV testing/CD4 analysis”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/)
- The familiar adult post-primary tuberculosis appearance includes upper-lung opacities and cavities. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Post-primary TB”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

**Yes: the central claim is supported**, because decreasing CD4 count was associated with fewer cavities and upper-lung abnormalities and with more normal radiographs, lymph-node enlargement, miliary disease and pleural effusions. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Results: “Influence of level of immune suppression on chest X-ray findings,” Figures 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/)

Among culture-confirmed pulmonary tuberculosis cases, normal radiographs occurred in **52/323** HIV-positive patients with CD4 ≤150 cells/µL, **19/525** with CD4 >150 cells/µL, and **26/1,085** HIV-negative patients. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/)

The authors describe the relationship as “significant, continuous trends,” rather than an abrupt switch at one CD4 threshold. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Abstract: Conclusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented:** the study does not identify a separate HIV lesion and tuberculosis lesion. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented:** its radiographic categories describe tuberculosis presentation, not two independently readable tissue components. [Kisembo et al., 2012, *British Journal of Radiology*, Tables 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3474111/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** altered distributions and reduced cavitation do not establish a novel, inseparable lesion unique to the pair. [Kisembo et al., 2012, *British Journal of Radiology*, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3474111/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Nonvisibility documented; expanded regime 4 inferred by us:** Figure 4 and Table 3 establish culture-positive tuberculosis with normal radiographs, but neither a visible HIV lesion nor structural burial is required. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Figure 4 and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/) |

**Reader error and disagreement between studies**

A second study found cavities in **25/186** HIV-positive versus **13/27** HIV-negative tuberculosis cases. [Kisembo et al., 2012, *British Journal of Radiology*, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3474111/)

However, its normal-radiograph frequencies were **14/102** at CD4 ≤50 versus **8/80** at CD4 >50 cells/µL, a nonsignificant difference (*p*=0.44), so it did not reproduce a significant normal-film contrast using that division. [Kisembo et al., 2012, *British Journal of Radiology*, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3474111/)

Our interpretation is that a normal film in confirmed disease measures radiographic nonvisibility, not necessarily reader error, and that immune-associated non-expression is an extension of the original structurally defined fourth regime. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Methods and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3033769/)

## 3. Pneumonia + emphysema

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Pneumonia can produce a white patch or a more extensive white region, sometimes containing visible dark, air-filled bronchi. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, “Consolidation-predominant pattern”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/)
- Emphysema can produce overexpanded lungs, flattened diaphragms, sparse vessels and large dark airspaces called bullae. [Wise, updated 2026, *Merck Manual Professional Edition*, “Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease,” Imaging tests](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-and-related-disorders/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-copd)

**What changes together**

Pneumonic opacity can surround pre-existing emphysematous airspaces, creating apparent cavities or a honeycomb-like pattern rather than uniform consolidation. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, Figures 21–22 and accompanying discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented in the selected figures:** Figures 21–22 illustrate pneumonia within emphysematous lung rather than an unaffected, spatially separate pair. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, Figures 21–22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Documented on CT:** Figure 21 identifies consolidation containing small airspaces corresponding to pre-existing low-attenuation areas, supporting our regional-overlap assignment. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, Figure 21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Strong radiograph candidate, not strict proof:** the caption states, “The reticular or reticulo-nodular opacities in both lower lungs on chest radiograph correspond to pneumonic consolidation with underlying emphysema on CT.” [Park et al., 2018, *Journal of the Korean Society of Radiology*, Figure 2A–B](https://jksronline.org/search.php?code=2016JKSR&id=10.3348%2Fjksr.2018.79.3.139&vmode=PUBREADER&where=aview) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented strictly:** the illustrated diagnostic problem is distorted appearance and mimicry, not independently confirmed pneumonia rendered invisible by emphysema. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, Figures 21–22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/) |

Our interpretation is that Park’s Figure 2 directly documents altered combined morphology on a **plain radiograph**, but does not establish that neither disease alone could generate a similar pattern or that readers cannot decompose it. [Park et al., 2018, *Journal of the Korean Society of Radiology*, Figure 2A–B](https://jksronline.org/search.php?code=2016JKSR&id=10.3348%2Fjksr.2018.79.3.139&vmode=PUBREADER&where=aview)

**Reader error**

The review labels an illustrated CT appearance “Pseudohoneycombing,” but provides no reader-error numerator or denominator for that example. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, Figure 22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/)

The Park evidence above comes from its published English caption, not from a translation of the Korean clinical prose. [Park et al., 2018, *Journal of the Korean Society of Radiology*, Figure 2](https://jksronline.org/search.php?code=2016JKSR&id=10.3348%2Fjksr.2018.79.3.139&vmode=PUBREADER&where=aview)

## 4. Pleural effusion + atelectasis

**Each condition on a chest radiograph alone**

- Pleural fluid can blunt the lower outer corner of the chest and form a curved upper border above a white basal opacity. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Chapter 5, “Pleural effusion,” Figure 5-137](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/)
- Atelectasis makes part of the lung whiter and smaller, potentially drawing fissures, the diaphragm or mediastinal structures toward the collapsed region. [Khan et al., 2009, *Annals of Thoracic Medicine*, “Atelectasis”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714572/)

**What changes together**

Pleural fluid can compress adjacent lung and produce atelectasis, making this a cause-and-consequence combination rather than necessarily two independent diseases. [Bankier et al., 2024, *Radiology*, “Atelectasis,” Figures 30–40](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10902601/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented in the causal example:** the effusion and dependent collapse are adjacent and mechanically related. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Figure 5-152](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Documented adjacent components on CT; regional assignment inferred:** the caption attributes denser dependent lung to “compressive atelectasis from the adjacent pleural effusions,” but the two abnormalities occupy different anatomical compartments rather than identical tissue. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Figure 5-152A–B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** an opaque hemithorax does not establish an appearance unique to effusion plus collapse. [Herring, 2012 copyright edition, *Learning Radiology*, 2nd edition, Chapter 6, “Opacified Hemithorax”](https://elsevier-elibrary.com/contents/fullcontent/58848/epubcontent_v2/OEBPS/B9780323074445000068.htm) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Prose-supported candidate on radiography:** large effusions can “mask whatever disease may be present in the lung enveloped by them,” but this general statement does not independently prove concealed atelectasis in a particular illustrated patient. [Herring, 2012 copyright edition, *Learning Radiology*, 2nd edition, Chapter 6, “Opacified Hemithorax,” Figure 6-10](https://elsevier-elibrary.com/contents/fullcontent/58848/epubcontent_v2/OEBPS/B9780323074445000068.htm) |

**Reader error**

No reader-error rate is reported for the illustrated effusion–atelectasis combination. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Figures 5-139–5-140 and 5-152](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/)

## 5. Pulmonary fibrosis + emphysema

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Pulmonary fibrosis can produce a network of white lines, especially near the bases and outer lung, with reduced lung volume. [Meltzer and Noble, 2008, *Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases*, “Radiographic findings,” Figure 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1750-1172-3-8)
- Emphysema can produce overexpanded lungs, flattened diaphragms and dark bullous spaces. [Wise, updated 2026, *Merck Manual Professional Edition*, Imaging tests](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-and-related-disorders/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-copd)

**What changes together**

Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema, or CPFE, can appear on radiographs as unusually dark upper lungs together with a basal and peripheral network of white lines. [Dias et al., 2014, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, “Imaging studies in CPFE”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4109203/)

Where the processes overlap, fibrosis can deform emphysematous spaces and create thick-walled cysts that complicate distinction from honeycombing. [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Imaging section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Documented on CT:** Figure 1 explicitly names a “separate emphysema and fibrosis pattern,” with upper-lobe emphysema and lower-lobe fibrosis, although complete absence of interaction is not tested. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Documented on serial CT:** the Figure 4 caption says isolated emphysema “becomes admixed with fibrosis over time,” while identifying both components over a two-year interval. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 4A–D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Strong candidate:** Figure 6 names “thick-walled large cysts,” but the imaging discussion explicitly leaves their specificity for CPFE unresolved. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 6 and Imaging section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented strictly:** difficulty distinguishing emphysema from honeycombing does not itself demonstrate one independently confirmed disease made invisible by the other. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, “Quantification of HRCT abnormalities”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) |

**Reader error**

The statement reports difficulty and interobserver variation in identifying and quantifying overlapping components, but supplies no single reader-error denominator applicable to these figures. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, “Quantification of HRCT abnormalities”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/)

**Qualification within the source:** its histopathology discussion describes certain thick-walled cysts as distinctive, whereas its imaging discussion remains uncertain about their specificity, so those passages do not justify an unqualified claim of radiographic uniqueness. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Imaging and Histopathology sections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/)

## 6. Pulmonary fibrosis + lung cancer

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Fibrosis produces coarse or fine net-like white markings and may contract the lungs. [Meltzer and Noble, 2008, *Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases*, “Radiographic findings”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1750-1172-3-8)
- Lung cancer may appear as a focal nodule or mass, or indirectly through obstruction and collapse. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Chapter 5, discussion of pulmonary masses and postobstructive abnormalities](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/)

**What changes together**

In a CT series containing **66 cancers in 63 patients** with idiopathic interstitial pneumonia, **35/66** cancers lay at the interface between fibrotic cysts and normal lung and **21/66** lay within fibrotic cystic regions. [Oh et al., 2015, *American Journal of Roentgenology*, Abstract: Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26001227/)

Cancer in fibrotic lung can resemble consolidation rather than a conspicuous separate mass. [Abu Qubo et al., published online 2023, *Breathe*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9973524/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented strictly in the selected figures:** peripheral tumour location alone does not establish spatial separation from fibrosis and unchanged appearance. [Abu Qubo et al., 2023, *Breathe*, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9973524/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Inferred from series prose:** cancers occurred “in the midst of fibrotic cysts,” establishing shared regions, although the accessible abstract provides no corresponding caption proving both components are independently readable. [Oh et al., 2015, *AJR*, Abstract: Conclusion](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26001227/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented strictly:** “The cancer looks like consolidation” establishes mimicry, not an appearance that neither cancer nor fibrosis can produce alone. [Abu Qubo et al., 2023, *Breathe*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9973524/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Documented diagnostic miss; regime assignment inferred:** a nodule at the diseased–normal lung boundary “was not reported on the first CT scan,” but the caption attributes the problem to satisfaction of search rather than proving structural burial. [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, Figure 15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/) |

**Reader error**

Figure 15 documents a missed cancer in **1/1 illustrated patient**, with enlargement on CT **five months later** and subsequent histological confirmation. [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, Figure 15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/)

The series of **66 cancers in 63 patients**, assessed across **193 CT examinations**, reported a median diagnostic delay of **46 days**, ranging from **8 to 760 days**, and interobserver agreement exceeding **κ=0.77** for the evaluated tumour characteristics. [Oh et al., 2015, *AJR*, Abstract: Methods and Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26001227/)

Our qualification is that the accessible abstract does not give a separate denominator for the delay calculation, so the cohort denominator should not be mistaken for a verified count of delayed diagnoses. [Oh et al., 2015, *AJR*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26001227/)

## 7. Old tuberculosis + aspergilloma

**Each condition on a chest radiograph alone**

- Healed pulmonary tuberculosis can leave scars, volume loss and persistent cavities after the active infection has resolved. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Imaging of post-treatment sequelae”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)
- An aspergilloma is a fungal ball within a pre-existing cavity, potentially appearing as a rounded opacity with surrounding air, so its “alone” appearance already presupposes a structural substrate. [Maheshwari et al., 2011, *BMJ Case Reports*, Background and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3094787/)

**What changes together**

One report shows an empty post-tuberculous cavity on radiography and a mass inside the same cavity on CT one year later, with Aspergillus established by tissue examination and culture. [Ortiz-Brizuela and Ponce-de-León, 2018, *CMAJ*, case description and Figure 1A–D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167218/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented in this example:** the fungal lesion occupies the pre-existing cavity rather than a different region. [Ortiz-Brizuela and Ponce-de-León, 2018, *CMAJ*, Figure 1A–B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167218/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Documented on CT:** the caption identifies the same cavity “with an irregular spongiform mass suggestive of a forming aspergilloma.” [Ortiz-Brizuela and Ponce-de-León, 2018, *CMAJ*, Figure 1B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167218/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** the caption explicitly separates the cavity from the intracavitary mass. [Ortiz-Brizuela and Ponce-de-León, 2018, *CMAJ*, Figure 1B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167218/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented strictly:** the fungal lesion was identifiable on CT and independently confirmed, rather than shown to be structurally hidden. [Ortiz-Brizuela and Ponce-de-León, 2018, *CMAJ*, Figure 1B–D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167218/) |

**Reader error**

A separate report describes presumed tuberculosis reactivation before CT and histology established aspergilloma in **1/1 reported patient**, but does not provide a blinded radiologist-error rate. [Maheshwari et al., 2011, *BMJ Case Reports*, Case presentation and Investigations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3094787/)

Our qualification is that this pair concerns a persistent tuberculosis sequela plus fungal disease, not necessarily two simultaneously active infections. [Ortiz-Brizuela and Ponce-de-León, 2018, *CMAJ*, case description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167218/)

## 8. Heart failure + pneumonia

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Heart failure with pulmonary oedema can produce enlarged vessels, short peripheral lines, central white airspace opacities, pleural effusions and an enlarged heart. [Khan et al., 2009, *Annals of Thoracic Medicine*, “Pulmonary edema”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714572/)
- Pneumonia can produce patchy or confluent white airspace opacities, sometimes with air bronchograms. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, “Consolidation-predominant pattern”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/)

**What changes together**

The BACH study identifies diagnosing pneumonia during acute heart failure as difficult because the clinical presentation and chest-radiograph abnormalities can overlap. [Maisel et al., 2012, *European Journal of Heart Failure*, Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284113/)

Of **155 patients with pneumonia**, **29 also had acute heart failure** in the study’s **1,641-patient** dyspnoea cohort. [Maisel et al., 2012, *European Journal of Heart Failure*, Results: study population](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284113/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented:** BACH establishes coexistence but does not provide an annotated figure separating the two abnormalities. [Maisel et al., 2012, *European Journal of Heart Failure*, Results and figures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284113/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented:** the study does not demonstrate independently readable oedema and infection within one labelled region. [Maisel et al., 2012, *European Journal of Heart Failure*, Results and figures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284113/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** shared airspace opacity is not evidence of a new morphology unique to the combined diseases. [Khan et al., 2009, *Annals of Thoracic Medicine*, “Pneumonia”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714572/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented strictly:** diagnostic uncertainty in concurrent heart failure does not establish that oedema physically concealed independently confirmed pneumonia on a particular film. [Maisel et al., 2012, *European Journal of Heart Failure*, Introduction and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284113/) |

**Reader error**

No pneumonia-specific radiographic miss fraction for the **29 concurrent heart-failure cases** is provided, so the study’s overall diagnostic-performance estimates should not be presented as a pair-specific error rate. [Maisel et al., 2012, *European Journal of Heart Failure*, Results: chest radiography and diagnostic performance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3284113/)

## 9. COPD + pneumonia

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- COPD includes airway-predominant and emphysematous disease, with possible radiographic hyperinflation and bullae, but confirmation of airflow obstruction depends on pulmonary function testing. [Wise, updated 2026, *Merck Manual Professional Edition*, “Diagnosis of COPD” and Imaging tests](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-and-related-disorders/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-copd)
- Pneumonia commonly produces a new focal or patchy white opacity. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, radiological patterns of community-acquired pneumonia](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/)

**What changes together**

Pneumonia and COPD exacerbation can have similar clinical presentations, and radiographic consolidation may be present without a discharge diagnosis of pneumonia. [Finney et al., 2019, *ERJ Open Research*, Abstract and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6589445/)

Among **100 selected patients** admitted with COPD exacerbation whose radiographs had excluded pneumonia, **24/100** had additional low-dose CT abnormalities considered suggestive of pneumonia. [Prins et al., 2022, *ERJ Open Research*, Results and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209851/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented strictly:** an illustrated pneumococcal pneumonia in a patient with COPD does not establish a second, separately localised COPD lesion. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Figure 5-71](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Documented for the emphysema phenotype, not COPD generally:** the CT example in section 3 identifies pneumonic consolidation and pre-existing emphysematous spaces within the same region. [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, Figure 21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Candidate only for the emphysema phenotype:** the caption quoted in section 3 connects radiographic reticular opacities to pneumonia with underlying emphysema, without establishing a unique COPD–pneumonia morphology. [Park et al., 2018, *Journal of the Korean Society of Radiology*, Figure 2](https://jksronline.org/search.php?code=2016JKSR&id=10.3348%2Fjksr.2018.79.3.139&vmode=PUBREADER&where=aview) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Modality-dependent nonvisibility documented; structural regime 4 unproved:** Figure 2 contrasts a “Conventional chest radiograph without evidence of radiological abnormalities” with CT showing right-upper-lobe consolidation. [Prins et al., 2022, *ERJ Open Research*, Figure 2A–B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209851/) |

**Reader error**

Across **941 admissions in 621 patients**, **235 admissions** had radiographic consolidation, but only **42.9% of those 235 admissions** received a discharge diagnosis of pneumonia, which measures diagnostic coding discordance rather than a blinded reader miss rate. [Finney et al., 2019, *ERJ Open Research*, Abstract: Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31249841/)

For the **100 low-dose CT examinations**, agreement between two radiologists was **κ=0.562**, with a **95% confidence interval of 0.371–0.752**. [Prins et al., 2022, *ERJ Open Research*, “Interobserver variation”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209851/)

Our qualification is that CT abnormalities suggestive of pneumonia do not independently establish infectious pneumonia in every one of the **24/100** patients or prove that COPD caused the radiographic nonvisibility. [Prins et al., 2022, *ERJ Open Research*, Tables 2–3 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9209851/)

## 10. Diabetes + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- In the principal study, diabetes was defined clinically and biochemically rather than through a chest-radiograph lesion, making it another systemic modifier in our classification. [Chiang et al., 2014, *PLOS ONE*, Methods: definitions of diabetes and glycaemic control](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974751/)
- Post-primary pulmonary tuberculosis commonly produces upper-lung opacity and cavitation. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Post-primary TB”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

A study of **1,209 culture-positive patients**, comprising **581 with diabetes** and **628 without**, associated diabetes and poorer glycaemic control with more extensive disease, lower-field involvement and cavitation. [Chiang et al., 2014, *PLOS ONE*, Results and radiographic comparison tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974751/)

Another study reported isolated lower-field disease in **23.5% of 187 diabetic patients** versus **2.4% of 505 non-diabetic patients**, with cavitation in **50.8%** and **39.0%** of those respective groups. [Shaikh et al., 2003, *Saudi Medical Journal*, Abstract: Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12704504/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented and not directly applicable:** diabetes was not represented by its own spatially localised radiographic abnormality. [Chiang et al., 2014, *PLOS ONE*, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974751/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented:** the study compares tuberculosis appearances between metabolic groups rather than two readable lung lesions. [Chiang et al., 2014, *PLOS ONE*, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974751/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** altered frequency and distribution of tuberculosis findings do not establish a unique joint lesion. [Shaikh et al., 2003, *Saudi Medical Journal*, Abstract: Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12704504/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented:** these studies do not demonstrate diabetes structurally concealing independently confirmed tuberculosis. [Chiang et al., 2014, *PLOS ONE*, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974751/) |

**Reader error and disagreement**

No reader-error rate or diagnostic-delay denominator was identified in the selected radiographic comparison studies. [Chiang et al., 2014, *PLOS ONE*, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974751/)

The broader review literature reports inconsistent findings about whether diabetes changes tuberculosis location, so the lower-field association should not be stated as universal. [Dooley and Chaisson, 2009, *Lancet Infectious Diseases*, discussion of clinical presentation and radiographic findings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2945809/)

## 11. Silicosis + lung cancer

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Silicosis produces small rounded opacities and, in complicated disease, large conglomerate masses. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)
- Lung cancer may produce a focal nodule or mass, although obstruction can produce secondary opacity or collapse. [Broder, 2011, *Diagnostic Imaging for the Emergency Physician*, Chapter 5, pulmonary masses discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8139021/)

**What changes together**

A reported case had a recognised dominant lung cancer but diffuse nodules initially interpreted as silicosis, with surgery demonstrating additional adenocarcinoma deposits. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Case presentation, Figures 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/)

After three months of targeted treatment, some nodules regressed while others remained unchanged and were considered silicotic, so the evidence for the two components was unequal: sampled cancer was histologically established, whereas the remaining silicotic nodules were inferred. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Case presentation and Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Candidate, not confirmed:** the prose describes “a left upper lung nodule with bilateral silicosis,” but the later discovery of metastases prevents treating all background nodules as independently verified silicosis. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Abstract and Case presentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented:** the captions do not establish a single region in which silicotic tissue and tumour are both separately identifiable. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Figures 1–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** similar-looking silicotic and metastatic nodules do not establish a jointly produced new morphology. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Diagnostic-masking candidate:** the authors conclude that “metastasis and silicosis coexisted,” but this is misattribution of visible nodules rather than demonstrated physical burial. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/) |

**Reader error**

Preoperative assessment failed to identify the full metastatic extent in **1/1 reported patient**, although the dominant cancer itself was recognised. [Wang et al., 2023, *Cell Cycle*, Case presentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732644/)

## 12. Asbestosis + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Asbestosis can produce basal irregular interstitial opacities and indistinct cardiac or diaphragmatic borders, while pleural plaques are a separate asbestos-associated finding rather than proof of asbestosis. [ATSDR, publication year not confirmed, *Asbestos Toxicity: Clinical Assessment—Tests*, chest-radiograph findings table](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_atsdr_cdc_gov/csem/asbestos/clinical_assessment-tests.html)
- Pulmonary tuberculosis can produce upper-lung opacities, cavities or residual scarring. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, post-primary disease and sequelae sections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

An occupational study examined **2,846 radiographs**, including **257 people classified with asbestosis**, but principally compared residual tuberculosis appearances and found only **one active tuberculosis case in the whole cohort**. [Segarra-Obiol et al., 1983, *American Journal of Industrial Medicine*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6606358/)

A later cohort reported previous pulmonary tuberculosis in **45/124 patients with asbestosis**, which establishes a history of both conditions but not the appearance of simultaneous active disease. [Tse et al., 2012, *Public Health*, Abstract: Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23164612/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented:** the occupational study’s accessible report does not supply a labelled combined-case figure. [Segarra-Obiol et al., 1983, *American Journal of Industrial Medicine*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6606358/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented:** historical coexistence in the later cohort does not localise both processes within a shared region. [Tse et al., 2012, *Public Health*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23164612/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** neither a joint morphology nor an inseparable lesion is established in the accessible occupational study. [Segarra-Obiol et al., 1983, *American Journal of Industrial Medicine*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6606358/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented:** the retrieved cohort evidence does not establish one independently confirmed disease hidden by the other. [Tse et al., 2012, *Public Health*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23164612/) |

**Reader error**

No combined-case reader-error rate was available in the retrieved occupational study. [Segarra-Obiol et al., 1983, *American Journal of Industrial Medicine*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6606358/)

A related illustrated report concerns **asbestos-related pleural disease with tuberculous pleurisy**, not proven parenchymal asbestosis, so it should not be substituted for this pair. [Zantah and Datta, 2017, *Respiratory Medicine Case Reports*, case description and Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5413209/)

## 13. Coal workers’ pneumoconiosis + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Coal workers’ pneumoconiosis can produce small pulmonary opacities and, when complicated, large masses of progressive massive fibrosis. [Shah et al., 2012, *RadioGraphics*, “AIRP Best Cases in Radiologic-Pathologic Correlation: Coal Workers’ Pneumoconiosis,” radiological discussion](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/rg.327115137)
- Post-primary tuberculosis commonly produces upper-lung opacities and cavities. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Post-primary TB”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

The classical cavitation study distinguishes tuberculosis-associated cavitation from non-tuberculous cavitation within massive pneumoconiotic lesions, showing why a cavity does not uniquely identify the infection. [Kilpatrick, Heppleston and Fletcher, 1954, *Thorax*, pp. 260–272](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/thoraxjnl/9/4/260.full.pdf)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented in the selected cavitation study:** its main question concerns cavities within massive fibrosis rather than separately labelled disease territories. [Kilpatrick et al., 1954, *Thorax*, Introduction and radiological examination section](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/thoraxjnl/9/4/260.full.pdf) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Candidate only:** a mass and cavity can both be visible, but recognising those structures does not establish that tuberculosis itself is separately readable. [Kilpatrick et al., 1954, *Thorax*, radiological examination section](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/thoraxjnl/9/4/260.full.pdf) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented strictly:** the authors state, “The two main types of cavity in P.M.F. cannot be differentiated radiologically,” undermining any claim that the combined appearance is unique to tuberculosis. [Kilpatrick et al., 1954, *Thorax*, p. 265](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/thoraxjnl/9/4/260.full.pdf) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Etiological-masking candidate, not structural proof:** radiographic inability to distinguish infected from non-infected cavitation leaves tuberculosis unidentifiable, but does not demonstrate that destroyed lung erased a separate tuberculosis lesion. [Kilpatrick et al., 1954, *Thorax*, radiological examination and pathological discussion](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/thoraxjnl/9/4/260.full.pdf) |

**Reader error**

The study’s bacteriological and pathological comparisons should not be converted into a reader-error rate because it does not report a blinded reader confusion matrix for the disease pair. [Kilpatrick et al., 1954, *Thorax*, Methods and radiological examination section](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/thoraxjnl/9/4/260.full.pdf)

## 14. COVID-19 + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- COVID-19 pneumonia commonly produces bilateral patchy or confluent white opacities with lower-zone and peripheral predominance. [Wong et al., 2020, *Radiology*, Results and Conclusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7233401/)
- Pulmonary tuberculosis may produce upper-lung cavities and nodules, although other distributions occur. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Post-primary TB”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

A microbiologically confirmed coinfection report describes an upper-lobe cavity with adjacent micronodules together with more widespread subpleural and peribronchovascular consolidations. [Martínez Orozco et al., 2020, *American Journal of Case Reports*, Case Report and Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649741/)

A separate series shows ground-glass abnormalities resolving while nodular abnormalities persist, allowing the authors to distinguish components using serial CT rather than a single examination. [Nyanti et al., 2022, *Respiratory Medicine Case Reports*, Case 1 and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022463/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Inferred from CT caption and microbiology:** after describing the cavity, the caption places consolidations “In the rest of the parenchyma,” supporting different territories but not proving unaffected solo appearances. [Martínez Orozco et al., 2020, *American Journal of Case Reports*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649741/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Inferred at regional scale:** the caption describes “ground-glass changes in both upper lobes with associated nodular changes bilaterally,” with subsequent differential resolution supporting two components. [Nyanti et al., 2022, *Respiratory Medicine Case Reports*, Figure 2 and Case 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022463/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented:** these reports do not establish a unique inseparable appearance produced only by coinfection. [Martínez Orozco et al., 2020, *American Journal of Case Reports*, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649741/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Diagnostic-masking candidate:** delayed tuberculosis confirmation in Case 2 does not prove that COVID-19 physically concealed its radiographic manifestations. [Nyanti et al., 2022, *Respiratory Medicine Case Reports*, Case 2 and Figure 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022463/) |

**Reader error**

In **1/3 patients** in the Nyanti series, tuberculosis culture became positive approximately **seven weeks after collection**, after the patient’s death, despite initially negative sputum smears. [Nyanti et al., 2022, *Respiratory Medicine Case Reports*, Case 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022463/)

Our qualification is that this is delayed microbiological recognition within a small clinical series, not a measured radiologist miss rate. [Nyanti et al., 2022, *Respiratory Medicine Case Reports*, Case 2 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9022463/)

## 15. Sarcoidosis + tuberculosis

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Sarcoidosis may produce enlargement of lymph nodes at both lung roots, pulmonary nodules and, in advanced disease, fibrosis with volume loss. [Ungprasert, Ryu and Matteson, 2019, *Mayo Clinic Proceedings: Innovations, Quality & Outcomes*, pulmonary manifestations, Table 1 and Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6713839/)
- Tuberculosis can produce cavities, lymph-node enlargement or widespread small nodules, depending on its form. [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, primary, post-primary and miliary tuberculosis sections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/)

**What changes together**

One case combined longstanding, biopsy-supported sarcoid lymphadenopathy with new lung opacities and culture-confirmed tuberculosis, with the opacities clearing while the lymphadenopathy persisted. [Papaetis et al., 2008, *Journal of Medical Case Reports*, Case presentation and Figures 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2474644/)

Another report initially attributed a diffuse nodular presentation to tuberculosis and added sarcoidosis after recurrent symptoms and further investigation. [Mandal et al., 2014, *BMJ Case Reports*, Abstract and Outcome and follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275725/)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Inferred from radiograph, history and follow-up:** the caption identifies “bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy together with lower bilateral interstitial lung densities,” while the case description assigns persistent nodes to sarcoidosis and reversible lung disease to tuberculosis. [Papaetis et al., 2008, *Journal of Medical Case Reports*, Figure 1 and Case presentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2474644/) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented in the diffuse case:** its captions describe shared interstitial or nodular abnormalities without separately identifying each disease within one region. [Mandal et al., 2014, *BMJ Case Reports*, Figures 1–3 and 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275725/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Not documented strictly:** “bilateral coarse lung markings with miliary mottling” describes a nonspecific pattern rather than a morphology unique to the combination. [Mandal et al., 2014, *BMJ Case Reports*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275725/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Diagnostic-masking candidate:** the authors describe difficulty diagnosing both diseases individually, but do not establish physical concealment of independently verified sarcoid lesions by tuberculosis. [Mandal et al., 2014, *BMJ Case Reports*, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275725/) |

**Reader error and diagnostic certainty**

In **1/1 patient** in the Mandal report, the initial working diagnosis was tuberculosis alone before sarcoidosis was added, but no separate radiologist-error measurement was reported. [Mandal et al., 2014, *BMJ Case Reports*, Treatment and Outcome and follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275725/)

The authors themselves note that non-necrotising granulomas can occur in tuberculosis and necrotising granulomas in sarcoidosis, so histological appearance alone cannot independently settle both diagnoses. [Mandal et al., 2014, *BMJ Case Reports*, Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4275725/)

## 16. Additional named combination: rheumatoid arthritis + pneumoconiosis — Caplan syndrome

**Each disease on a chest radiograph alone**

- Rheumatoid lung disease can produce rounded pulmonary nodules, including cavitating nodules, as illustrated on plain radiographs. [Sidhu et al., 2011, *Journal of Clinical Imaging Science*, “Pulmonary nodules,” Figures 4 and 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3177462/)
- Pneumoconiosis can produce small rounded opacities or large masses of progressive massive fibrosis. [De Capitani et al., 2009, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, Introduction](https://www.jornaldepneumologia.com.br/Content/imagebank/pdf/2009_35_9_17_english.pdf)

**What changes together**

Caplan syndrome is a named rheumatoid pneumoconiosis characterised by peripheral pulmonary nodules, with mineral dust incorporated into rheumatoid-type nodules on pathological examination. [Shaw et al., 2015, *European Respiratory Review*, “Pulmonary nodules”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9487778/)

The reported classical case showed bilateral peripheral rounded opacities on radiography and a biopsied rheumatoid nodule containing refracting particles. [De Capitani et al., 2009, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, Case report, Figures 1b and 3](https://www.jornaldepneumologia.com.br/Content/imagebank/pdf/2009_35_9_17_english.pdf)

| Regime | Verdict and evidence |
|---|---|
| **1 — Separate** | **Not documented strictly:** the named syndrome does not require a separate visible background pneumoconiosis pattern alongside the nodules. [De Capitani et al., 2009, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, Introduction and Discussion](https://www.jornaldepneumologia.com.br/Content/imagebank/pdf/2009_35_9_17_english.pdf) |
| **2 — Overlapping but readable** | **Not documented on imaging:** pathological identification of dust within a rheumatoid nodule does not make both components separately readable on the radiograph. [Shaw et al., 2015, *European Respiratory Review*, “Pulmonary nodules”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9487778/) |
| **3 — Merged** | **Pathology-supported candidate with radiographic examples:** “Biopsy reveals inorganic dust within the necrotic nodule,” but Figures 8–9 labelled Caplan syndrome do not establish a radiographic appearance impossible in rheumatoid disease alone. [Sidhu et al., 2011, *Journal of Clinical Imaging Science*, “Caplan’s syndrome,” Figures 8–9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3177462/) |
| **4 — Concealed** | **Not documented:** the case does not demonstrate independently confirmed disease made radiographically invisible by the other component. [De Capitani et al., 2009, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, Case report](https://www.jornaldepneumologia.com.br/Content/imagebank/pdf/2009_35_9_17_english.pdf) |

**Reader error**

No reader-error rate or diagnostic-delay denominator is reported for the classical illustrated case. [De Capitani et al., 2009, *Jornal Brasileiro de Pneumologia*, Case report and Discussion](https://www.jornaldepneumologia.com.br/Content/imagebank/pdf/2009_35_9_17_english.pdf)

Our interpretation is that Caplan syndrome is a useful joint-lesion candidate, but pathological combination should not be promoted into proof of an undecomposable radiographic regime. [Shaw et al., 2015, *European Respiratory Review*, “Pulmonary nodules”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9487778/)
