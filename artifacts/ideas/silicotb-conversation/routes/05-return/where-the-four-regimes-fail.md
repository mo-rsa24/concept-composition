# Where the four regimes fail

**Yes: the literature documents a candidate fifth state, “both diseases present, neither visible,” through reports of simultaneous pulmonary infections with normal chest radiographs; treating this as a fifth regime is our inference, not the authors’ terminology.** [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, Results: “Diagnoses among HIV-seropositive patients with normal chest radiography”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/)

## 1. The strongest counterexample: neither disease visible

Among 54 patients with normal radiographs, Yoo and colleagues reported four with simultaneous respiratory infections: two with pulmonary tuberculosis plus pulmonary cryptococcosis and two with pulmonary tuberculosis plus pulmonary aspergillosis. [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, Results: “Diagnoses among HIV-seropositive patients with normal chest radiography”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/)

The decisive published wording is: “Four (7%) of the 54 patients had two simultaneous respiratory infections”. [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, same Results subsection](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/)

These are author-reported paired diagnoses, but the accessible main text does not identify the individual microbiological confirmation pathway for each of these four patients, and the supplementary diagnostic definitions could not be retrieved in this search. [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, Methods and Supplementary Material](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/)

This is prose-supported evidence, not a caption-supported example, because the article’s Figure 1 is an enrolment flowchart rather than a patient radiograph. [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/)

**Our logical comparison with the supplied Route 05 definitions:**

| Existing way | Why a normal radiograph falls outside it |
|---|---|
| 1 — Separate places | Requires two readable appearances; neither is visible. |
| 2 — Same region, both readable | Requires two identifiable components; neither is visible. |
| 3 — Merged, neither nameable alone | Requires an abnormal combined appearance; the reported radiograph is normal. |
| 4 — One present and unreadable | Requires the image to show one disease; neither is visible. |

**Our proposed fifth way:** both diagnoses are established independently of the image, but neither has a detectable appearance on the specified examination.

**Our qualification:** this is an observation state for a modality and time point, not proof that the diseases lack structural effects, and it does not establish why neither appeared.

**Our boundary decision:** if the framework is restricted to already abnormal regions, this counterexample becomes an exclusion from its scope rather than a fifth regional morphology.

## 2. A different failure: one disease changes where the other can appear

Jackson and colleagues described chronic pulmonary emboli with noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, reporting that “the occluded segments of lung were spared from pulmonary edema, but not from neutrophil infiltration”. [Jackson, Thompson and Miller, 1986, *Chest* 89:463–465, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3948563/)

The authors explicitly identified radiographic sparing associated with chronic emboli in this single reported case, rather than concealment of edema by an overlying opacity. [Jackson et al., 1986, *Chest*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3948563/)

**Our inference from that prose:** “separate places” is insufficient when the separation itself results from interaction, because way 1 requires the diseases to leave each other’s appearance unchanged. [Jackson et al., 1986, *Chest*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3948563/)

**Our proposed mechanism label:** *exclusion or redistribution* describes one process altering the spatial expression of another, without requiring a merged lesion.

**Evidence limit:** the original figure and caption were not verified, so this is a prose-supported challenge to the framework, not a caption-supported regime assignment.

## 3. A third failure: non-decomposable does not establish a novel combined appearance

Shah and colleagues documented Pneumocystis pneumonia with cytomegalovirus pneumonitis using bronchoalveolar-lavage cytology showing both Pneumocystis-compatible organisms and CMV cytopathic changes. [Shah et al., 2017, *IDCases* 10:42–45, “Investigations” and Figure 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5581851/)

Their initial CT caption reads “CT chest showing diffuse bilateral ground glass opacities”, while the subsequent caption reads “CT chest 3 weeks later showing ‘crazy-paving pattern’”. [Shah et al., 2017, *IDCases*, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5581851/)

Neither caption attributes separate image components to the two infections or establishes that the appearance requires their combination. [Shah et al., 2017, *IDCases*, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5581851/)

**Our verdict:** this case cannot be assigned confidently to way 3 merely because both infections were demonstrated and the image was not decomposed by organism. [Shah et al., 2017, *IDCases*, Figures 1–3 and “Investigations”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5581851/)

**Our proposed unresolved category:** *shared nonspecific appearance, attribution undetermined* should remain available when independent evidence establishes two diseases but the publication does not establish what each contributes visually.

**Our logical objection to way 3:** inability to separate two contributions and proof of an appearance that neither disease produces alone are different evidential requirements.

## 4. When immune state and treatment are the more useful questions

### HIV plus tuberculosis: invisibility without demonstrated burial

Greenberg and colleagues studied 133 patients with AIDS and respiratory cultures positive for *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, of whom 19/133 had normal chest radiographs. [Greenberg et al., 1994, *Radiology* 193:115–119, Abstract: “Materials and methods” and “Results”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7916467/)

Among patients with reported CD4 strata, normal radiographs occurred in 10/48 with fewer than 200 CD4 cells/µL and 1/20 with more than 200 cells/µL. [Greenberg et al., 1994, *Radiology*, Abstract: “Results”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7916467/)

The initial radiographic interpretations did not suggest active tuberculosis in 43/133 patients, which is broader than the subgroup with entirely normal radiographs. [Greenberg et al., 1994, *Radiology*, Abstract: “Conclusion”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7916467/)

Chamie and colleagues subsequently found continuous associations between declining CD4 count and changing tuberculosis appearances, including cavitation, upper-lung disease and normal radiographs. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease* 14:1295–1302, Abstract: “Results” and “Conclusion”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20843421/)

**Our interpretation:** these findings support immune-state-associated alteration and invisibility, but do not establish the original way-4 mechanism of one visible disease physically destroying or burying another. [Chamie et al., 2010, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20843421/)

**Our classification recommendation:** retain the observation “confirmed disease not visible” separately from its proposed mechanism, rather than treating every instance as physical masking.

### Treatment can change the combined case without changing its disease names

Narita and colleagues observed paradoxical worsening in 12/33 HIV-positive tuberculosis patients receiving antiretroviral therapy, compared with 1/55 HIV-negative tuberculosis patients and 2/28 HIV-positive tuberculosis patients not receiving antiretrovirals. [Narita et al., 1998, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine* 158:157–161, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9655723/)

In the antiretroviral-treated group, paradoxical responses occurred a mean of 15 ± 11 days after antiretroviral initiation, compared with 109 ± 72 days after tuberculosis-treatment initiation. [Narita et al., 1998, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9655723/)

The authors interpreted these observations as evidence of a paradoxical response associated with immune recovery after antiretroviral initiation. [Narita et al., 1998, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9655723/)

**Our implication for synthesis:** the conditioning information should include treatment sequence and immune state when these define the intended case, because a disease-pair label alone leaves that distinction unspecified.

## 5. Published schemes that divide the problem differently

| Published scheme | Divisions and exact source | Our comparison with the four ways |
|---|---|---|
| **Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema: distribution** | The official statement distinguishes separate entities, progressive transition, paraseptal emphysema with fibrosis, thick-walled large cysts, and an admixed pattern. [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine* 206:e7–e41, Table 10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) | These describe distribution and morphology rather than whether a reader can name both diseases. |
| **Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema: multiple descriptive axes** | The same statement separately records fibrosis pattern, emphysema pattern, underlying cause, extent and relative distribution. [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Table 10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) | This supplies a precedent for several independent attributes instead of one exclusive regime. |
| **Coexisting interstitial-pneumonia patterns** | The ATS/ERS classification distinguishes patterns coexisting within one biopsy, differing between biopsy sites, or differing between histopathology and CT. [Travis et al., 2013, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine* 188:733–748, “Coexisting Patterns”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5803655/) | The unit of comparison can be tissue sample or modality, and multiple patterns need not establish two independent diseases. |
| **Tuberculosis-associated immune reconstitution** | The consensus separates paradoxical TB-IRIS, ART-associated tuberculosis and unmasking TB-IRIS. [Meintjes et al., 2008, *Lancet Infectious Diseases* 8:516–523, Abstract and consensus case definitions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2804035/) | Treatment timing and inflammatory presentation organise this scheme. |
| **Machine-learning label dependencies** | CheXGCN represents relationships between pathology labels as graph-structured dependencies learned for multilabel classification. [Chen et al., 2020, *IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics* 24:2292–2302, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31976915/) | Label association is represented without requiring a particular spatial arrangement. |

**Our scope finding:** none of these schemes supplies a direct, universal replacement for all four proposed ways, but each separates attributes that the four-way framework combines.

## 6. What multilabel chest X-ray research actually establishes

### Its account of the difficulty

HydraViT identifies variation in lesion size and location, visual similarity between abnormalities, and heterogeneous co-occurrence relationships as challenges for chest X-ray classification. [Öztürk, Turalı and Çukur, 2025, *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control* 100:106959, Abstract and Introduction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1746809424010176)

The CXR-LT challenge explicitly treats the interaction between rare labels and label co-occurrence as a learning problem. [Holste et al., 2024, *Medical Image Analysis* 97:103224, Abstract and Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11365790/)

CXR-LT also found that automated annotation yielded a mean of 5.4 positive findings per report versus 4.2 with manual annotation across the same 406 reports. [Holste et al., 2024, *Medical Image Analysis*, Table 4 and accompanying text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11365790/)

The authors identified an apparent text-mining artifact linking pneumomediastinum and enlarged cardiomediastinum, illustrating that measured co-occurrence can arise from annotation rather than anatomy. [Holste et al., 2024, *Medical Image Analysis*, discussion accompanying Supplementary Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11365790/)

**Our implication:** a label pair should not automatically be treated as a verified pair of diseases, much less as evidence that two diseases share tissue.

### Measured performance: the direct chest X-ray comparison showed an increase

HydraViT reported mean AUC of **0.798 ± 0.086 for single-label images** and **0.863 ± 0.036 for multiple-label images**, averaged across 14 labels, with the reported standard deviations describing variation across labels. [Öztürk et al., 2025, *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*, Table 1; matching author manuscript, Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06143)

The experiment used **25,596 test radiographs**, but the sizes of the single-label and multiple-label subgroups were not reported in the inspected table or dataset description. [Öztürk et al., author manuscript, “Dataset” and Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06143)

**Our calculation:** the multiple-label aggregate AUC was 0.065 higher, but this comparison does not isolate exactly two labels, a particular pair, or the causal effect of adding a disease. [Öztürk et al., author manuscript, Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06143)

**Search finding:** a chest-radiograph study reporting a verified, pair-specific accuracy decrease from “A alone” to “A with B,” together with both subgroup denominators, was **not documented in the sources inspected**.

### Adjacent CT evidence must remain labelled CT

Tushar and colleagues reported nodule-classification AUC below 0.65 without other co-occurring diseases and above 0.80 when nodules co-occurred with emphysema. [Tushar et al., 2022, *Co-occurring Diseases Heavily Influence the Performance of Weakly Supervised Learning Models for Classification of Chest CT*, Abstract](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11709)

Their test set contained **771 CT volumes**, including **296 nodule-positive volumes**, but the relevant pair-specific denominators could not be verified from the accessible text extraction of Figure 4. [Tushar et al., 2022, “Results” and Figure 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11709)

The authors warned that improved classification could reflect learning easier associated abnormalities rather than the target nodule’s appearance. [Tushar et al., 2022, “Discussion and future work”](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11709)

**Our interpretation:** these results challenge an assumption of universal performance deterioration with additional labels, while leaving the requested pair-specific chest-radiograph question unresolved.

## 7. Our proposed repair to the framework

The following is **our proposed annotation design**, not an established medical classification:

| Attribute | Proposed values |
|---|---|
| Independent disease evidence | Confirmed, probable, uncertain, absent. |
| Visibility on this examination | Both visible, only A visible, only B visible, neither visible, uncertain. |
| Spatial relationship | Separate, overlapping in projection, overlapping in tissue, undetermined. |
| Attribution of the appearance | Separately attributable, shared but nonspecific, demonstrably modified, undetermined. |
| Proposed mechanism | Structural masking, altered immune response, exclusion or redistribution, treatment-related change, unknown. |
| Context | Modality, disease stage, treatment sequence, immune state and observation time. |

**Our recommendation:** preserve the original four ways as useful descriptions, add “neither visible,” and allow “undetermined” rather than forcing every independently confirmed pair into a visual category.

**Our recommendation:** require separate evidence for spatial overlap, altered appearance and inability to identify a disease, because none logically establishes the other two.

**Our recommendation:** reserve “an appearance neither disease produces alone” for a publication that explicitly supports that claim, rather than using diagnostic ambiguity as its substitute.

## 8. Search record and limits

This was a targeted web literature search completed on **4 November 2026**, using primary studies, original methods papers and society statements where available.

| Question | Search strings and checks performed |
|---|---|
| A fifth observation state | “HIV tuberculosis normal chest radiograph”; “normal chest radiographs two simultaneous respiratory infections”; review of Yoo et al.’s methods, results and supplementary-material link. |
| Interaction without a merged lesion | “pulmonary edema pulmonary embolism spared radiograph”; review of Jackson et al.’s original case abstract and related literature. |
| Shared nonspecific appearance | “Pneumocystis cytomegalovirus coinfection CT biopsy”; review of Shah et al.’s CT captions and cytological confirmation. |
| Immune state and treatment | “tuberculosis immune reconstitution paradoxical unmasking”; review of Greenberg, Chamie, Narita and the INSHI consensus. |
| Alternative schemes | “combined pulmonary fibrosis emphysema classification transition admixed separate patterns”; review of the official CPFE statement and ATS/ERS coexisting-pattern classification. |
| Multilabel performance | “chest X-ray co-occurring diseases isolated performance AUC”; “single-label multiple co-occurring labels”; inspection of HydraViT’s table, CheXGCN, CXR-LT and the separately identified CT study. |

No established publication naming “neither visible” as a formal fifth member of this exact framework was found; the published observations and our proposed taxonomy remain separate.

The search did not establish that the candidate fifth state persists on CT, nor did it recover a published radiograph of the four dual-infection cases in Yoo et al.
