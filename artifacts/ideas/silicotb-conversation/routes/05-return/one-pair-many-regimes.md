# One pair, many regimes

**Yes: one disease pair can occupy different spatial arrangements within one patient and change arrangement over time**, with particularly explicit CT documentation for pulmonary fibrosis with emphysema. [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Figures 4 and 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/)

**Our classification convention:** the numbered regimes below are our mappings onto published descriptions, not categories assigned by the cited authors.

**Our counting rule:** a case counts when the authors describe separate and overlapping manifestations within one patient, or a transition between those arrangements while both diseases remain present.

**Our evidentiary boundary:** disappearance after successful treatment, retrospective recognition of a missed lesion, and uncertainty between alternative diagnoses do not automatically establish regime 4.

**Three qualifying cases**

| Case | Published observations and caption evidence | Our regime assignment | What explains the difference? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Fibrosis + emphysema, simultaneous arrangements on CT** | The right upper lobe contains “a combination of admixed (black arrow) and isolated emphysema (white arrowhead),” with additional mixed distributions below. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) | **1 + 2, caption-supported mapping:** emphysema outside fibrotic areas and emphysema intermingled with fibrosis coexist in one examination. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) | **Location:** this is spatial heterogeneity, without a reported interval or reader-error experiment. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) |
| **2 — Fibrosis + emphysema, serial CT** | Across **two years**, isolated right-upper-lobe emphysema “becomes admixed with fibrosis over time,” while left-lower-lobe emphysema expands under traction from evolving fibrosis. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 4A–D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) | **1 → 2, caption-supported mapping:** the same initially separate emphysematous region becomes involved by fibrosis, with both processes still named. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) | **Progression:** baseline panels A/C and follow-up panels B/D document changing overlap, without establishing strict regime 3. [Cottin et al., 2022, *AJRCCM*, Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/) |
| **3 — Fibrosis + emphysema, simultaneous arrangements on CT in an independent report** | Figure 2a describes “centrilobular and paraseptal emphysematous change at bilateral upper zones,” whereas Figure 2c describes basal septal thickening, reticulation and honeycombing. [Cheung and Chan, 2022, *Hong Kong Medical Journal*, Figure 2a,c](https://www.hkmj.org/abstracts/v28n1/85.htm) | **1 + 2, caption-supported regional mapping:** the upper/basal separation accompanies left-sided overlap at carina level, described as “emphysematous change with subpleural reticulations on the left side.” [Cheung and Chan, 2022, *HKMJ*, Figure 2b](https://www.hkmj.org/abstracts/v28n1/85.htm) | **Location:** this establishes coexistence within the same lung region, although the caption does not establish microscopic colocalisation or pixel-level overlap. [Cheung and Chan, 2022, *HKMJ*, Figure 2](https://www.hkmj.org/abstracts/v28n1/85.htm) |

The third report also compares radiographs from **2009 and 2020**, but their captions establish the emergence of basal interstitial abnormalities rather than a proven transition between two combined-disease regimes. [Cheung and Chan, 2022, *HKMJ*, Figure 1a,b](https://www.hkmj.org/abstracts/v28n1/85.htm)

**Our qualification:** these three cases meet the requested regional reading of regimes 1 and 2, but none tests the stronger counterfactual assertion that a separated lesion looks *exactly* as it would in a patient without the other disease.

**Silicosis + tuberculosis: the proposed active-to-healed transition**

**Search finding:** I did not verify a published same-patient sequence establishing **regime 2 during active TB → regime 4 after healing** under the stated requirement for independent confirmation of the hidden disease.

The existing Ehrlich review contains a relevant sequence beyond its general discussion: radiographs from **16 October 2012 and 26 February 2013** show attributed tuberculous opacification resolving while the nodular background persists. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, Figure 1a,b](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)

The caption explicitly describes “leaving a residual thin-walled cavity,” and the same patient’s October 2013 CT identifies residual cavities alongside silicotic nodules. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *ERR*, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)

**Our inference:** this sequence documents treatment-dependent morphological change but does not show healed TB becoming unreadable, because a residual structural abnormality remains identifiable. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *ERR*, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)

The historical microbiology included *M. tuberculosis* and *M. kansasii*, whereas treatment during the illustrated 2012 episode was empirical, limiting attribution of that episode to independently confirmed active TB. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *ERR*, Figure 1 caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)

**A second serial case, with weaker confirmation**

Maboso and colleagues publish January 2018 and October 2019 radiographs—approximately **21 months apart, calculated from the caption dates**—in a patient with bilateral nodules, fibrosis and cavities. [Maboso et al., 2023, *American Journal of Industrial Medicine*, Case 2 and Figure 2A,B](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajim.23460)

The authors separately describe reduced cavity-wall thickness and opacification after **12 months of treatment**, interpreting this as treatment response and assessing silicotuberculosis “including both old/healed and active TB.” [Maboso et al., 2023, *AJIM*, §3.2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajim.23460)

**Our inference:** this supports different TB stages within a chronically abnormal chest, but negative Xpert testing and empirical treatment prevent counting it as independently confirmed switching between regimes 2 and 4. [Maboso et al., 2023, *AJIM*, §§3.2 and 4.2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajim.23460)

Their Case 4 raises possible effacement of silicotic nodules by tuberculous fibrosis, but the unavailable histology prevents confirming the proposed hidden silicosis. [Maboso et al., 2023, *AJIM*, Figure 4 and §4.4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajim.23460)

**Longitudinal evidence that healing does not restore a simple baseline**

In **15 miners with culture-positive miliary TB**, increased radiographic nodularity persisted relative to premorbid films in **13/15 after two months** and **8/15 after six months**, despite clinical improvement in all patients. [Charalambous et al., 2001, *International Journal of Tuberculosis and Lung Disease* 5:1044–1050, abstract, Methods and Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11716341/)

Bacteriological cure was documented in **8/15**, and the authors proposed a silica-associated fibrotic response rather than demonstrating that persistent nodules represented ongoing infection. [Charalambous et al., 2001, *IJTLD*, abstract, Results and Conclusions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11716341/)

**Our inference:** these observations support persistent modification after TB treatment, but the accessible abstract supplies neither patient-specific regime assignments nor verifiable figure captions for a transition. [Charalambous et al., 2001, *IJTLD*, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11716341/)

Sureka and colleagues’ specifically relevant report, *Silicotuberculosis: importance of evaluation of serial radiographs*, was located bibliographically, but its captions and clinical sequence could not be verified and are not counted. [Sureka et al., 2013, *Annals of African Medicine* 12:255–256, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24309416/)

**Other pairs: useful transitions that require careful counting**

| Pair and sequence | Published evidence | Interpretation and counting decision |
|---|---|---|
| **Pneumonia + emphysema: apparent honeycombing resolves** | Initial CT was reported as honeycombing, but the appearance disappeared **two months** after antibiotics for pneumococcal infection; another panel identifies contralateral emphysema. [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, Figure 11a–c](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/) | **Regime 3 candidate, followed by resolution:** the caption states “Honeycombing was reported on the first CT scan,” documenting misinterpretation in **1 illustrated case**, but successful treatment does not establish another combined-disease regime afterward. [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, Figure 11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/) |
| **Drug-induced lung injury + adenocarcinoma: masking followed by recognition** | Bilateral infiltrates improved **two months** after drug withdrawal, a left-upper-division lesion persisted, and resection confirmed adenocarcinoma. [Watanabe et al., 2026, *Respirology Case Reports*, “Clinical Image” and Figures 1–3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rcr2.70678) | **Regime 4 inferred from prose:** the authors report “lung adenocarcinoma masked by drug-induced lung injury,” but the sequence does not demonstrate that both diseases remained sufficiently present afterward to establish regime 1 or 2. [Watanabe et al., 2026, *Respirology Case Reports*, abstract and “Clinical Image”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rcr2.70678) |
| **Pulmonary fibrosis + cancer: missed nodule subsequently enlarges** | A nodule at the boundary of fibrotic and normal lung was unreported initially and enlarged over **five months**, with subsequent histological confirmation of carcinoma. [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, Figure 15a,b](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/) | **Not counted as regime 4:** the caption attributes the miss to satisfaction of search and describes an existing nodule, rather than proving anatomical concealment by fibrosis. [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, Figure 15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/) |

**Our distinction:** the pneumonia example is evidence that the joint appearance can disappear when one component resolves, whereas the fibrosis–emphysema serial case demonstrates changing overlap while both components persist.

**Is stage responsible, or location?**

- **Location can account for simultaneous differences:** one published CPFE figure places upper-zone emphysema and lower-zone reticulation with thick-walled cysts in the same patient. [Cottin, 2017, *European Respiratory Journal*, Figure 1D,D′](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/50/1/1700846)
- **Treatment and stage can change appearances:** resolving tuberculous opacification can expose a persistent cavity rather than eliminate all recognisable evidence of previous TB. [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *ERR*, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/)
- **Immune recovery can change the image without changing the named pair:** serial radiographs in HIV-associated TB show enlargement of a hilar mass after **nine weeks** of antiretroviral and antituberculous treatment, attributed to immune restoration disease. [Jenkins et al., 2006, *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, Figure 1A,B](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/12/4/05-0455-f1)
- **Our inference:** that HIV example establishes a treatment-associated change in TB expression, but its two visible hilar masses do not establish switching between the four spatial regimes. [Jenkins et al., 2006, *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, Figure 1](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/12/4/05-0455-f1)

**Does any pair cover all four?**

**Search finding:** no pair was verified across all four *strict* definitions, and no patient was verified as traversing all four.

**Our proposed strongest candidate is fibrosis + emphysema**, because its documented spatial arrangements are accompanied by published evidence of a distinctive combined morphology and independently confirmed masking.

For the distinctive morphology, Cottin describes thick-walled large cysts as “not observed in either IPF or emphysema alone,” alongside illustrated examples of admixed disease. [Cottin, 2017, *ERJ*, discussion preceding Figure 1 and Figure 1C,D′](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/50/1/1700846)

**Our regime-3 verdict:** this supports a joint morphology, but it does not establish the additional requirement that neither constituent disease can be recognised by a reader. [Cottin, 2017, *ERJ*, discussion preceding Figure 1](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/50/1/1700846)

For masking, Saito and colleagues report that interstitial pneumonia was “masked by emphysematous findings and thus not detected on preoperative CT” in **6/7 patients with both postoperative ARDS and histologically confirmed interstitial pneumonia**. [Saito et al., 2011, *European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery* 39:190–194, Results](https://academic.oup.com/ejcts/article/39/2/190/548964)

**Our regime-4 verdict:** this is prose-supported masking with independent histological confirmation, but the denominator is a selected postoperative subgroup, not all patients with fibrosis and emphysema. [Saito et al., 2011, *EJCTS*, Methods and Results](https://academic.oup.com/ejcts/article/39/2/190/548964)

**Our disease-label qualification:** the study’s category is histological interstitial pneumonia, so these observations cannot automatically be assigned to one specific fibrosis subtype or to the patients illustrated in the CPFE cases above. [Saito et al., 2011, *EJCTS*, abstract and Methods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20598898/)

**Our modelling implication:** retain disease pair, region, modality and time as separate annotation fields, and record “joint morphology,” “not recognised” and “independently confirmed but obscured” separately rather than treating them as interchangeable labels.
