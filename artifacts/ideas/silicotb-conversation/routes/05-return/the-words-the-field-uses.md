# The words the field uses

**Search finding:** I found established language for separation, admixture, altered appearances and concealment, but no source establishing your four regimes as one general classification of paired chest diseases.

**Status of this document:** The four regimes remain your proposed framework; the mappings below distinguish published terminology from our interpretation of its fit.

**Scope:** This is a bounded vocabulary search, not a claim to have recovered every expression ever used.

**1. Separate places**

| Published term | Specialty | Meaning and fit to your regime |
|---|---|---|
| **Separate entities; separate emphysema and fibrosis pattern** | Chest radiology; respiratory medicine | The caption names “separate emphysema and fibrosis pattern” and locates the components in different lung zones, supporting spatial separation but not proving unchanged appearance or causal independence. — [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Figure 1 and Table 10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/). |
| **Coexisting; concurrent** | Chest radiology | These words establish simultaneous disease without prescribing separation, as demonstrated by a study explicitly investigating tuberculosis and carcinoma in the same lobe. — [Kim et al., 2001, *Korean Journal of Radiology*, title and Abstract](https://www.kjronline.org/DOIx.php?id=10.3348/kjr.2001.2.3.138). |
| **Comorbidity; multimorbidity** | Clinical epidemiology; general medicine | These are patient-level concepts describing multiple conditions, with an index disease usually distinguished for comorbidity, rather than classifications of their positions or appearances. — [Valderas et al., 2009, *Annals of Family Medicine*, “Reviewing the concept of comorbidity”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713155/). |
| **Label co-occurrence; interdependency** | Machine learning on chest radiographs | These describe relationships among diagnostic labels and do not establish that their associated abnormalities occupy separate regions. — [Chen et al., 2020, *IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31976915/). |

**Our mapping verdict:** A published spatial analogue exists, but no retrieved term carries all three of your requirements—different locations, unchanged appearances and no interaction.

**2. Same region, both still readable**

| Published term | Specialty | Meaning and fit to your regime |
|---|---|---|
| **Admixed pattern; progressive transition** | Chest radiology; respiratory medicine | These describe intermingling or transition between emphysema and fibrosis, providing anatomical counterparts to your second regime without guaranteeing diagnostic separability everywhere. — [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, Table 10 and Figures 3–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/). |
| **Intracavitary aspergilloma; fungus ball; Monod sign** | Chest radiology; infectious disease | These identify fungal material within a pre-existing cavity, with Figure 1 explicitly describing “mobile intracavitary contents (simple aspergilloma),” providing a specific example rather than a general name for the regime. — [Garg et al., online 2022, print 2023, *American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene*, “Simple aspergilloma” and Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9833062/). |
| **Superimposed disease; disease on a background of another disease** | Chest radiology | These expressions describe an additional process affecting already abnormal lung, but their use for edema with emphysema does not guarantee that both retain their usual appearance. — [Kabadi, Hanley and Bueno, 2016, *Applied Radiology*, “Diagnosis” and “Discussion”](https://www.appliedradiology.com/articles/sponge-lung-pulmonary-edema-superimposed-on-emphysema). |
| **Coinfection; co-infection; mixed infection; polymicrobial infection** | Infectious disease; medical indexing | MeSH groups these terms around multiple pathogens infecting one host, which establishes neither shared lung tissue nor separately readable imaging findings. — [US National Library of Medicine, 2026, *MeSH*, “Coinfection,” scope note and entry terms](https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D060085). |

**Our mapping verdict:** Admixture and an intracavitary fungus ball are useful precedents, while *superimposed* and *coinfection* are broader than the regime.

**3. Merged, neither nameable alone**

| Published term | Specialty | Meaning and fit to your regime |
|---|---|---|
| **Traction emphysema; thick-walled large cysts** | Chest radiology; respiratory medicine | These describe morphological change where fibrosis and emphysema meet, but the statement explicitly leaves the specificity of the resulting cystic appearance unresolved. — [Cottin et al., 2022, *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, “Imaging features—Overview” and Figures 4, 6–7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7615200/). |
| **Pseudohoneycombing** | Chest radiology | The explanation “Indeed, the filling of alveoli surrounding the emphysematous changes may mimic honeycomb pattern” supports a combination-generated mimic, although equating it with your full third regime remains our inference. — [Du Pasquier et al., 2019, *BJR Open*, discussion immediately preceding Figure 11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7592491/). |
| **Swiss cheese appearance; pseudocavitations** | Chest radiology | These terms describe consolidation interrupted by pre-existing emphysematous airspaces, establishing modified morphology without asserting that neither component can be recognized. — [Nambu et al., 2014, *World Journal of Radiology*, “Pneumonia on a background of pulmonary emphysema,” Figures 21–22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209424/). |
| **Sponge Lung** | Chest radiology | The authors introduce this name for edema affecting emphysematous lung and illustrate it on a radiograph, but their caption identifies both components, so it does not establish your strict non-decomposability requirement. — [Kabadi, Hanley and Bueno, 2016, *Applied Radiology*, Figure 1 and “Discussion”](https://www.appliedradiology.com/articles/sponge-lung-pulmonary-edema-superimposed-on-emphysema). |
| **Combined disease; conglomeration** | Occupational medicine; pulmonary pathology | The silicotuberculosis review describes tuberculosis contributing to the joining of silicotic nodules into masses, but neither expression is presented as a general category requiring an appearance impossible for either disease alone. — [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, “Pathology”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11668511/). |

**Our mapping verdict:** Published names exist for particular altered appearances, but I found no established general term requiring both novelty of appearance and inability to identify either component separately.

**Our proposed distinction:** Record *modified combined appearance* separately from *non-decomposable appearance*, because the former alone does not satisfy your third definition.

**4. One disease present and unreadable**

| Published term | Specialty | Meaning and fit to your regime |
|---|---|---|
| **Masking; masked lesion** | Chest radiology | The authors report “failure to observe new nodules masked by coexisting stable TB lesions,” directly documenting concealment associated with another disease. — [Kim et al., 2001, *Korean Journal of Radiology*, Results and Figure 1](https://www.kjronline.org/DOIx.php?id=10.3348/kjr.2001.2.3.138). |
| **Obscuring; obscuration** | Occupational medicine; chest radiology | The review describes tuberculous abnormalities obscuring silicosis and recommends reassessment after treatment, making this a close verbal counterpart to your fourth regime. — [Ehrlich et al., 2024, *European Respiratory Review*, “Imaging—Plain chest radiography”](https://publications.ersnet.org/highwire_display/entity_view/node/696223/full). |
| **Effacement** | Occupational medicine; chest radiology | The case series discusses possible loss of recognizable silicotic nodulation through confluence and contraction during tuberculous fibrosis, offering a term closer to destruction of a pattern than simple image overlap. — [Maboso et al., 2023, *American Journal of Industrial Medicine*, Discussion, passage on effacement of nodular silicosis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajim.23460). |
| **Radiographically occult disease** | Chest radiology | This describes disease undetected on radiography that may be detected by CT, without specifying another disease as the cause of its invisibility. — [Bhalla et al., 2015, *Indian Journal of Radiology and Imaging*, “Imaging modalities,” CT paragraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4531444/). |
| **Normal chest radiograph in culture-positive pulmonary tuberculosis** | Infectious disease; respiratory medicine | This descriptive category establishes disease without radiographic abnormality, occurring in 14 of 148 culture-positive pulmonary tuberculosis patients in the HIV-positive cohort. — [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, Results, paragraph preceding Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/). |
| **Satisfaction of search** | Diagnostic radiology | This names impaired detection of another abnormality after one is encountered, rather than requiring physical concealment or destruction of the second abnormality. — [Berbaum et al., 2015, *Academic Radiology*, Introduction and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4609286/). |
| **Subsequent search miss** | Cognitive science; radiology research | This is the newer term for the multiple-target detection phenomenon historically called satisfaction of search, rather than a synonym for an inherently invisible lesion. — [Adamo et al., 2021, *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications*, Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8403090/). |

**Direct answer:** The fourth phenomenon has published names, particularly *masking*, *obscuration* and *effacement*, although I found no single standardized category unifying physical concealment, immune-associated absence of an imaging response and reader error.

Kim and colleagues distinguish masking by stable tuberculosis in 8 of 21 patients with serial radiographs from masking by active tuberculosis in 3 of 21, while separately classifying misinterpretation and subtle lesions. — [Kim et al., 2001, *Korean Journal of Radiology*, Results](https://www.kjronline.org/DOIx.php?id=10.3348/kjr.2001.2.3.138).

Yoo and colleagues associate normal radiographs with greater immunosuppression, but their observational findings do not establish that HIV physically concealed an otherwise visible tuberculous lesion. — [Yoo et al., 2011, *Respirology*, Table 2 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3126910/).

**Our interpretation:** A normal radiograph in HIV-associated tuberculosis requires broadening your fourth regime from “one disease visible, the other hidden” to “confirmed disease without a separately recognizable image manifestation.”

**Your five vocabulary entries**

| Your term | Standard usage and mismatch | Proposed wording for your intended meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Comorbidity** | Standard, but broader than unrelated diagnoses with unchanged appearances, because its conceptual literature includes several causal relationships between conditions. — [Valderas et al., 2009, *Annals of Family Medicine*, “Relationships between comorbid diseases”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713155/). | **Our proposal:** *Coexisting diagnoses with spatially separate, independently recognizable manifestations*, adding causal independence only when separately supported. |
| **Superimposed disease** | Established descriptive wording, but it does not require reciprocal modification, because the edema–emphysema account principally describes pre-existing emphysema changing the appearance of edema. — [Kabadi, Hanley and Bueno, 2016, *Applied Radiology*, “Discussion”](https://www.appliedradiology.com/articles/sponge-lung-pulmonary-edema-superimposed-on-emphysema). | **Our proposal:** *Superimposed disease with altered morphology*, specifying which condition changes which appearance. |
| **Co-occurrence** | Standard in chest-image machine learning for joint labels and their dependencies, without a requirement that the findings occupy different regions. — [Chen et al., 2020, *IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics*, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31976915/). | **Our proposal:** Retain *co-occurrence* for joint presence and use *spatially separate manifestations* for your narrower meaning. |
| **Intersection** | Standard as a geometric operation in image evaluation, including intersection-over-union between predicted and reference boxes, rather than an established clinical category of two diseases sharing tissue. — [Wang et al., 2017, *Proceedings of CVPR*, “Pathology Localization,” evaluation paragraph](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Wang_ChestX-ray8_Hospital-Scale_Chest_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf). | **Our proposal:** *Annotation overlap* for intersecting boxes and *anatomical overlap* only when the evidence establishes a shared anatomical location. |
| **Interaction** | Standard but ambiguous, because epidemiological interaction concerns joint effects relative to a specified scale rather than necessarily a novel image appearance. — [Whitcomb and Naimi, 2023, *American Journal of Epidemiology*, “Weighing the scales of interaction”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505417/). | **Our proposal:** *Morphological interaction*, explicitly defined as your research term and distinguished from statistical or biological interaction. |

There is genuine definitional disagreement over *comorbidity*, because Ording and Sørensen propose excluding conditions caused by the index disease. — [Ording and Sørensen, 2013, *Clinical Epidemiology*, “Confusion concerning terminology used in clinical epidemiology”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/3704301/).

Valderas and colleagues instead discuss direct causation among the pathways connecting comorbid conditions. — [Valderas et al., 2009, *Annals of Family Medicine*, “Pathways to comorbidity”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713155/).

**Our interpretation:** These definitions disagree about causal inclusion, but neither supplies your image-based definition.

**Nearby terms that should not become accidental synonyms**

| Term | Why it needs separate treatment |
|---|---|
| **Superinfection; superadded infection; superimposed infection** | MedGen groups these expressions as synonyms for an infection concept, rather than defining a spatial relationship between two image patterns. — [US National Library of Medicine, accessed 2026, *MedGen*, concept C0038826, synonyms and SNOMED CT terms](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/medgen/11659). |
| **Overlap syndrome** | In respiratory and sleep medicine this commonly denotes obstructive sleep apnea with chronic lung disease, especially COPD, rather than overlapping radiographic pixels. — [Owens, 2014, *Sleep Medicine Clinics*, “Pulmonary overlap syndromes, with a focus on COPD and ILD,” Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4671775/). |
| **Crazy paving; reticulonodular pattern** | These are combinations of imaging features in the thoracic glossary, not definitions requiring two independently established diseases. — [Bankier et al., 2024, *Radiology*, “Pattern,” Figures 121–129](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/full/10.1148/radiol.232558). |
| **Local labels; global labels** | VinDr-CXR distinguishes boxed findings from image-level diagnostic impressions, so these annotation categories should not be treated as equivalent units of disease interaction. — [Nguyen et al., 2022, *Scientific Data*, “Data labeling”](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01498-w). |

**Our methodological inference:** Intersecting finding boxes do not by themselves establish two diseases in the same tissue, two readable components or mutual alteration of appearance.

**Our proposed reporting vocabulary**

| Regime | Working name—our proposal, not a published taxonomy | Additional information to record |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spatial separation with preserved recognition | Location and evidence that both diagnoses are identifiable. |
| 2 | Anatomical overlap with preserved recognition | Evidence for a shared region and independently identifiable components. |
| 3 | Morphological interaction with loss of separability | Evidence of altered morphology and the separate basis for claiming non-decomposability. |
| 4 | Confirmed disease without separate image recognition | Whether the explanation is obscuration, effacement, radiographic occultness or an observer miss. |
